# 04 — The *Short Course*: splits, node topology, and the prose of visibility

Read-only research briefing, 2026-10-25. Sources, all local:

- `/media/user/data/old-hdd/old-hdd/www.marxists.org/reference/archive/stalin/works/1939/x01/` — `intro.htm`, `ch01.htm`, `ch08.htm`, `ch09.htm`, `ch10.htm`, `ch11.htm`.
- `.../prolewiki/Exports/Library/Library_On the shortcomings of party work and measures to eliminate Trotskyist and other double-dealers_Our Tasks.txt`

**Coverage limit, stated up front.** This on-disk copy holds only the Introduction and chapters 1, 8, 9, 10, 11. Chapters 2–7 (1901–1918: the Second Congress split, Menshevism, 1905, the reaction, Otzovism/ultimatism, Liquidationism, the Prague conference, February and October) and chapter 12 are **absent from this archive**. Where this briefing names those deviations it does so from the *Short Course*'s own retrospective lists — the Introduction's enumeration, ch. 1's Narodism / Legal Marxism / Economism, and ch. 11 §4(b), which commands Party members to study "how it combated and vanquished the Trotskyites, the 'Democratic-Centralists,' the 'Workers' Opposition,' the Zinovievites, the Right deviators, the Right-Leftist freaks." That list is itself the finding: the book treats the *catalogue of deviations* as a curriculum.

**Guardrail observed.** Trotskyism appears here only as the *Short Course* and *Our Tasks* treat it: an anti-Leninist, Menshevik deviation that degenerates into double-dealing, wrecking and espionage. No Trotskyist text is used as authority. The analytical spine is MLM: the party is forged in two-line struggle; the main danger is opportunism inside the ranks; unity is a *product* of struggle, never its precondition.

## Part I — The split is the growth mechanism, not the failure mode

### The thesis is printed on page one

The Introduction does not say the Party grew *despite* splits. It says growth *was* the splits:

> "The C.P.S.U.(B.) grew and gained strength in a fight over fundamental
> principles waged against the petty-bourgeois parties within the working-class
> movement … and, within the Party itself, against the Menshevik, opportunist
> trends — the Trotskyites, Bukharinites, nationalist deviators and other
> anti-Leninist groups."

And ch. 1 states the law in eight words: "Marxism in Russia could therefore grow and gain strength only by combating Narodism." Not "grew, and also polemicized." The combat is the growth. This is the single most important design consequence in the whole source: **a Babylon organization that avoids splits does not accumulate strength, it accumulates undigested line.** Cohesion without clarification is rot with good manners.

### The catalogue, with each deviation's material content

Every deviation in the book is a *practical* error with a class basis, never a mere opinion. That is what makes them mechanically representable.

| Deviation | The error, concretely | Class content the book assigns |
|---|---|---|
| Narodism / individual terrorism | Heroes make history; assassinate the tsar instead of organizing the class. Khalturin pulled off union work to do terrorism. | Petty-bourgeois intelligentsia; later, kulak interest |
| Legal Marxism | Fellow-travellers who fight Narodism while cutting out "the very core of Marxism, namely, the doctrine of the proletarian revolution and the dictatorship of the proletariat." Struve: "go to capitalism for schooling." | Bourgeois intellectuals; later Cadets, then White guards |
| Economism | Workers do the economic struggle; politics belongs to the liberal bourgeoisie. The "young" who replaced the arrested Petersburg leadership. | The first compromisers *inside* the Marxist organizations |
| Menshevism | (Referenced throughout; ch. 8 uses it as the yardstick: "compromisers of the type of the Russian Mensheviks.") | Liberal-bourgeois tail |
| Liquidationism, Otzovism, ultimatism | Named in the retrospective lists only — absent chapters. | — |
| "Left" deviation | Lominadze/Shatskin panic that NEP = restoration. Also forcing collectivization by decree. | Political illiteracy; in 1930, objectively serving the class enemy |
| Right deviation | Kulaks will "grow into" Socialism; class struggle subsides as we advance; heavy industry is premature. | Kulak agency inside the Party |
| Trotskyite-Bukharinite double-dealing | Professes loyalty, maintains an illegal press, dues and platform; then wrecking, diversion, terror. | Foreign intelligence and the smashed exploiters |

Two structural facts fall out of the table:

1. **Deviations are not symmetric.** The book fights on two fronts but always nominates a main danger and concentrates fire. Ch. 10: "a fight must be waged on two fronts, with the fire concentrated on the Right deviation." Ch. 9: "the congress condemned both deviations … At the same time, it directed its main blow at the bigger danger."
2. **The main danger is a function of neglect, not of size.** Ch. 11 §3, Stalin on the national question: *"The major danger is the deviation against which we have ceased to fight, thereby allowing it to grow into a danger to the state."* This is the cleanest mechanic in the source. Weight is not intrinsic; it is a function of attention withheld. A player who spends ten periods hunting Trotskyists while the Right grows in the trade unions has made a losing move the game should be able to compute and never display.

### How a non-antagonistic contradiction becomes antagonistic

The book gives a four-stage gradient, and it is a *state machine*, not a mood:

1. **Fellow-traveller.** Legal Marxism: "It usually happens with big social movements in history that transient 'fellow-travellers' fasten on them." At this stage agreement is *permitted*: Lenin "considered it permissible to come to a temporary agreement with the 'legal Marxists' in order to use them against the Narodniks … At the same time, however, Lenin was unsparing in his criticism." The rule is exact: **cooperate on the immediate enemy, never suspend criticism, never cede the programme.** The unity is instrumental and time-boxed.
2. **Conciliator / buffer.** Bukharin's "buffer" group that "defended and shielded the Trotskyites" — Lenin calls this "the acme of ideological depravity," and the buffer soon merges with what it was buffering. The conciliator is a distinct role from the deviationist, and the book treats it as *worse*, because it buys time for the deviation at the cost of clarity.
3. **Faction.** Separate platform, separate discipline, illegal press, dues collected. This is the threshold at which the Tenth Congress's resolution on Party Unity bites: factions "inevitably lead to intensified and repeated attempts by the enemies of the Party … to widen the cleavage."
4. **Antagonist.** Once defeated in open discussion, the faction goes underground, then appeals to hostile classes: the parallel demonstration of November 7, 1927; then the "Leningrad Centre"; then the Kirov murder. The book's step from (3) to (4) is: *appealing to the Party against the Central Committee is still internal; appealing to the class enemy against the Party is not.*

The design rule: **a contradiction becomes antagonistic at the moment the minority stops trying to win the organization and starts trying to win outside it.** Everything before that moment is a fight worth having; everything after is policing. The player should be able to feel the crossing without being told which side of it they are on — the *symptoms* are visible (an illegal press, a parallel event, a foreign contact), the threshold is not.

### Sincere error is not the same as a wrong grievance

Ch. 8's "Military Opposition" is the best evidence in the corpus that the book is not a simple loyalty test. The Opposition comprised ex-"Left Communists" *plus* "some Party workers who had never participated in any opposition, but were dissatisfied with the way Trotsky was conducting the affairs of the army" — and their grievance was real (Trotsky had tried to shoot prominent army Communists; the Central Committee had to intervene to save their lives). Lenin and Stalin nonetheless fight them, because their *programme* defended guerrilla habits and resisted a regular army. And in the same session the congress "dealt a blow at Trotsky." **Both.**

For Babylon: an internal bloc can hold a legitimate grievance and an incorrect line simultaneously, and the correct response is to settle the grievance and destroy the line. A game that models opposition as "loyalty meter" cannot represent this. A game that models it as *line + grievance + method* can.

### The purge is not the split; the purge is the cleanup after the split

Numbers the book is willing to print: the 1921 purge expelled "nearly 170,000 persons, or about 25 per cent of the total membership," conducted "at open meetings, in the presence and with the participation of non-Party people," on Lenin's instruction to cleanse the Party "of rascals, bureaucrats, dishonest or wavering Communists, and of Mensheviks who have repainted their 'facade' but who have remained Mensheviks at heart." The next three congresses all report membership *falling* (735,881 → 643,000) as a result, and the book presents the fall as strength. A game whose org sheet treats member count as a score is playing a different book.

## Part II — Concrete topology mechanics

### 1. Circles → League: fusion requires five things, or it is only a name

Ch. 1's founding sequence is a precise recipe. In 1895 Lenin "united all the Marxist workers' circles in St. Petersburg (there were already about twenty of them) into a single League of Struggle" — but the *content* of the fusion was a turn in method: from propaganda in circles "to political agitation among the broad masses … on issues of the day," plus a leaflet apparatus that responded to each strike within days.

Then the negative proof. The First Congress (Minsk, March 1898) "was attended by only nine persons," announced the Party's formation, and produced nothing:

> "in reality no Marxist Social-Democratic party was as yet formed in Russia …
> there was still no common line of action … nor was there a party program,
> party rules or a single leading centre."

**Therefore: a merge needs (a) a common line, (b) a programme, (c) rules, (d) a single centre, and (e) a paper.** Without all five you have a press release. The book says the congress's significance was that the formal act "played a great revolutionary propagandist role" — i.e. the announcement has *agitational* value and zero organizational value, which is exactly the distinction Babylon should render: the Ticker reports the merger, the Topology screen shows the nodes still unconnected.

### 2. The paper is the integration technology

*Iskra* is described in fully material terms because it had to be: printed abroad on "very thin but durable paper," smuggled, "reprinted in Russia by secret printing plants in Baku, Kishinev and Siberia," with codes for secret correspondence and addresses for literature worked out in advance at meetings in Ufa, Pskov, Moscow and Petersburg. Its function is stated as organizational, not journalistic: "Only such a newspaper could link up the disunited Marxist organizations and prepare the way for the creation of a real party," against "amateurishness and the parochial outlook of the circles."

This is a Babylon mechanic with no current analogue: **a network-wide publication that converts many small nodes into one line**, at the cost of a physical supply chain that the state can smash. Its failure is an arrest; its success is that local disputes start being argued in a common vocabulary.

Note also the succession problem, which the game must model because the source does:

> After the arrest of Lenin and his close associates, the leadership … changed
> considerably. New people appeared who called themselves the "young" and Lenin
> and his associates the "old fellows." These people pursued an erroneous
> political line.

**Arrest does not just remove cadre; it changes the line of the node that survives.** Repression is a selection filter that favours the least-exposed, not the most correct.

### 3. The congress as adjudicator — six distinct powers

The *Short Course* uses congresses for six different operations, and a game that collapses them into one "vote" loses the interesting part:

1. **Ratify a line** — the ordinary case ("endorsed Lenin's platform by an overwhelming majority").
2. **Dissolve a formation** — Tenth Congress: "ordered the immediate dissolution of all factional groups," with "unconditional and immediate expulsion" for non-observance, and authority for the Central Committee to expel even its own members.
3. **Expel named persons** — Fifteenth Congress, a list of thirteen names plus "the whole 'Democratic-Centralism' group."
4. **Rewrite the organization itself** — Fourteenth Congress adopts new Rules and renames the party (R.C.P.(B.) → C.P.S.U.(B.)); Seventeenth adds a preamble, tightens admission, and renames "nuclei" as "primary organizations."
5. **Replace a delinquent regional leadership** — 1930: "The Central Committee changed the leadership of certain regional and territorial Party organizations (Moscow Region, Transcaucasia) which had committed political mistakes and proved incapable of rectifying them."
6. **Send emissaries to a captured delegation** — 1925: the Leningrad delegation arrived mandated against the Central Committee ("Never in its history had there been a case when the whole delegation from an important Party centre like Leningrad had prepared to come out in opposition"). Molotov, Kirov, Voroshilov, Kalinin and Andreyev were sent to Leningrad to explain the congress; the result was that "over 97 per cent" of Leningrad members endorsed the congress decisions. The delegation had "secured their mandates under false pretences" — a *mandate* can be a captured artifact, and a node's delegates need not represent its members.

Power (6) is the one Babylon's Topology screen most needs: **a node's delegate is not the node.** A captured delegation is a visible anomaly (100% opposition from a node with 60% loyal members) that the player can only resolve by sending people, not by clicking.

### 4. Scale, as the book gives it

Congress sizes are printed as (voting delegates / members represented / voice delegates). This is the book's standard instrumentation, and it is enough to build a real internal-politics model:

| Congress | Voting | Members | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| VIII (1919) | 301 | 313,766 | 102 |
| IX (1920) | 554 | 611,978 | 162 |
| X (1921) | 694 | 732,521 | 296 |
| XI (1922) | 522 | 532,000 | 165 |
| XII (1923) | 408 | 386,000 | 417 |
| XIII (1924) | 748 | 735,881 | 416 |
| XIV (1925) | 665 | 643,000 (+445,000 candidates) | 641 |
| XV (1927) | 898 | 887,233 (+348,957) | 771 |
| XVI (1930) | 1,268 | 1,260,874 (+711,609) | 891 |
| XVII (1934) | 1,225 | 1,874,488 (+935,298) | 736 |

Two readings worth having in the design doc:

- **The voice/vote ratio drifts.** 1919–21, voice delegates are a third of voting. By 1923 they outnumber voting delegates (417 vs 408). By 1934 they are 60% of voting. The book never remarks on it. A participant reading the tables would notice. That is exactly the class of fact Babylon may show: *counted, printed, uninterpreted.*
- **The one time the book prints a discussion result, it is devastating.** October 1927: "724,000 Party members voted for the policy of the Central Committee; 4,000, or less than one per cent, for the bloc." A faction with 877,000 members to appeal to got 4,000 votes. The lesson the book draws is not "the minority was small" but "the opposition had already ceased to be a political trend and become a careerist clique" — i.e. **the vote is evidence of the *prior* degeneration, not the cause of the defeat.**

### 5. The double-dealer ladder (the internal-enemy state machine)

This is the most directly implementable sequence in the corpus, because ch. 10 and ch. 11 spell out each rung:

1. **Open opposition** — platform, discussion, defeat.
2. **Recantation** — public statement condemning their own factionalism.
3. **Probation with public conditions.** The Party's four terms (ch. 10) are a template any org could adopt: (a) publicly denounce the rejected line as anti-Party; (b) publicly acknowledge the Party line as the only correct one; (c) unconditionally abide by decisions; (d) serve a term of probation, with reinstatement considered case by case on results. The book is explicit that this has value *either way*: "if the applicants were sincere" it recovers workers; "if they were not sincere" it unmasks them "no longer as misguided individuals, but as unprincipled careerists … and incorrigible double-dealers."
4. **Clandestine faction** — "an illegal printing press, collected membership dues … circulated their platform," while professing loyalty. Ch. 10's description of the 1927 platform is a checklist of the double-dealer's surface: *professes* no objection to Party decisions / *professes* support for unity / *professes* support for industrialization and collectivization, then in reality does the opposite of each.
5. **Appeal outward** — the parallel demonstration; contact with foreign services.
6. **Wrecking** — from ch. 11 and *Our Tasks*: mismanaging mines to lower output, spoiling machinery and ventilation, causing roof-falls and fires; in agriculture, "destroy the horses … by deliberately infecting them with glanders, mange," damaging tractors; in the 1939 text, "it requires not more than a few dozen men to blow it up."
7. **Terror** — the "Leningrad Centre," Kirov, 1 December 1934.

Three properties make this a *system* rather than a script:

- **The wrecker must produce real successes.** This is the sharpest single insight in *Our Tasks*: "the real wrecker has from time to time to show successes in his work, for this is his only means of keeping himself going as a wrecker, of winning the confidence of people." Corollary, stated: "the individual who … even occasionally shows successes in his work cannot be a wrecker" is a "rotten theory." **A sabotage model in which saboteurs always perform badly is trivially solvable and wrong.** The tell is not poor output; it is poor output *in a place where output is unusually good*, or a pattern across otherwise unrelated failures.
- **The wreckers change tactics after open defeat.** Ch. 11 on the kulaks: "Formerly the kulaks had fought the collective farms openly … Now that their open struggle … had failed, they changed their tactics. They laid aside their sawn-off shotguns and posed as innocent, unoffending folk … Once inside the collective farms they stealthily carried on their sabotage." **Repression selects for concealment**; the game should make a defeated open enemy more dangerous, not less.
- **A recantation is a broadcast with an audience.** At the Seventeenth Congress, "the congress detected a ring of insincerity … The Party saw that the hollow speeches of these gentry were in reality meant for their supporters outside the congress, to serve as a lesson to them in duplicity." The *content* is loyalist; the *function* is a signal to a network. This is the single best argument for Babylon's three-feed narrator: the same utterance read hegemonically, partisantly, and coldly.

### 6. Vigilance: the virtue, and four ways it fails

*Our Tasks* is a treatise on one organizational virtue and two diseases.

- **Political carelessness / trustfulness.** "The weakness from which our people suffer now is not technical backwardness, but political carelessness, blind faith in people who have come by chance into possession of Party membership cards, the failure to check up on people not according to the political declarations they make, but according to the results of the work they do." The check is *results*, not testimony. Note the inversion: the earlier period's weakness was technical, and the slogan was "master technique"; the current weakness is political, and the slogan is "master Bolshevism." **The key link changes with the period**, and Stalin is explicit that both slogans could not have been advanced at once: "At the turning points … some basic slogan is always advanced as the key slogan in order, by catching on to it, to draw in the whole chain." One live emphasis at a time; everything else subordinate.
- **Complacency.** The "rotten theory" that the class struggle subsides as success accumulates: "the further forward we advance, the greater the successes we achieve, the greater will be the fury of the remnants of the broken exploiting classes … the more will they clutch at the most desperate means of struggle, as the last resort of doomed people." Same law in ch. 11 §4(a): "the more hopeless the position of the enemies, the more eagerly will they clutch at 'extreme measures.'"
- **The scale fallacy.** "We Bolsheviks are many while the wreckers are few … This more than strange theory has been invented so as to bring solace." The arithmetic: "Tens of thousands of workers have to be set to work to build a Dnieprostroy, but it requires not more than a few dozen men to blow it up." Asymmetry of effort is the attacker's advantage; a majority is not a defence.
- **Registry integrity.** Ch. 11 §4 is a mundane and therefore very usable passage: chaos in Party-card registration, cards issued by "persons in minor positions," enemies "using the possession of a Party card as a screen." The remedy: verification of records, exchange of cards, then resumption of admission "on the basis of a strictly individual enrolment." **Membership is an access token with a registry; the registry is an attack surface.**

### 7. Rectification: central correction of a local enthusiasm

The 1930 sequence is a complete, dated cycle and Babylon should be able to run it verbatim in structure:

1. Local cadres over-fulfil a correct line by coercion (forced collectivization, inflated percentages, skipping the artel for the commune).
2. The centre publishes a named intervention — Stalin's "Dizzy With Success," *March 2, 1930* — blaming *cadres*, not the line, and re-explaining the voluntary principle and the district-by-district tempo.
3. A formal resolution two days later (March 5) analysing the errors, declaring that "'Left' distortions were of direct service to the class enemy," and ordering that "persons who are unable or unwilling earnestly to combat distortions of the Party line must be removed from their posts and replaced."
4. Leadership changes in the offending regions.
5. A second clarifying document (April 3, "Reply to Collective Farm Comrades") naming the root cause: wrong approach to the middle peasant, violation of voluntariness, violation of local diversity, skipping the artel.
6. Consolidation, then renewed advance on a firmer base.

And the sentence that names the cost of doing this: "It required the utmost firmness on the Central Committee and its ability to **go against the current** in order to promptly correct that considerable body of Party workers who, carried away by success, had been rapidly straying from the Party line."

**Mechanic:** enthusiasm is a resource that *overshoots*. A node that over-delivers against a correct line produces the conditions for an incorrect one. The correction is cheap if it comes while cadres still trust the centre and expensive if it comes after the excess has produced a grievance the enemy can organize (here: the peasant discontent of February 1930, which "the kulaks and their agents" attempted to convert into anti-Soviet action).

### 8. Temporary organizational forms with sunset clauses

The MTS political departments are a small masterpiece of org design and the book notes both the creation and the dissolution: January 1933, "some 17,000 Party members were sent into the countryside," organized as political departments inside the machine-and-tractor stations; two years later, "since the political departments … had served the purpose for which they had been temporarily created, the Central Committee decided to convert them into ordinary Party bodies by merging them with the district Party Committees."

**Mechanic:** an intervention body that (a) inherits authority, (b) has an explicit task, (c) has an explicit expiry, and (d) is absorbed rather than left to become a rival centre. Most game "committees" are permanent. This one is designed to die, and its dying is the success condition.

### 9. Cadre: the book's own headcount, from *Our Tasks*

The 1939 text gives an internal establishment that any org-builder will recognize:

- 3,000–4,000 "first rank leaders … our Party's corps of generals"
- 30,000–40,000 "middle rank leaders … our corps of officers"
- 100,000–150,000 "lower rank Party command staff … our non-commissioned officers"
- Per leader, **two trained deputies**, so that no post is a single point of failure and so that people "should not remain in their old places too long and begin to rot."
- A school ladder tied to rank: four-month "Party courses" in each regional centre for unit secretaries; eight-month "Lenin courses" in ten cities for district and regional first secretaries; six-month CC courses for town secretaries; a six-month CC conference on internal and international policy for divisional and provincial secretaries and national CCs.
- And a target that is really a succession plan: these comrades "should provide not one but several persons really capable of replacing the leaders of the Central Committee."

**Two mechanics.** (a) *Depth*: cadre is a pyramid with named tiers, and promotion is a training-time cost, not a purchase. (b) *Redundancy*: the deputy rule is an explicit hedge against arrest — the same hedge the book shows failing in 1895 when the arrested leadership's successors had the wrong line.

## Part III — The *Short Course* as a prose and visibility model

The canonical rule — show only what a participant could learn from a communist history book — is operationalized by this book in ways that can be copied directly. What follows is an anatomy, then a list of devices with their Babylon use, then the two things Babylon must *not* copy.

### What it shows

- **Named people, with role and often origin.** "Pyotr Moiseyenko … had been a member of the Northern Union of Russian Workers"; "Khalturin, a carpenter, and Obnorsky, a fitter." Persons are never anonymous when the book wants the reader to feel a decision.
- **Dates to the day when the date matters.** 1 March 1881; 2 June 1897 (the 11½-hour law); 26 May 1927; 7 November 1927; 14 November 1927; 2 March 1930; 5 March 1930; 1 December 1934.
- **Counts, plainly.** 706,000 → 1,433,000 workers (1865–90); 48 strikes and 80,000 workers (1881–86); 221,000 strikers (1895–99, "according to incomplete data"); nine delegates in 1898; 600 arrested at Morozov; 30,000 in the 1896 textile strike.
- **Ratios only where a participant would compute them.** 25% expelled; less than 1% voting for the bloc; 97% of Leningrad reversing; grain marketed at 37% of pre-war; industry at 201% of 1929 while the USA is at 65%; 90% of crop area collectivized.
- **Physical stakes.** "a bread ration of one-eighth of a pound … every other day"; 12–14 hour days; "from 30 to 50 kopeks" of every rouble in fines; the ice on the way to Kronstadt that "broke in places and many were drowned"; Lenin writing between the lines of a book on medicine in milk.
- **Documents as evidence, with citation lines.** Every major claim is underwritten by a quoted resolution with a volume and page number.
- **The enemy's own words, in quotation marks, where they damn him.**
- **Songs and jokes.** The Siberian rhyme about Kolchak ("Uniform British, Epaulettes from France, Japanese tobacco, Kolchak leads the dance"), and Zubatov's confidential line about Lenin — "there is nobody bigger than Ulyanov in the revolution today," followed by the proposal to have him assassinated. The second is the single most useful narrative device in the book: *the antagonist's assessment of the protagonist*, delivered as an intelligence intercept.
- **One-sentence laws.** "Marxism in Russia could therefore grow and gain strength only by combating Narodism." "It is not heroes that make history, but history that makes heroes." "Those who fall behind get beaten."

### What it never shows

- **No internal state as a quantity.** There is no morale, cohesion, discipline-score, legitimacy, or militancy index anywhere in the book. Their *signs* are printed instead: abstentions, expulsions, votes at meetings, leaflets issued, strikes, crowds, arrests, applause ("amidst hearty acclamation"), and silence.
- **No formulae, no elasticities, no marginal reasoning.** Nothing is "efficient." Output is "201 per cent of the 1929 output," never "a 12% return on capital."
- **No psychology.** Motive is class position, careerism, or cowardice — assigned, not analysed. The book will tell you a man was a careerist; it will not show you his interior.
- **No counterfactuals except rhetorical ones**, and those are closed: "Could we have advanced both slogans at once? No, it was not possible."
- **No doubt about the outcome.** The reader always knows who wins. Suspense is replaced by *cost* — the interest is in what the victory took, not whether it arrives.
- **No criticism of the line at the top.** Errors are always *implementation* errors by *local* cadres ("Dizzy With Success" blames the enthusiasts, not the policy). This is the book's honesty ceiling and Babylon's biggest opportunity: a journal that prints the player's own line-level defeats is *more* honest than the model text, and the model text's form survives that increase in honesty intact.

### The devices, and what Babylon does with them

1. **Chapter = a period plus a verb.** "The Struggle for the Creation of a Social-Democratic Labour Party in Russia (1883–1901)," "The Bolshevik Party in the Struggle for the Collectivization of Agriculture (1930–1934)." The title is the task, not the era. → **Chronicle chapter titles are generated from the period's dominant task, in verb form.** Not "1927" but "The year the bloc was broken."
2. **Numbered sections with argument-bearing headers.** The table of contents is itself the thesis: "Lenin's Struggle Against Narodism and 'Legal Marxism'" is an assertion about causality, not a label. → The Wiki's period page gets its section headers generated first, then filled. A player who reads only headers has read the argument.
3. **`BRIEF SUMMARY` at chapter end.** Six to ten declaratives, no new facts, a compressed causal chain that restates the chapter as a lesson. → The **reckoning page**: period-close summary generated under a hard constraint — *entailment only from journal rows already published in that period*. This is the natural home for the existing entailment-eval contract.
4. **The enumerated "because" list.** Ch. 8's eight reasons the Red Army won, each a short declarative with a counter-case for the enemy (they "had everything … They lacked only one thing — the support and sympathy of the peoples"). → "Why we lost" / "Why we held" pages. Lists of causes, not scores.
5. **The Socratic turn.** "What is the way out? … There is no other way out." "The question may be asked: … No, it was not possible." → The narrator's permitted rhetorical move: pose the player's likely objection and answer it in the book's voice. Use sparingly; it is the strongest and most wearisome device in the book.
6. **Evaluative naming.** "Judas Trotsky," "the rascal Sheflo," "generals without an army," "political double-dealers," "the rotten theory." → Permitted in the **Liberated Signal** feed only. The Intel Stream stays adjectiveless. This is precisely the three-feed split the porch session already specifies, and the *Short Course* is the proof that partisan naming is a legitimate register for a participant's document.
7. **The intercept.** Zubatov's assessment; the kulak's overheard argument that "they will be taken away anyhow." → **INVESTIGATE resolutions return the adversary's own words**, which is both the most dramatic and the most honest form of intel: it tells the player what the enemy thinks, not what the engine knows.
8. **Contrast pair.** Stalin at the collective-farm congress sets the old system against the new in two mirrored sentences ("Under the old system … Under the new, collective farm system …"). → Before/after rendering for any structural change: two columns, same categories, no verdict.
9. **The lesson as a reading assignment.** Ch. 11 §4(b) orders Party members to study *how* the Party beat each deviation, because "a knowledge and understanding of the history of our Party is a most important and essential means of fully ensuring the revolutionary vigilance of the Party members." → **This is the in-game Wiki's charter.** It is not lore. It is a vigilance instrument: a catalogue of past errors with the tactics that defeated them, written so the reader can recognize the next one. The Wiki entry for each deviation should be a case file — symptoms, surface arguments, real content, what worked — not an encyclopedia article.
10. **The unremarked table.** The book prints delegate counts and lets the reader do the arithmetic (see the voice/vote drift above). → **The single most important prose rule Babylon can take: print the count, withhold the interpretation.** Counts are facts a participant could know. The trend is an inference the player is entitled to make and argue about on the forum.

### The arithmetic boundary

Extending the canonical test "would Mao have put it in the treatise?" to numbers: **countable world-events may show; derived quantities may show only if a participant could compute them from the same book; engine-computed abstractions never show.**

- Yes: members, delegates, votes, expulsions, arrests, strikers, hectares, poods, roubles, hours, ratios of pre-war output, shares of trade.
- Marginal: percentages the book itself prints for rhetorical effect (25% expelled, <1% voting). Permitted, because the book does it.
- No: cohesion deltas, suspicion weights, infiltration probabilities, sabotage modifiers, "morale 62". The moment these appear the player plays the formula instead of the politics, which is the failure mode the porch session already names.

### Two things not to copy

1. **Teleological certainty.** The book knows, from 1938, that every opponent was always an agent. A game must not: the player's world has no future to read back from, and the whole premise ("no teleology, bias in the start conditions only") dies if the narrator adjudicates retrospectively. The *form* — a participant's history, written with conviction — is usable. The *verdict* is not.
2. **The erasure of the defeated argument's voice.** Defeated positions appear only in paraphrase, and the paraphrase is the winner's. Babylon's journal makes this impossible: the platform was published, the vote was counted, the minority's text sits in the record. That is a *feature of the fiction* (the player's world is better instrumented than 1938) and should be owned explicitly in the Wiki's own voice, not hidden.

## Five open questions for the designer

1. **Does a split have to be a decision?** The book's splits are usually *recognitions* of a split that already exists (illegal press, separate dues). A game where the player clicks "expel" may be modelling the epilogue. The interesting verb may be "investigate" and "publish."
2. **How does the game make a correct line feel costly?** The *Short Course*'s emotional register is exhaustion plus vindication. Babylon has the exhaustion budget (period costs, arrests) but has not yet decided what vindication looks like when there is no teleology.
3. **What is the player's relationship to the Party's own history?** The book insists history study *is* vigilance training. If the Wiki is a case file, reading it should change what the player can see — which is already the Veil of Money's logic. Extend the Veil to organizational theory: Tier 0 sees a quarrel; Tier 2 sees a deviation with a class basis.
4. **Where does the Line live?** A line must be a first-class, named, dated, versioned object — otherwise "line struggle" is a mood. The book always names the line and dates the congress that ratified it. Babylon should be able to answer "what is the current line, when was it adopted, by what vote."
5. **Is the wrecker a role or a state?** *Our Tasks* implies a role with a *performance floor* (they must show successes). If Babylon implements infiltration as a hidden boolean, it will be either unplayable or trivial. The source's model — a node whose outputs are good in aggregate and wrong in specific, load-bearing places — is far better and much harder to detect.
