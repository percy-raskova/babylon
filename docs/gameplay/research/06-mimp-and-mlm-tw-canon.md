# 06 — MIM(Prisons) and the MLM-TW canon: the class question, answered against the player

Read-only briefing, 2026-10-25. Question: **if the country on the map is a net-exploiting country, who is the
player actually organizing — and what is a revolutionary organization even for, at home, in a country whose
modal worker is paid out of imperial rent?**

---

## 0. Thesis

**The American player is not the protagonist of the decisive struggle, and MIM(Prisons) is the only current
in this corpus that says so out loud while still giving the player something to do on a Tuesday.**

The org is not a party waiting to become a party. It is a *node cluster inside a net-exploiting country*
whose strategic product is three things actually available: **public opinion, independent institutions, and
divisions among the exploiters.** The revolution arrives from the periphery; the home org's job is to still
be alive and still internationalist when the rent ceiling cracks.

# Part I — "Who are our enemies?" asked of the wrong country

## 1. The transposition is the whole problem

Mao's 1926 question is a *method* — analyse economic status and attitude toward revolution — and for a
semi-colonial country the answer is that "**our closest friends are the entire semi-proletariat and petty
bourgeoisie.**" Run it on the United States and the answer **inverts at the semi-proletariat**: the stratum
that was the staunchest ally in Hunan is in the core the *beneficiary* of the same relation. MIM(Prisons)
states the correction in a 2011 editorial note to its own reprint of the Mao essay, and it is the
load-bearing sentence of this sweep:

> "Exploitation of the oppressed nations is the exception, not the rule in the United States. Certainly,
> there is no super exploitation to speak of in this country."

**Consequence, and it is hard.** The internal semi-colonies are *nationally oppressed*, not
*super-exploited*. Do not model the Black Belt as a value-transfer victim of the Bangladesh kind. Its
oppression is political, legal, territorial, superstructural — police, courts, prisons, schooling, credit —
and its material position sits *inside* the imperial rent. Not a softening: this is what makes the internal
colony's grievance *satisfiable by political concession*, which is exactly why reform is more available to it
and therefore more dangerous.

## 2. `W_c > V_c` — the arithmetic that decides the question

> "The ultimate or minimum limit of the value of labour-power is formed by … the value of the physically
> indispensable means of subsistence." (*Capital* I ch. 6, via MT10)

The "historical and moral element" is real but is not an escalator to infinity — otherwise "at what point do
the workers have the choice to start selling their stuff to get out of working?" MT10 does the accounting:

| Measure (MT10, 1991–93 data) | Figure |
|---|---|
| US-parent manufacturing compensation, domestic vs. foreign affiliates | $43,091 vs $28,806 per worker |
| Transfer attributable to being in the US | **$136.25 bn** |
| US production workers paid at Mexican rates | $251 bn less |
| White–Black earnings gap, capitalised | **$414.1 bn "in the pockets of the labor aristocracy, courtesy of the capitalists who want their allegiance"** |

**Cope**: the labour aristocracy is "nationalised" — "the entire working class of the imperialist nations is
subsumed into the richest fraction of the world petty bourgeoisie"; "little or no legal exploitation takes
place within First World borders." **Smith** (via Whalen): "$21 an hour can be replaced by a Chinese factory
worker who is paid 64 cents an hour" — global labour arbitrage as "capitalist imperialism par excellence,"
"entirely within the orbit of the capital-labour relation." MIM's own voice, the one to give the game:

> "They do not create surplus value as much as reapportion the surplus which results from super exploitation
> of the Third World. … the ideology which leads white workers to seek more TVs instead of less capitalists
> has a **material basis which is itself a barrier**."

That last clause is the rent ceiling in one sentence: not a failure of leadership — *a material basis*. No
exposure lifts it. Briefing 05 carries this as the second ceiling; MIM supplies the arithmetic and the politics.

## 3. The strata that are actually here

| Stratum | Position | Attitude | Corpus line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Labor aristocracy / white-nation petty bourgeoisie** (majority) | Paid above `V_c` out of Φ; pension, home equity, cheap imports | Pro-imperialist by interest | "bought off by imperialism form a new petty-bourgeoisie" |
| **First World lumpen** (in the semi-colonies) | Excluded from production *and* the wage; parasitic on the petty bourgeoisie | Available — nothing to lose | "a distinct class … only evident in the United States *within the oppressed nations*" |
| **White lumpen** | Same exclusion, different national identity | "national identity pulls them much more strongly towards fascism" | "we will disregard poor whites in most of the analysis below" |
| **Proletariat proper** | Wage relation, value creation | The vehicle — and almost absent | "**virtually non-existent within U.$. borders, limited primarily to the small migrant worker population**" |
| **Internal semi-colonies** | Captive nations; national oppression, not super-exploitation | National-democratic; can lead, cannot be led | "a captive nation … trapped within the geographic bounds of one of the world's most powerful imperialist countries" |
| **Revolutionary youth** | A *strata*, not a class | "Any strata can lead the revolution" | "the most revolutionary group among the white nation because of their special status outside of the class to which they were born" |

**(a) The proletariat is a migrant** — a proletarian node in the US is farmworkers and day labourers, and it
sits *across* the border; that reorganises the Physical Map. **(b) The lumpen is the base and is not
romanticised** — MIM(P) refuses "pork-chop nationalism" and calls the lumpen parasitic; its interest is
*negative*, exclusion from the circuit, hence "**You can't sell dope forever**" as a structural argument.
**(c) Youth is the crack** — "Where there is potential for revolutionary white lumpen, it will be more common
when in close proximity or integrated with oppressed nation lumpen. And these will be the exception to the rule."

## 4. Haywood: the colony has a territory, and the line came before the attitudes

US Blacks are "an oppressed nation" whose territory is the Black Belt, whose content is "the completion of the
agrarian democratic revolution in the South," whose slogan is "**the right of self-determination in the South,
with full equality throughout the country**." Northern Blacks are "an unassimilable national minority":

> "The shadow of the plantation falls upon them throughout the country … Afro-Americans are not only 'a nation
> within a nation,' but a **captive nation**, suffering a colonial-type oppression while trapped within the
> geographic bounds of one of the world's most powerful imperialist countries."

The methodological lesson matters more. Haywood's Comintern teachers told him the CPUSA's wrong practice was
not fundamentally an attitude problem:

> "the white chauvinist attitudes persisted precisely because the Party's line was fundamentally incorrect in
> that it denied the national character of the question."

**Rule:** wrong line produces wrong sentiment, not the reverse. Chauvinism is a *consequence* of a
mis-specified edge (a nation modelled as a race, a class as an identity); correcting the sentiment without
the edge does nothing. A player who runs anti-racism trainings on nodes with mis-specified edges should watch
a number move and the behaviour not.

## 5. JDPON — what winning means here

> "In the case of imperialist nations, a Joint Dictatorship of the Proletariat of the Oppressed Nations
> (JDPON) must play this role where there is no internal proletariat or significant mass base that favors
> communism."

The destination is not "socialism in America" but a **re-civilization stage**: receivership, independent
national courts and police for the oppressed nations, reparations, thought reform, and — Wiki only, never a
tooltip — "instead of sending UN troops to Iraq, Somalia and Kampuchea, the proletarian dictatorship will send
troops from Somalia, Iraq and Kampuchea to patrol the imperialist countries held in receivership." Plus the
limit: "there is a high likelihood that the labor aristocracy will never be re-proletarianized before
communism is achieved."

**Consequence.** No "win the white working class" victory condition — refused not as a lock but as an
*unavailable action with the reason in it*, which `decision_surface.rs` already ships. The endgame pair is
imperial-circuit rupture or fascist consolidation; most of Babylon's declared outcomes fit, and the electoral
door does not, and MIM is why.

# Part II — Practice in the belly of the beast, and the traps that eat it

## 6. The traps, with receipts

| Trap | The tell | Receipt |
|---|---|---|
| **Tailism / workerism** | A demand payable only out of Φ | "little pigs asking for a bigger piece of the pie"; "the difference between the pro-labor aristocracy line and imperialist assimilationism is **non-existent** at this time" |
| **Imperialist economism** | Wage politics where wages are a dividend | "It is no longer progressive to fight for '30 for 40' in the imperialist countries, because those workers are not exploited." |
| **Electoralism** | Confusing a space demand with a distribution demand | "Don't vote, organize" |
| **Faux-vanguard LARP** | Membership test is acceptance of vanguard status | "Pictures of Bob Avakian … plastered all over"; "whether or not candidates consider the RCP as **THE VANGUARD** outside of any discussions of political line" |
| **Focoism** | Heroic action substituting for mass base | "If the focoists survive the battles, they burn out and sell out. The focoist 'revolution' is never more than a flash of fury." |
| **Ultra-left purity** | "How do we not contribute" replaces "how do we end it" | below |

**Imperialist economism has a one-boolean test:** *does the demand name the periphery?* MIM's counter-example
is exact — a global minimum wage, currency pegged to a subsistence basket, WTO-enforced labour and
environmental standards, all falling on "the comprador elites of the Third World" and the core's own labour
aristocracy. A core wage demand that does not name the periphery is a demand to enlarge the rent.

**Ultra-left purity**, in MIM(P)'s worked example (a prisoner proposing inmates stop buying commissary and
send the money home): "You can tell that the motivation is purity because the question is **how do we not
contribute to the system** rather than **how do we contribute to something that will change or end the
system.**" Same page, the symmetric error: rightism is "organizing the majority of the people in the United
States" — "if you're an organization in the First World and have a large following you stand for bourgeois
ideals." MIM names the *reward*: rightism buys growth and legitimacy, ultra-leftism buys moral clarity and
nothing else.

## 7. What practice looks like — five moves

### (i) Division tactics — the verb the corpus is missing

> "**Surrounded by oppressed and exploited we must unite. Surrounded by enemies, we must divide.**"

Every worked example is a material contradiction, not a propaganda line. **Arms workers on strike** were
reactionary under WWII and are progressive now because "the important issue is supplying the war machine" — the
lever is "contradictions among the exploiters over how to divide the loot," never class sympathy; same for
**longshore hold-ups** ("It's not that we agree with their economic aims"). **Prison guards vs. sheriffs** over
convict labour: "if contracts get done for free, their wages are going down … some unions have an interest in
line with our battle for freedom and the lumpen's interests." **City police vs. university police** over
jurisdiction: "the exploiters have contrasting interests and **the proletariat passes through the cracks**."
**INS vs. the Minutemen**: "There is never anything wrong in setting exploiters against exploiters." **The
border**: "When we are among the exploiters our tactic is to divide to allow in." The border contradiction needs
no invention: the labour aristocracy's pension solvency depends on the migrant labour it organizes against —
"the solvency of the labor aristocracy's Social Security and Medicaid programs is at stake, because of the
shortage of the right age of workers."

### (ii) The prison as the front line

> "the U.$. prison system is a major part of the imperialist state used to prevent the self-determination of
> oppressed nations. … we see prisoners in this country as being at the forefront of any anti-imperialist and
> revolutionary movement."

And the reframing one-liner: "**all prisoners are political prisoners because all imprisonment under imperialism
is political.**" The economy is documented well enough to be a system: 2.3m held; $17bn (1980) → $80bn+ (2010),
$261bn with police and courts; aggregate burden **$1 trillion**, "$10 in social costs for every $1 spent on
corrections"; 1,370/100k in Louisiana against ~300 at the bottom, the top eight being "territories of particular
importance to the internal semi-colonies" — Kush, the Black Belt, Aztlán, Sequoyah. The class content is the
gift: prisons were built as a **rural white employment programme** ("imprisoning mostly oppressed-nation people
from urban communities") and prison towns "lobby aggressively for prison construction," yet "in 2017, New
Afrikans made up over 24% of bailiffs, correctional officers and jailers … a growing neo-colonial class within
the semi-colonies that is allying with imperialism to oppress the semi-colonies themselves." **A node with its
internal contradiction already written into it.**

### (iii) The united front, with five enforceable principles

> "This statement does not grant authority to any party over any other party."

**PEACE** (end intra-prison violence; the state's divide-and-conquer is the target), **UNITY** (open lines, true
facts, against rumours and snitches), **GROWTH** (education; the right to leave), **INTERNATIONALISM** ("We
cannot liberate ourselves when participating in the oppression of other nations"), **INDEPENDENCE** ("We build
our own institutions and programs independent of the United $tates government and all its branches, right down
to the local police"). Membership test, precise enough to implement: "Members don't have to agree with
MIM(Prisons)'s cardinal points … **but they can't consciously disagree with any of them.**"

### (iv) Independent institutions — the program point that is a mechanic

MIM's program, point 5: "the oppressed need independent institutions to provide land, bread, housing, education,
medical care, clothing, justice and peace." The central task, reprinted every month: "**to create public opinion
and the independent institutions of the oppressed to seize power.**" The list is unglamorous and therefore
playable: web work; study-by-mail; bulk book purchasing ("Books for Prisoners"); legal work against censorship;
publishing prisoners' writing; a typing pool requiring a postal address and PGP; a book-sharing institution so a
prisoner transferred to a control unit donates rather than abandons a library. MIM(P) calls it its "University of
Thought": people learn "from the sharing and coordinating of materials as a collective group outside of a
for-profit/business structure." **Rule:** an institution keeps producing when its founders are arrested, and its
product is a *good* — a book, a lawyer, a bed, a name — rather than a claim. That is the difference between dual
power and a mailing list.

### (v) The cell, not the party — a deliberate refusal, and the third ceiling

> "We uphold the need for a vanguard party to seize power and build socialism, but **do not fill that role
> ourselves.** … Our principal task is to maintain the prison ministry as a source of educational and
> agitational material."

The chain: the 2005 congress passed cell resolutions; "the current complete decentralization seems to be the
logical outcome"; "there is no center of the MIM today"; "the strength of a centralized party comes into play
when vying for state power … That is not in the cards for Maoists in the imperialist countries at this time."
The security corollary is *What Is To Be Done?* Ch. IV transposed to a country with a bigger budget: "The
Panthers inspired many lumpen with their audacity. Our challenge is to create the same inspiration without
bringing the same attention and repression from the state. … we do not need to advertise to the cops and the
cop-loving Amerikan public."

**This is the third ceiling.** Briefing 05 named two — the *sphere* ceiling (lifted by exposures) and the *rent*
ceiling (lifted only by material crisis). MIM adds:

> **The form ceiling.** No amount of correct line and no amount of crisis converts a First World organization
> into the vanguard of a revolution whose main force is elsewhere. The org wins by **subtraction** (removing
> props), not by **addition** (accumulating a majority).

A game that lets the player grind to vanguard status by doing enough good work is not simulating this corpus; it
is simulating a fantasy the corpus explicitly refuses. Refusing it is what makes the American run *interesting*
rather than merely uphill.

## 8. Size is not a score

> "Size of an organization says nothing about its eventual historical impact … disciplined organizations have
> seized power again and again, [while] mush-collections and individualist organizers have failed again and again."

**Membership is not a resource, it is a composition.** A 4,000-member org with a tailist line is smaller than a
40-member cell with a line, because the 4,000 dissolves at the first repression that makes the tail visible.

# Part III — Contradictions and the five screens

## 9. Φ as the edge-killer

Φ is already `Φ = W_c − V_c` in the corpus. Three rules follow.

1. **The graph is dense where it should be sparse.** Every core worker node already has an edge into the imperial
   circuit — wage, pension, home equity, cheap goods, a mortgage whose rate the Fed sets. Those edges are why the
   org cannot recruit there. Not "false consciousness": weighted edges.
2. **Only Φ falling severs them.** Briefing 05's bifurcation term is the routing question: class or nation? MIM's
   answer is nation *unless an internationalist pole already exists* — and the default is named: "this is what the
   CPUSA and other social-democrats can reap … the Buchananite movement." Cope agrees in shape: "a
   pro-imperialist working class may be both unable and unwilling to forestall it [fascism]."
3. **Φ's floor is the periphery, and it is the one variable the player cannot see directly:**

> "it is unlikely that trade balances or income transfers can really mean much in terms of crisis unless there
> comes **a sudden increase in Chinese or other Third World wages relative to U.$. wages.** It will be the class
> struggle that brings down imperialism."

The Ticker's American numbers are therefore a *derivative*. The independent variable is `periphery_wage /
core_wage`, reaching the player only as Intel Stream fragments, Archive dossiers on Southern workplaces, and the
*absence* of cheap goods on a shelf. A player watching only American indicators will be surprised by their own
endgame — correct, because the same is true of every American organization in this corpus.

## 10. Collective nodes, and eight rules that split or flip them

| Node | Formation | Produces | Contradiction | Flip trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cell** (MIM(P) type) | 3–7, shared line, high security, task division | Study, mail, one campaign | Small by construction — cannot absorb an upsurge | **Vanguard flip** |
| **Mass organization** (USW type) | Open; cardinal points not required, conscious disagreement barred | A newsletter, campaigns, a name list | Cadre control vs. open growth | **Spokesperson flip** |
| **United front** (UFPP type) | Multi-org, five principles, no authority over members | **Peace** — less intra-lumpen violence | Can only enforce by expulsion | **Peace collapse** |
| **Single-nation party** (NAMP/NABPP type) | Territorial claim + national base | Program, legitimacy | Petty-bourgeois leadership vs. lumpen base | **Comprador flip** |
| **Reform NGO** | Grant-funded, staffed, legal | Visitation, legal aid, media | Needs respectability, cannot name imperialism | **Respectability flip** |
| **Spontaneous node** (riot, strike, takeover) | Appears; no formation | Disruption, martyrs, new edges | "Tactics are mistaken for strategy" | **Fanon flip** |
| **State cluster** (DOC / guard local / prison town / county PD / university PD) | Exists; edges are budget and jurisdiction | Repression | **Genuinely divided** — this is the target | **Division** |
| **Imperial node** (TNC, bank, vendor) | Exists | Φ | — | — |

The eight flip rules, as computable conditions:

1. **Respectability** — reach rises while `line_clarity` decays. Symptom: exposures lose name, date, charge; returns no PC.
2. **Spokesperson** — share of *outside* voices in the node's output exceeds *inside* voices. Symptom: bylines change before the line does.
3. **Bigger-slice** — adopts a demand whose incidence falls only on the core. Test: does it name the periphery? Symptom: popular, and the state does not oppose it.
4. **Purity** — refuses every reform. Symptom: base shrinks while the line stays correct.
5. **Vanguard** — membership test becomes acceptance of vanguard status. Symptom: new members recite the line and cannot apply it.
6. **Breeder** — survives a raid intact. Symptom: none. That is the point.
7. **Fascist** — white-lumpen grievance routes to national identity; trigger is the bifurcation term with no adjacent internationalist pole. Symptom: demands become about who is *in* the country.
8. **Comprador** — national-bourgeois leadership converts national grievance into a claim on Φ. Symptom: "a bigger piece of the pie" in national colours.

## 11. Screen by screen

**Topology.** The shape choice *is* the game: cell vs. mass org vs. front vs. single-nation party, each with a
`reach × security × absorptivity` profile. Add the two verbs the corpus supplies and the grammar lacks —
**divide** (widen an edge between two enemy nodes) and **institutionalise** (turn a campaign into a node that
produces without its founders). Carry briefing 04's line/grievance/method triple so a legitimate grievance with a
wrong line is representable. Keep the state's side honest: it *farms* nodes it can see.

**Electoral.** Its job is to show the vote cannot reach Φ. It can buy **space** — MIM's only carve-out:
candidates who support "putting up public bulletin boards all over town and making public space available for
revolutionaries to hold educational events." Space is a topology input (a venue, a legal meeting, a press);
distribution is a rent claim. The DSA character is the most competent person in the room and the least able to
say the word "rent" — "to even call oneself 'democratic socialist' in the United $tates is to give credit where
credit is not due to the rulers." Reparations belongs here and only here, justified material before moral: it
undercuts "the mythology of oppressor nation 'productivity.'"

**Physical map.** The internal semi-colonies are *contiguous counties*, not demographic overlays — Black
Belt/Kush, Aztlán, Sequoyah and Navajo territory, Puerto Rico. Haywood's territorial claim means the layer must
draw a border that does not exist yet and let the player argue about it. Prison siting is a first-class map
object: one facility is at once an oppression vector against an urban oppressed-nation population and a jobs
programme for a rural white one, both in the same hex. The 1,370-vs-300 gradient is already a map.

**Ticker.** Render `W_c − V_c` as *the gap*, never a formula. The player sees wages that keep beating the cost of
living, goods that keep getting cheaper, and a militancy that keeps disappointing. Add the MIM-specific tell: the
*composition* of the wage. A paycheque that is a dividend behaves differently from one that is a cost, and the
difference shows in what the employer does when the worker organizes.

**Wiki.** Where the hard facts live, because they are artifacts rather than tooltips: $43,091 vs $28,806; $414.1
bn; $21/hr vs 64c/hr; 2.3m; $1trn; 1,370/100k; 24% of guards. Hand-written, AI-forbidden entries for *labor
aristocracy*, *internal semi-colony*, *imperial rent*, *super-wage*, *JDPON*, *national oppression vs.
super-exploitation*, *united front*, *cell*. The Wiki must be able to say "this is a net-exploiting country" in a
cited dossier while no tooltip ever does — which is how the corpus teaches value theory, and what porch-01's
visibility rule already requires.

## 12. The asymmetry the game should not waste

The internal colony's grievance is **political**, so a political concession can *satisfy* it — a plebiscite,
independent courts, independent police, a territory. The proletarian's grievance cannot be satisfied by
concession at all. That is why the BPP's tenth point asked for "a United Nations supervised plebiscite," why
Haywood's slogan was "self-determination in the South, with full equality throughout the country," and why MIM(P)
insists the internal semi-colonies have "the right to secede from the United $tates."

Mechanically: **reform is available to the oppressed-nation node and unavailable to the proletarian node.** Not a
bug to balance away — the trap. The concession is real, the relief is real, and the circuit is untouched.

## 13. Proposals

- **P1 — the vanguard role is an unavailable action, not a locked upgrade.** A slot with the reason in it ("no
  internal proletariat; the main force is elsewhere"); under the hood a permanent `form_ceiling` flag.
- **P2 — division is a verb.** Pick two enemy nodes with an existing material conflict and spend to make it
  visible and irresolvable. Edge creation inside the `exploiter` set, gated on a conflict recorded in the data.
- **P3 — reform incidence.** Every demand carries a hidden `who_pays` set; the player sees who screams. Payers
  all core ⇒ `appendage_risk`; naming the periphery ⇒ not.
- **P4 — membership is composition, not count.** The org sheet shows *who*; count is derived, never a headline.
- **P5 — the spokesperson test.** A mass org's byline mix is visible, and a shift in it precedes a shift in line
  by two or three periods.
- **P6 — the institution test.** A campaign becomes an institution only if it produces a good without its founders
  (`autonomous_output`); the player sees a program running after an arrest.
- **P7 — prison siting is a two-sided map event.** −oppressed-nation safety, +local white employment, +budget,
  from the same hex.
- **P8 — the pension/migrant contradiction is always live.** Actuary notices in the Ticker, Minutemen in the
  Topology, and an edge the player can put between them.
- **P9 — chauvinism is downstream of the edge.** Correcting sentiment on a mis-specified edge moves a number and
  changes no behaviour.
- **P10 — the third ceiling speaks.** Reaching past it yields a character, not a padlock: *"we're not a party and
  we shouldn't pretend to be one — here is what we can actually do this month."*

## 15. Open questions for the porch

1. **Does the American run have a win condition at all?** MIM implies three simultaneous conditions — Φ cracks,
   an internationalist pole routes the crack, the institutions survive the repression. Is that a pattern at the
   horizon, or does it collapse into "survive and be correct"?
2. **How does the player learn the class analysis without a lecture?** The corpus's pedagogy is a pamphlet and a
   study group; Babylon's is a Wiki and a refusal to explain. Does the player learn it only from *failure*?
3. **Is the internal colony one node or many?** Haywood: one nation, one territory. MIM(P) names New Afrika,
   Aztlán, First Nations, Puerto Rico, and warns against "pork-chop nationalism" and "Pan-therism" alike.
4. **Do we model the lumpen's parasitic character?** It is the base and it is not a proletariat. Romanticise it

5. **What is the positive program for the white nation?** MIM's answer is negative — recruit individuals, refuse
   strategic unity, divide the bloc. Is "divide" enough for a screen?
6. **Does Φ ever crack from the inside?** MIM says no, absent rising Third World wages. Is the player therefore a
   spectator to their own endgame — and is that the point — or does the home front get to move the periphery
   variable?
7. **Reparations: demand or effect?** As a demand it is a rent claim the ballot cannot deliver; as an effect it
   belongs in the post-JDPON epilogue. Can it be an intermediate object — a *demand that teaches*?
