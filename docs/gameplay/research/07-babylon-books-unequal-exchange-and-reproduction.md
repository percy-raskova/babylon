# 07 — Unequal Exchange, Super-Exploitation, and the Reproduction of Labor-Power

Read-only shelf sweep, 2026-10-25. Question: **what drives the world circuit Babylon puts on screen,
and how does a player feel it without ever being shown a formula?**

**06** owns the class question inside the core; **05** the vanguard; **08** the enemy's manuals; **02**
the surfaces. This briefing owns the **world circuit** and the **reproduction sphere** that supplies
labor-power to it, plus MIM's line across class, nation, and gender.

Guardrail, observed throughout: **Trotskyism is an error, not a source.** Wherever MIM names it — the

# Part I — The material contradictions

## 1. Emmanuel: the wage is the independent variable

The Babylon CSV says, of realization strains, "Centre: Wages follow productivity." **The CSV is wrong.**

> "Wages continue to determine relative prices, and relative prices have no effect on equilibrium
> wages … Increased productivity does not directly increase this basket of goods; it reduces the time
> needed to produce them. … The immediate effect, however, of increased productivity is an increase in
> surplus value, not an increase in wages." (p. 110)

Productivity does not bid up wages; it cheapens wage-goods and raises `s/v`. Core wages are high for a
**political** reason — which is why Φ needs "constant energy input" (Geometry deck, p. 6) and can be
lost. Emmanuel's distinction does the rest:

- **Broad-sense** unequal exchange follows from differing organic composition and prices of production;
  it occurs "in every exchange that occurs under the capitalist system, whether inside or outside a
  nation" (p. 161). Not the imperial relation.
- **Narrow-sense** follows from the wage differential alone. Make Guinea a French *département* with free
  circulation of capital **and people** and narrow-sense UE "would vanish" while broad-sense continues
  (p. 161). Difference "not of degree but of quality."

So **labor immobility is the permissive condition.** Capital crosses borders, labor does not, and the
wage gap becomes a rent surface. This is the most gameable lever in the corpus and it is a *policy* —
border regimes, guest-worker programmes, deportation, passports — not a number.

Emmanuel's summary is the macro-premise: unequal exchange is "the **elementary** transfer mechanism,
and, as such, it enables the advanced countries to begin and regularly to give new impetus to that
unevenness of development that sets in motion all the other mechanisms of exploitation" (p. 265). The
core "succeeded in **exporting impoverishment** so effectively that the forecasts of Marxism … are
being realized to perfection on the scale of *world* economy" (p. 263).

## 2. Marini: super-exploitation is the periphery's *answer* to the drain

Unequal exchange costs the dependent economy surplus value. It cannot stop the loss at the level of
market relations, **so it compensates at the level of production**:

1. **Intensification of labor**; 2. **Prolongation of the working day** (absolute surplus value);
3. **Expropriation of part of the necessary consumption fund** — the wage pushed below the value of
labor-power (pp. 131–132).

One feature unites them, and it is the definition: "the worker is denied the conditions necessary to
replenish his labor power that has been worn away … labor [power] is remunerated below its value, and
thus [these] amount to a **super-exploitation** of labor" (p. 132).

Three consequences a designer will otherwise get wrong:

- **Super-exploitation raises the profit rate, not just the surplus rate.** Low value composition of
  capital means exploitation and profit rise together (p. 132). No internal economic brake — this is
  why the periphery does not catch up by itself.
- **It needs a replaceable labor reserve.** The system can be indifferent to the worker's replenishment
  "provided that new hands can replace him," and this "assumption was always thoroughly fulfilled" via
  Indigenous reserves and migrant flows (p. 139). Kill the reserve and super-exploitation becomes
  self-limiting — a real, non-moral brake the player can discover.
- **Slavery is incompatible with it**, because the slave's minimum is fixed independent of output; the
  free wage has no floor (p. 133). Coercion is not "worse exploitation" on one axis — it is a different
  mechanism (§5).

The structural result is the **fracture**: production is realized abroad, so the worker's individual
consumption "does not interfere with the realization of the product." The domestic market splits into a
"low" sphere (wages, domestic goods, deliberately restricted) and a "high" sphere (non-wage income,
imports, deliberately widened) (pp. 139–140). Two circulation spheres, one economy, and the seam is
visible from the street.

The anti-moralism rule, for the Wiki: "**It is not because the non-industrial nations were abused that
they have become economically weak; it is because they were weak that they were abused.** … this leads
to a call for equitable trade relations between nations, when what is at issue is the need to abolish
international economic relations based on exchange value" (p. 126).

## 3. Amin: imperialist rent is an iceberg with two submerged halves

Amin's model rests on three conditions — "(a) all products are world commodities …; (b) capital is
mobile on the world scale; (c) **labor is not mobile**, and obtains different rewards at the center and
at the periphery" (ch. 4 §1). Then the loop in one sentence:

> "Unequal exploitation is manifested in unequal exchange. Unequal exploitation (and the unequal
> exchange that results from it) dictates inequality in the international distribution of labor. It
> distorts the structure of demand, accelerating self-centered accumulation at the center while
> hindering dependent, extroverted accumulation in the periphery." (§1)

And the epistemics: unequal exchange "makes up only **the visible part of the iceberg.** The concept of
'imperialist rent' … implies a deconstruction of everything constituting 'globalized capitalist
economics'" (Introduction). The unmeasurable second dimension is "unequal access to the utilization of
planetary resources" (§4). Φ therefore has **two channels**, and only the first is auditable.

Amin's conclusion is a late-game possibility space: the visible rent can be confiscated by the South
"only to the extent that they disconnect themselves — if only relatively — by prioritizing in their
development their internal market"; and "the advance posts of the Northern peoples are dependent on
defeat of the imperialist states in their confrontation with the Southern nations" (§8).

## 4. Nkrumah: formal sovereignty, real direction from outside

The neo-colonial state "is, in theory, independent and has all the outward trappings of international
sovereignty. In reality its economic system and thus its political policy is directed from outside."
Three cheap-to-implement, expensive-to-fake mechanics:

- **The revolving credit.** "Aid … is merely a revolving credit, paid by the neo-colonial master,
  passing through the neo-colonial State and returning to the neo-colonial master in the form of
  increased profits" (p. xv). Aid is a *circuit*, not a gift.
- **The rent-funded social wage.** The post-war settlement "deliberate[ly] attempt[ed] … to divert
  colonial earnings from the wealthy class and use them instead generally to finance the 'Welfare
  State'" — adopted "even by those working-class leaders who had before the war regarded the colonial
  peoples as their natural allies" (p. xii). **This is Φ entering the household as a benefit**, and the
  cleanest in-world reason the core's class truce held.
- **Balkanisation** — breaking "former large united colonial territories into a number of small
  non-viable States" (p. xiii). Viability is manufactured, so the map can be gerrymandered.

The counter-force must also be modellable: "the existence of the socialist nations makes it impossible
to enforce the full rigour of the neo-colonialist system" (p. xv) — a *structural* modifier on enemy
coercion, not a moral bonus.

## 5. The reproduction sphere: the submerged base, and where MIM breaks with it

**Mies.** "Women, nature and colonies were externalized, declared to be outside civilized society,
pushed down, and thus made invisible as the under-water part of an iceberg is invisible, yet constitute
the base of the whole" (p. 77). Her definition of the other half of exploitation points away from
Marini's:

> "I define their exploitation as superexploitation because it is not based on the appropriation … of
> the time and labour over and above the 'necessary' labour time … but of the time and labour necessary
> for people's own survival … It is not compensated for by a wage … but is mainly determined by **force
> or coercive institutions**." (p. 48)

Marini means *wage below value* (market relation); Mies means *appropriation without a wage* (coercive
relation). Both are real and they fail differently — the first responds to organization, the second
does not respond to a wage demand at all. Babylon needs both.

Mies's **impossibility theorem** is a rule for every reform the player can buy:

> "the privileges of the exploiters can never become the privileges of all. If the wealth of the
> metropoles is based on the exploitation of colonies, then the colonies cannot achieve wealth unless
> they also have colonies. If the emancipation of men is based on the subordination of women, then women
> cannot achieve 'equal rights' with men, which would necessarily include the right to exploit others."
> (p. 76)

**Meillassoux.** The domestic community "is indeed the only economic and social system which manages the
physical reproduction of human beings … by the ordered manipulation of the living means of reproduction,
that is: **women**" (p. xii); and capitalism "depends both on the domestic communities of the colonised
countries and on its modern transformation, the family, which still maintains its reproductive functions
although deprived of its productive ones." Two mechanics: **a non-commodity reservoir subsidizes
accumulation** and does not refill from wages; and **gerontocratic power is a real power block** — the
elder "controls the group's reproduction" through subsistence advances and the circulation of women, a
hierarchy "based on anteriority (or 'age')" (pp. 42–43). Commodity production does not dissolve it; it
re-rents it.

**Fortunati.** Housework is "a process of commodity production … it produces labor-power as use-value for
value" (p. 79), and:

> "When capital buys the male worker's labor-power with the wage, the latter is not only giving credit on
> the use-value of his labor-power, he is also giving credit on the use-value of hers. **Capital settles
> two credit debts when it pays the wage.**" (p. 78)

The best non-sentimental account of why the household is *inside* the capital relation while outside the
wage: the second claim exists, is enforceable only by custom and coercion, and is invisible in the
contract.

**MIM's dissent, and it governs.** "**MIM has rejected reproduction as the center of gender**, but the rest
of gender can encompass many things" (`garistocracybenefits`). Its candidate for gender's organizing
concept is "access to the leisure-time body, as it existed before money," and its candidate structure is a
**gender aristocracy** — the gender analog of the labor aristocracy, securing access "by closing the
borders," criminalising prostitution, and monopolising the pornographic communications apparatus, so that
"the largest and most dominant pornography business [is] necessarily restrictive, and thus a tool of the
gender aristocracy."

Design fork: do **not** build domestic-labor-theory-of-value as the gender engine. Build (i) the
reproductive reservoir as a material constraint all classes draw on, and (ii) gender as a **ranked field
with a top stratum that gains from exclusion below it**, the border as its instrument. That is both MIM's
line and the better game, because it produces conflict *within* the oppressed.

## 6. What happens when one barrier gives way

One cascade, ordered the same way by all five books: **wage gap + labor immobility** → narrow-sense
unequal exchange → **surplus-value drain** unstoppable at the market → **compensation by
super-exploitation** → **demand distortion** (low sphere suppressed, high sphere import-fed; accumulation
turns extroverted rather than self-centred) → **rent concentrates at the centre** as super-wages plus a
service-stratum parasite — `csdraft`: "the service sector is largely a parasite on productive labor in
different sectors, mainly the Third World productive sector" → the core's class truce is **funded**, not
earned (Nkrumah p. xii) → **the periphery's ruling class is rentier**, its authority external, so its
"reform" is a change of masters → **de-linking or collapse** (the CSV already names it: "triggers
'De-linking' when value drain exceeds surplus needed for simple reproduction"). These are **not five
independent meters drifting down.** They are one loop with one slack variable — the labor reserve — and two
exits: the periphery breaks the relation (and Φ collapses), or the centre runs out of the political energy
that keeps Φ positive.

`The_Geometry_of_Collapse.pdf` supplies the slogan — "Collapse is certain. Revolution is possible.
Organization is the difference" — and one usable idea: percolation, gas → liquid → solid, "PHASE SHIFT:
Liquid state detected. Movement broad but brittle" (p. 10). **Organization has a threshold and a
brittleness phase, and the phase is more informative than the size.** The rest of the deck is a liability:
built on the retired Python engine, it imports "geometry" and "tensor" as *generative* metaphors and treats
Φ as potential energy. The constitution forbids exactly this — BSL cannot add a mathematical primitive, and
only the engine judges mechanics. Keep the slogan, not the physics.

# Part II — Player-visible stakes on the five screens

The rule is the Mao-treatise test (`04`, porch-01 §94): **countable world-events may show; derived
quantities may show only if a participant could compute them from the same book; engine-computed
abstractions never show.** No screen displays Φ, `s/v`, an "extraction coefficient," or a rate.

## 2.1 Household time — exhaustion that reads as a schedule (Ticker, Topology)

The visible object is a **week**: hours at the mill, on the queue, on the wash, with the sick child,
asleep. Fortunati's two debts become a visible **double booking** — the same woman's hours counted once
in the wage and once, silently, in the reproduction of the worker who earns it. What the player sees is a
person who cannot attend a meeting, and the reason is legible without a stat: she is already working.
Mies's invisibility is diegetic, not cosmetic: the Ticker shows wage-hours and simply *does not show* the
rest — while a Topology edge appears (the neighbour who takes the child, the aunt who cooks), because
that invisible infrastructure is being counted for the first time. "She was up until two with the baby"
is a fact in the world; "reproduction deficit 0.31" is the author's desk drawer.

The failure the player must be able to cause: **socializing production without socializing
reproduction.** Mies's finding that socialist accumulation reproduced the dual economy and the same
housewifization should be a discoverable trap, not a lecture — collectivise the mill, leave the household,
get a second shift and a female cadre base that quietly stops coming.

## 2.2 Super-wage erosion and the cheap-import shock (Ticker, Physical map, Wiki)

`06` already rules that `W_c − V_c` renders as *the gap*, never a formula, and that the MIM-specific tell
is the **composition** of the wage: "a paycheque that is a dividend behaves differently from one that is a
cost, and the difference shows in what the employer does when the worker organizes." This shelf sharpens
why: Emmanuel — the super-wage is not paid out of the worker's product, so it cannot be defended by
producing more; Nkrumah — its fiscal half is a *foreign receipt*.

The felt form is ordinary: **the raise that arrives the same month the imports get cheaper, and the plant
that closes anyway.** Two visible events, no explanation, and the player's theory of the world does the
work. Cope supplies the payoff as a stake, not a spoiler: "a pro-imperialist working class may be both
**unable and unwilling** to forestall" fascism.

The import half is `csdraft`'s mechanism: the service economy and the import flood are one fact seen
twice. A town that made a thing now distributes a thing made elsewhere, and its employment is
"unproductive" in the strict sense — it realizes value produced abroad. On the map this is a change in
*what a hex does*: the works becomes a warehouse becomes a call centre, and a wage that was a cost becomes
a subsidy. This is where the aristocracy's ideology strains visibly: `csdraft` records the shift from "end
outsourcing" to "jobs that can't be outsourced" — construction, law — a demand for *immovability*, i.e.
for the rent to be defended by geography. A legible position with a legible cost, which the player can
adopt.

## 2.3 The internal colony — national oppression, not value transfer

`06` is decisive and this briefing defers: the internal semi-colonies are **nationally oppressed, not
super-exploited** — "there is no super exploitation to speak of in this country." MT1's arithmetic is the
reason: of $173bn US corporate profits after tax, "the exploitation of dark-skinned minority workers within
U.S. borders" accounts for "$26.5 billion or 3% of the GNP," and the discrimination-generated wage
differential is about 6% of GNP — "nearly all the profits before taxes" (p. 11). The transfer is real,
superstructural, and **payable in political coin**; that satisfiability is the asymmetry the game should
not waste (`06` §12). MT1's other findings belong in the Wiki as artifacts: "There is no way that the
white working class is exploited" (p. 11), and the two slogans that are a programme in six characters —
the aristocracy's **"30 for 40"** against MIM's **"30 for 300"** (p. 4).

## 2.4 Two orderings of the same four cells — the political overlay

The best mechanic on the shelf, from MIM Theory 2&3, "Diagrams of gender oppression: a picture saves a
hundred pages" (pp. 85–86):

| Cell | By overall oppression | By gender privilege |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — First World biological men | oppressor | top |
| 2 — First World biological women | 2nd | 2nd |
| 3 — Third World biological men | 3rd | 3rd |
| 4 — Third World biological women | most oppressed | bottom |

**The marker between 3 and 4 is the dividing line between male and female.** The same node sits at a
different rank depending on which contradiction you read. MIM then pushes past biology — "gender is a
social construction," and "some groups of biological women systematically oppress other groups of
biological women" — and asks whether "First World biological women are socially constructed as men to
Third World women." The question is left standing, and it is a *question the engine can answer* rather
than a doctrine it must assert.

Two things to build: **gender position as an edge property, not a node attribute** (who has access to
whose time and body, computed from the relation — native to Babylon's public hyperedges); and **the
overlay switch as a decision, not a toggle** — whichever ordering the party acts on determines which
coalition is available and which is destroyed. Reading only class/nation produces MIM's absurdity on p. 86
("Third World women should not complain if they are raped"); reading only gender produces the therapeutic
separatism of p. 77. Let both fail empirically.

## 2.5 The gender aristocracy — the household as a border post (Ticker, Topology, Electoral)

MIM's "magic wand" method — hold the mode of production fixed, change one thing — yields a scenario Babylon
can literally run: open the border to women only, on condition they cannot work or own assets. Competition
arranged through a monopolised pornographic apparatus benefits a stratum of First World women who then
close the border behind them and legislate to raise the price of access. Benefits flow not from superprofits
but from control of access, demography, and the border.

The visible object is concrete and countable: **who is in the house.** A nanny from the periphery, a care
worker on a tied visa, a wet-nurse, a clinical trial. `garistocracybenefits` names them plainly —
"contraceptive testing, wet nursing and nanny care." Each is a Topology edge crossing a border and a Ticker
line where one household's hours fall as another's rise. No formula required, and the thing MIM argues
about — that the oppressed are ranked — becomes felt.

## 2.6 Aid, and what must never appear

**Aid (Electoral, Physical map, Wiki).** Nkrumah's circuit is traceable: money lands, passes through the
state, returns as profit. On screen: a hospital wing with a foreign plaque, a loan tranche, a debt-service
line, a currency that cannot buy the machine it needs. The available move is not "refuse aid" (a morality
choice) but "find the terms" — and the terms are the sovereignty overlay: who directs policy.

**Never on screen:** Φ, `s/v`, "extraction coefficient," ERDI as a live readout, any rate of profit; any
"reproduction deficit" meter, "solidarity" scalar, or "legitimacy" bar; any option summarized by yield
(porch-01 §60 forbids it and this shelf gives no reason to reopen it); any narrator sentence that explains
the causal chain. Marini's loop is only interesting if the player discovers it. The one place a formula may
appear is a **leaked document** (`02`'s doctrine-as-leaked-SOP pattern) — a World Bank country strategy, an
IMF letter of intent, a TNC sourcing manual. The formula is the enemy's artifact and the footnote is the
argument.

# Part III — MIM theory: organizational line and class stand

## 3.1 The line is derived from an arithmetic, and the arithmetic is public

MT1 is one argument in 32 pages: `$173bn` profits, `$26.5bn` (3%) from super-exploiting minority workers
inside the US, a ~70% minority/white wage ratio, a discrimination differential of ~6% of GNP; therefore "there
is no way that the white working class is exploited," and the profits "go almost entirely to the top half of
Amerika" (p. 11). The programmatic consequence is unhedged:

> "MIM does not attempt to organize the white working class **as a group** because it is not exploited and
> does not have a material interest in revolution. Working to organize the white working class would make
> the party a pro-imperialist, reformist party. … The main base for revolution will come from the exploited
> and super-exploited — mainly in the Third World — and MIM seeks to organize all who work in the interests
> of the Third World." (p. 11)

That is a **class-stand** claim, not an identity claim: the party is defined by whose interests it serves in
practice, and the test is what it does when the two conflict. The Wiki entry is a case file, not a definition
(`04`'s charter). MT1 also records the error it argues against — the failure mode to encode: "the RCP
program says that a majority of white workers are objectively revolutionary. By this the RCP means that
white workers are exploited" (p. 4).

## 3.2 The five thought-experiment worlds — theory the engine can adjudicate

MT 2&3 ch. 4 (pp. 72–73) turns intersection theory into **runnable counterfactuals** and says the answers
are checkable rather than declarable: (1) a world where class, nation, and gender oppression are the same
thing — "that's easy, right? This one, no imagination necessary"; (2) class and nation but no gender;
(3) class alone; (4) class oppression rising while gender oppression falls without disappearing; (5) all
three rising together, "impossible to have one oppression without the other" — split into withering away
together (reductionist) or decreasing together (tautological). The attached rule is the design rule:

> "If you are sure of the above categories overlap when you are done or if you come up with cases not covered
> by MCS, label it the case of MCS's making a mistake with the analytical categories." (p. 72)

**This is Babylon's scenario-probe family.** Five start conditions, one engine, and the *player* labels the
result — satisfying the constitution exactly: "AI can parse, retrieve, and narrate. Only the engine judges
mechanics." It also produces forum arguments, which is the point.

## 3.3 Three strands, no reduction

MIM's settled position is "a three strand analysis" of class, nation, and gender, adopted by party vote
(p. 86). The chapter's content is the *reductio* of each single-strand reading — the class-reductionist
"Bourgeois women workers unite!", which MIM names as **Trotskyite revisionism**; the
"experiential-tokenist-liberal" ranking that puts white women above minority men; the nation-only reading
that ends in the Cleaver absurdity. The discipline that survives belongs on a loading screen:

> "there is no free lunch in theory. Once you accept a theory, you should be willing to struggle through its
> costs and not opportunistically close your eyes when there's a snag." (p. 86)

On separatism MIM is unexpectedly permissive and the tactic should stay available: "It is possible to hold a
revolutionary separatist position; there is nothing inherent to separatism that makes it non-Maoist" (p. 77).
What is not allowed is the therapeutic version — the "safe space within the current system" whose "determined
hostility to theory" makes it "a power strategy whose objective is to support and justify the social practice
of the oppression of women" (p. 77).

## 3.4 Class stand in practice: the Ms./PCP case

The clearest test in the archive (`pcp.feminism`, MIM Notes 70, Nov 1992): *Ms.* denounced the Communist Party
of Peru over the killing of María Elena Moyano, praising soup-kitchen organisers and calling the PCP
patriarchal. MIM's reply is the class-stand argument in a paragraph — *Ms.* "spends an entire article
discussing the death of one woman and barely touch[es] the Fujimori dictatorship's killing of thousands of PCP
cadres and civilians, many of whom are women"; the effect is that "First World white women who read *Ms.* can
sift through the facts and decide their own fates, but poor Peruvian women are just stupid followers of a male
oppressor."

Design consequence: the core party will be *offered* alliances with exactly this press and NGO. Accepting
should be mechanically attractive and politically fatal — it buys legitimacy and costs the line. A decision
with a real cost on both sides is the only kind worth building.

## 3.5 Party form: cells, not size

`american.leninism` names the two errors that kill core organizations, and both are *method* errors:
**"sizeism"** (judging a party by membership) and **"pragmatism"** (judging it by visible success) — "size of
an organization says nothing about its eventual historical impact as the Bolshevik party and the Chinese
Communist Party have both already proved in comparison with larger organizations." Read politically and never
displayed numerically, that is what the percolation idea formalizes: what matters is whether the giant
component spans, and in what state. The player should be able to watch a movement go broad and brittle and be
unable to prevent it, because they spent the months before threshold on reach instead of cadres. `05` owns the
rest of the party-form question.

## 3.6 Errors as failure modes, each with its tell

| Error | Tell on screen | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Trotskyism / class-reductionism** | "workers unite" coalition that dissolves once the wage question is settled; the party defends the aristocracy's rent without noticing | MT 2&3 pp. 85–86; `on.trotskyism` |
| **Economism** | every campaign ends in a contract; membership rises, Φ untouched | MT1 p. 11; `csdraft` |
| **Bourgeois feminism** | international press praise; the line softens exactly where the periphery is involved | `pcp.feminism` |
| **Therapeutic separatism** | a space that survives by never taking a position; attrition without expulsion | MT 2&3 p. 77 |
| **Aid-ism / NGO-ism** | projects multiply, sovereignty overlay worsens | Nkrumah pp. xiv–xv |
| **Sizeism** | the org chart grows while the giant component does not | `american.leninism` |
| **Moralism** | the player demands equitable trade instead of confronting exchange value | Marini p. 126 |

## 3.7 What a core party can actually do

`06` §7 already gives the five moves; two of them are re-specified by this shelf. **Contest the border** —
labor immobility is the permissive condition (Emmanuel, Amin), so border politics is the rent mechanism's
control surface, not a distraction from the economy. **Division** — `csdraft` supplies the fissure: "jobs
that can't be outsourced" is a demand for immovability, and immovability is a demand to keep the rent
geographically enclosed. And the class-stand test applies to every decision: does this weaken Φ or
redistribute it downward?

# Part IV — Design rulings (proposed)

1. **Two transfer channels, one visible.** `value_transfer` (measurable, wage-scaling) and
   `resource_appropriation` (unmeasurable, access). Only the first may ever be a number, and only in a leaked
   document.
2. **Two exploitation mechanisms, distinct failure modes.** `wage_below_value` (Marini) responds to
   organization; `unwaged_appropriation` (Mies) responds to force and to destruction of the coercive
   institution. One "exploitation" scalar destroys both.
3. **The labor reserve is the slack variable.** Migration policy sets the periphery's exploitation ceiling;
   core border policy sets the periphery's reserve. One knob, two ends.
4. **Gender is an edge property, not a node attribute**; the four-cell overlay is a decision the player makes,
   not a filter they toggle.
5. **The reproduction reservoir does not refill from wages** — only from time, social provision, or coercive
   extraction. A player who raises wages and changes nothing else will watch it drain, and will be right to be
   confused.
6. **No screen ever shows a rate.** Counts, hours, prices, bodies, votes, hectares, tonnes (`04`, porch-01 §94).
7. **Ship the five thought-experiment worlds as scenario probes**, not a tutorial. The player labels the result;
   the Wiki records the argument.
8. **Φ has no natural decay.** It is a political achievement maintained by borders, repression, and a service
   stratum. Stop paying and it falls — and the fall is the late game.

# Part V — Open questions for the porch

- **Marini's reserve brake vs. Babylon's demography.** Can the model represent a periphery past the reserve
  turning point (Korea, Taiwan, China post-2010) without smuggling in a convergence story? The CSV note —
  "China's shift from 42% (2005) to 16% (2017) of total value transfer" — is the empirical hook, and it is not
  the same thing as development.
- **Where does the periphery's own ruling class sit?** Nkrumah says its authority is external; Marini says it is
  a sub-imperial partner. Those license different overthrows, and the sources do not settle it.
- **Mies's socialist-housewifization finding** is hard for a game whose intended end states are socialist. It
  must not be softened into "and then it was fine," and must not become an argument against socialist
  construction. The honest form: the trap is real, avoidable, and avoiding it costs something specific. What?
- **Is the gender aristocracy a stratum or a relation?** "Access to the leisure-time body" is relational; "the
  too young, too old, the handicapped" is categorical. The engine will force a decision, and it will be visible.

  for the retired Python path. The Ricci/Hickel series in the CSVs are real and citable, but every table above
