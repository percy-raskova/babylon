# 08 — Networks, Policing, and Insurgency: the enemy's own manuals on the Topology screen

Read-only shelf sweep, 2026-10-25. Question: **the Topology screen is a graph under fog — what
does the graph do to the people inside it, and what does the state actually see?** Siblings own
the party's internal line (04) and the vanguard's topology (05); 02 owns the surface inventory.
This briefing owns the bourgeois security-studies shelf, read **against the grain**: as a map of
the enemy's blind spots, not a suppression manual. Every one of these books was written to help
a state win; Babylon's use of them is inverted. Sources are listed at the end.

---

## 1. Why the Topology screen works the way Sparrow, Borgatti, and Scott say it must

### 1.1 Sparrow's real finding: the map is a portrait of the analyst

The famous passage is the six centralities. The usable passage is the attack on Anacapa charts —
the actual practice of police link analysis:

> "the determination of centrality will depend upon who you know most about, rather than who is
> central or pivotal in any structural sense… it may incline an agency to pay closest attention
> to those it already knows most about, individuals who may not in fact be the principal
> characters."

So the fog is not a UI nicety bolted onto a correct model — **the fog is the model.** The holes
are shaped like the investigator's priors, and Sparrow closes the "collect more data" escape: the
incompleteness "will be anything but random — it will be systematic… in accordance with the
biases introduced by investigative methods." Hence his dismissal of the global measures: "fuzzy
boundaries render precise global network measures (such as radius, diameter, even density) almost
meaningless." The state cannot compute a diameter honestly; its network has no edge.

### 1.2 Which of Sparrow's tools the state can use, and the five it cannot

Sparrow ranks six centralities and keeps three — **betweenness** (removal lengthens everyone's
paths), **point strength** (removal raises the component count), **business** (equilibrium
transmission load) — discarding degree as a targeting criterion and closeness/Euclidean as
arbitrary under fuzzy boundaries. Then the move that matters for a game: **set strength**, the
component increase from removing a *set* (Menger), with the note that the interesting cutsets
are those *inside the agency's jurisdiction*. That is a specification for three legitimate verbs
and two captured ones:

| Verb | Ground | Effect on the player's graph | Cost to the state |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sweep** | "known-by" closure (05) | takes every node the transcript links to a seized node | spends only what it already knew |
| **Cut** | point/set strength, Menger | severs a flow; graph intact but slower | needs the cutset *before* acting |
| **Strike** | Kalyvas's selective violence | removes one named person at a near-hegemonic moment | needs a denunciation; makes a grievance |
| *Monitor* | degree | watches the loudest node | wastes a thread on the node you wanted watched |
| *Suppress* | composite risk score | hits the visible node | hits visibility, not load |

**The five documented blind spots — this is the exploit surface:**

1. **Degree, not betweenness.** Degree is a by-product of prior collection. The bridge is
   low-degree and structurally invisible to an agency that sorts by what it already knows.
2. **Weak ties read as noise.** On the IRA: cells are dense cliques, "particularly difficult to
   infiltrate"; command traffic runs on channels that "look exactly like Granovetter's weak
   ties," and the channels worth monitoring are "those which are seldom used and which lie
   outside the relatively dense clique structures." Volume-based collection cannot see them.
3. **No substitutability test.** Removal damage is centrality × non-substitutability. Two
   structurally equivalent nodes mean the arrest bought nothing; the dossier has no such field.
4. **Role and person collapse.** Template matching (the FBI's *Big Floyd*) compares a node to an
   idealised role; blockmodelling compares nodes to each other. Neither handles "an individual
   who played more than one role" — and dropping the zero-matching requirement is exactly what
   lets a decoy pass. **A node playing two roles is a legal invisibility cloak.**
5. **Metric capture.** *Informing Enforcement* is about how information systems "enshrine old
   values, focus attention on outdated and inappropriate performance measures… cast in concrete
   old ways of doing business." The state counts what it can count; the player chooses what is
   countable.

### 1.3 Borgatti–Everett: every view of the graph is a coarsening

Role equivalence is defined through **orbits**: two nodes are automorphically equivalent if a
label-preserving permutation maps one onto the other. The algorithm is a refinement — split by
degree, then by successively higher-order neighbourhood degree vectors — and the asymmetry is
the point:

> "the actual orbits can only be a finer partition than that produced by the algorithm."

That is the screen's epistemology in one line. There is a true partition; every observer holds a
strictly coarser one; the coarsening is produced by *which invariants the observer bothered to
compute*. The state computes degree and geography; the player computes solidarity and material
interest; the historian computes neither. The later role-*similarity* measure softens the binary
into a gradient, which is what a screen needs: "these two cadres are 0.8 the same role" is a
sentence; "same orbit" is not. So **do not draw one graph** — draw the player's partition with
the state's coarser one superimposed, and make the gap the game.

### 1.4 Scott: legibility remakes the territory, and mētis is what it destroys

> "They were, moreover, not just maps. Rather, they were maps that, when allied with state
> power, would enable much of the reality they depicted to be remade."

A cadastral survey does not describe tenure; it creates a tenure regime that can be taxed. The
state's dossier is therefore not a description of the player's organization — **it is the mould
the organization will be forced to become.** Once the state has inscribed "the Party is the paper
and the paper is Kovalenko," what survives is what fits the inscription.

Scott's second gift is *mētis*: the uncodifiable knowledge of people who actually do the thing —
his example is a work-to-rule strike, where "by merely following the rules meticulously, the
workforce can virtually halt production." For the player, formalizing (rulebook, paper, named
cadre) buys scale and spends mētis, so the screen should show formalization as *edges becoming
legible*, never as a stat rising. For the state, the high-modernist planner believes the abridged
map *is* the territory; counterinsurgency bureaucracy is that planner in uniform, which is why
its campaigns fail.

*Hidden Transcripts* supplies the third layer. Every relation has a **public transcript**
(performed before power) and a **hidden transcript** (spoken behind its back), and subordinates
have "a self-interest in conspiring to reinforce hegemonic appearances." **The quiescent village
is performing quiescence** — cheap for the performer, expensive for the observer. Infrapolitics
(foot-dragging, gossip, pilfering, dissimulation, false compliance) is the substrate the player
organizes out of, and it is invisible to every instrument the state owns.

### 1.5 The synthesis

Three layers, all rendered: **Territory** (engine — the graph as it materially is; never drawn,
only inferred from consequences), **Transcript** (state — dossiers, aliases, "known associates,"
arrests; coarser, ghost-populated, confidently wrong), and **Hidden** (player — debts,
relationships, the thing said in the kitchen; edges with provenance and temperature).

Sparrow gives the transcript its *shape*; Borgatti proves no observer holds the true partition;
Scott supplies the *politics*. Together: **the Topology screen is a screen about the
impossibility of the Topology screen, and the player's skill is reading which coarsening they
are looking at.**

## 2. Concrete contradictions inside insurgent organizations

### 2.1 Weinstein: the organization is rotted by its own success, through who joins

*Inside Rebellion* is the best book on the shelf for Babylon because it makes organizational
quality a **derivative of the recruitment base**, not a virtue leadership possesses: **endowments
→ recruitment → control → governance → violence → resilience.**

- **Economic endowments** (lootables, patrons, easy money) let leaders *pay*, attracting
  **consumers**: "where participation involves fewer risks and individuals can expect to be
  rewarded immediately… groups tend to attract consumers," who fight opportunistically.
- **Social endowments** (shared belief, dense prior networks, ideological ties) let leaders
  *promise*, attracting **investors**: "where participation is risky and short-term gains are
  unlikely, rebel groups tend to attract only the most committed investors."
- The consequence is forced, not chosen. Opportunistic groups "must permit indiscipline in order
  to maintain their membership, while holding on to the reins of military strategy"; they cannot
  commit to non-abuse, so they extract coercively, so civilians resist — "a cycle of civilian
  resistance and retribution by group members that spirals quickly out of control." And it is
  hard to walk back: "activist movements tend toward opportunism, a change in strategy which is
  difficult to reverse."

**Design reading.** No "discipline" meter. Discipline is a *composition effect*: the growth
curve selects the membership that will destroy the growth curve. Every expropriation, patron,
lootable revenue stream and rush of post-victory recruits is a **composition shock** to the node,
and the player sees only symptoms — orders obeyed slowly, a village that stops volunteering, an
action that overshoots. That is fog *inside* the player's own organization, the one fog the genre
has not exhausted. It also blocks a temptation: ideology must not be a button. Weinstein's
investors are not nicer people; their material situation made a promise credible. Ideology works
*because* it is a commitment technology where pay cannot be.

### 2.2 Kalyvas: violence is a transaction, and the state's blindness is manufactured

*The Logic of Violence* supplies the microfoundation the screen has been missing: **why the
state's raids land where they land.**

1. Control, not sympathy, determines collaboration: it "precludes options other than
   collaboration by creating credible benefits for collaborators and, more importantly,
   sanctions for defectors."
2. Selective violence needs private information, held only by civilians, who choose to sell or
   withhold, so "selective violence is the result of transactions between political actors and
   individuals: it is jointly produced by them."
3. Denouncers speak only where counter-denunciation is impossible — where the state is *nearly*
   dominant. Hence "violence is most likely to occur where one actor is near hegemonic, not where
   this actor is in full control or is being contested."
4. Without information the state substitutes category for person — "we do not know insurrectos
   and bad men from good ones, so we are often compelled to arrest all alike." Indiscriminate
   violence is not ideology; it is **an information failure with a doctrine.** Denunciation is
   also moral hazard — "civilians may effectively use political actors to settle their own
   private conflicts" — so the state's intelligence is partly a market in private scores.

**Design reading.** The raid is a transaction among three parties, not an enemy action. Making a
county illegible does not hide the player — it **shifts the state's violence to the nearest
county where the state is nearly hegemonic**, where denunciation is safe and partly false. The
Vietnamese cadre: "the Front is strongest in villages which haven't been shelled… Front cadres
need quiet." The player wants the shelling and must not be attributable for provoking it — the
best political mechanic on the shelf, and 02's "blowback the state's assess step attributes to
its own strike" is already its embryo. Corollaries: the **informant is a node with two owners**,
not a spy unit with a loyalty bar — the player's only instrument is what they say in front of it
— and the **near-hegemonic band is a terrain feature**, not a "control %": its width is where the
state's violence is efficient, and the player's job is to keep it narrow and moving.

### 2.3 Wood: participation is not purchasable

> "Participation per se expressed outrage and defiance; its force was not negated by the fact
> that victory was unlikely and in any case was not contingent on one's participation."

Wood names the motive no utility function contains — **the pleasure of agency** — and insists
political culture "evolved in response to the experiences of the conflict itself"; her campesinos
occupy land they already have access to. So there must exist an action that costs resources and
returns nothing measurable, and it must be correct to take it. If the engine prices it as waste,
the engine teaches something false about politics. Corollary: **commitment is an edge property,
not a node stat** — willingness is a function of what a node has already done.

### 2.4 Arquilla & Ronfeldt: topology is doctrine, with honest limits

- **Three shapes, three uses.** Chain (end-to-end traffic passes through intermediaries), hub
  (everyone goes through the centre), all-channel. Real actors are hybrids: "an all-channel
  council or directorate at its core but… hubs and chains for tactical operations." **Shape is
  per-task, not per-organization** — all-channel for politics, hub for finance, chain for
  supply, each vulnerable to a different state tool.
- **The acephalous claim, precisely.** "No single, central leadership, command, or headquarters
  — no precise heart or head that can be targeted… acephalous (headless)… polycephalous
  (Hydra-headed)." Defensively, "the deniability built into a network affords the possibility
  that it may simply absorb a number of attacks on distributed nodes, leading an attacker to
  believe the network has been harmed… when, in fact, it remains viable."
- **Swarming is pulsing, not massing.** "A sustainable pulsing of force and/or fire… coalesce
  rapidly and stealthily on a target, then dissever and redisperse," from "pods organized in
  clusters." Its information requirement is the highest of the four historical forms (melee,
  massing, maneuver, swarming), so a comms cut hurts a swarm more than a mass.
- **The limits.** Of ten prominent cases 1994–2000: five substantial successes, three limited,
  one failing, one failure — the failures being networks against "hierarchical authoritarian
  governments willing to use substantial force." Chechnya I won; Chechnya II lost because the
  state learned. And: "even a networked swarm force will have a hard time dealing with guerrilla
  forces enjoying the support of a populace that can sustain, hide, and nurture them."

**Design reading.** Every topology choice costs something the state can exploit: all-channel is
decapitation-proof and expensive to hide; chain is cheap and fragile at exactly one point.
Chiapas says the decisive terrain was legitimacy — the EZLN never won militarily; a
transnational swarm made repression too expensive. That is a graph property *plus* a public.

### 2.5 Jones & Libicki, and Paul/Clarke: the base rates, and no scoreboard

Of 648 groups (1968–2006), 268 ended completely: **43% politics, 40% policing, 10% victory, 7%
military force.** A further **136 splintered**, excluded for a precise reason: "the end of a
group does not signal the end of terrorism by its members." Religious groups are far harder to
end (32% vs 62% overall; none achieved victory); size predicts victory; and once in an
insurgency "it does not end easily" — 50% negotiated, 25% victory, 19% military defeat.

**Design reading.** The state's optimal play is politics plus policing, so the player's real
enemy is not the raid but **absorption**: a seat, a legal party, a ministry, a paragraph in the
constitution. The victory condition cannot be "survive the raid"; it must be **"become
unabsorbable"** — a political property, not a graph property.

*Victory Has a Thousand Fathers* adds the warning. Thirty COIN cases; 15 good and 12 bad
practices; "effective COIN practices run in packs" — every win had a positive balance, every
loss zero or negative. The authors conclude with a literal scorecard: "assess the presence or
absence of the factors… and add them up." **The game must not honour that.** If the engine
implements 15-minus-12, the state becomes a solvable optimisation and the player's job becomes
arithmetic. The correct implementation is that the practices *correlate because they share a
cause* — a state that is competent, legitimate and locally embedded does many good things at
once — while a state that buys them individually gets the bad ones free. **Correlation, without
a linear engine.** The leaked SOP may print the scorecard; the engine may not.

**The state does not defeat the player. The player's growth curve does, and the state is the
instrument that makes the curve visible.**

## 3. Translation: collective nodes and contradictions on the Topology screen

### 3.1 What a node is

A Babylon node is **a collective**: a workplace committee, tenants' assembly, congregation,
mutual-aid network, militia, distribution crew, faction. This is where Babylon departs from the
whole shelf, and the departure is the design. Sparrow's nodes are individuals, so removal is
arrest or death and substitutability is a person with the same address book. A collective node
has **internal composition** (consumers and investors), **internal contradiction** (the
reformist chair and the revolutionary shop steward), and its own legibility problem (the minutes
versus what the committee does). Therefore: **decapitation is a composition shock, not a
deletion** — remove the chair and the node does not vanish, it changes composition, which is the
change the state failed to model. **Substitutability is organizational, not personal** —
Everett & Borgatti's role equivalence, not Lorrain & White's structural equivalence, and the
state tests the wrong one. And **a node can be a split waiting to happen**: 04's splits and
Weinstein's drift are one mechanic from two sides, since line struggle is how a collective
resolves a composition problem it cannot otherwise name.

### 3.2 The four faces (tracked, never displayed)

| Face | What it is | Player sees | State sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Capacity** | what it can do this period | what it did last period | what it was arrested for |
| **Commitment** | what it has already risked | who shows up, who sends word | nothing |
| **Visibility** | how much is inscribed | who is talked about, who is named | the dossier |
| **Function** | its role-equivalence class | "we have three of these" / "one" | a template match |

No numbers. Capacity shows as events, commitment as behaviour, visibility as the state's
artifacts, function as the player's own sentence — "we have no one else who can run a press."

### 3.3 What the player sees

Edges with **provenance, not weight** — `told`, `seen`, `shared a cell`, `owes a debt`, `is
watched` — the only honest edge attribute and the only thing that makes the Intel ledger a graph
rather than a list. **Temperature, not strength**, since links "wax and wane from one period to
another." **Two overlays with the gap drawn as a shape**: dashed edges the state believes, solid
edges it does not, ghost nodes it invented, and, the cruel one, **solid player edges the state
also has**, which are the ones about to be used. And the hidden transcript as texture — who
speaks in the meeting, who speaks in the yard, who has stopped speaking at all.

The state, by contrast, sees only §1.2's five blind spots at once — degree-sorted,
template-matched, alias-collapsed, ghost-populated, metric-bound. That asymmetry (the player sees
relationships, the state sees records) is the entire screen.

### 3.4 Seven moves that make graph manipulation thrilling without exposing formulas

1. **Build the bridge in the open.** Let the state watch a link form between two clusters; it
   computes degree, sees a new high-degree node, monitors it. Real traffic runs on the weak tie
   beside it. *You are being watched on purpose.*
2. **Manufacture a twin.** Duplicate a function so removing either half changes nothing. Cost:
   density, and density is interceptable. *Insurance paid for with exposure.*
3. **Play two roles.** Editor and treasurer in one body defeats template matching; the risk is
   the state's *other* template catching the overlap. *Hiding inside your own job.*
4. **Feed the transcript.** Let a false edge be inscribed — a meeting that was a wedding, a
   payment that was rent. Cost: the state's map becomes half yours, and you start believing it.
   *Editing the enemy's archive.*
5. **Live in the near-hegemonic band.** Be almost dominant in one county so the state's logic
   sends its selective violence there, where denouncers are safe and denunciations false.
   *Steering a machine that thinks it is steering you.*
6. **Provoke the blind giant without fingerprints.** Turn the state's information failure into an
   indiscriminate action, then be the only force on the ground afterwards. The prohibition is
   attribution: the atrocity must read as the state's incompetence, not your provocation.
   *Winning by being attacked — the moral hazard.*
7. **Absorb or refuse absorption.** Taking the seat converts the graph into a hierarchy with a
   legible head, which is what Jones & Libicki say usually ends movements; refusing costs
   legitimacy with the reformist nodes. *The trap that looks like victory.*

Nothing of this appears as a number. No centrality readout, no risk score, no "network health";
no preview of what a raid will take (closure is computable, but *which* nodes the state holds is
fog); no discipline or commitment meter; and no scoreboard for the state either, because a
readable enemy is a solvable puzzle.

## 4. Design rulings this shelf forces

**R1 — Fog must be systematic, not random.** The state should be *predictably wrong in a
learnable direction* (degree, geography, template, alias), so skill is model-error exploitation.
This resolves 02's honest-unknown-versus-false problem: **unknown where no informant exists;
false only where the state has a source**, because that is when a false value is *produced*.

**R2 — The player's view is also a coarsening.** If only the state is blind, the screen becomes an
omniscient dashboard with a villain overlay. The player's partition needs its own systematic
error: **the player sees solidarity and misses material interest** — over-reads commitment,
under-reads the consumer — and Weinstein's symptoms are the tell.

**R3 — No single lever, on either side.** Good practices must be co-produced by a common cause,
so buying one alone yields the bad ones. The leaked scorecard is a document, not a function.

**R4 — The collective node must not decay into an individual with stats.** Compress its internal
contradiction into one loyalty number and every move in §3.4 becomes arithmetic. The
contradiction *is* the content: two named people in one body who want different things.

**R5 — Degeneration is material.** Composition drift, information markets, formalization and
absorption explain organizational rot without any metaphysics of bureaucracy. No "degeneration"
event, no "correct line" meter — a recruitment base, a revenue flow, a rulebook, a negotiating
table, and the rot follows.

## 5. Open questions for the designer

1. **Does the transcript have a history?** If the dossier accretes across periods — stale edges,
   dead aliases — the player can inherit and exploit an *old* error, which fits the Archive's
   `observed_period`/`acquired_period` machinery.
2. **Can the player read the state's partition, and at what price?** The interesting version: only
   through a node that is also the state's, so reading the enemy costs a piece of your graph.
3. **What is the unit of a split — people or functions?** It decides whether splits feel political
   or statistical.
4. **Is absorption a loss state, an ending, or a phase?** At 43% it is the most common real
   outcome; "no prescribed endings" suggests a recognised pattern at the horizon, but it must be
   unambiguous enough to be feared.
5. **How does the state learn?** Chechnya II and the Zapatista counter-strategy both say it does,
   slowly and expensively. A state that updates doctrine after two failures is a better
   antagonist than one with a bigger budget — and it makes the player's signature moves expire.

## 6. Closing

Sparrow's *systematic* incompleteness, Everett & Borgatti's *refinement*, Scott's *remaking*;
Weinstein's composition drift *inside* each collective node, so the player's own success selects
the members who will undo it; Kalyvas's price on state violence as a transaction in denunciation,
so illegibility is not hiding but *redirection*, and the atrocity the player needs is one the
player must not be seen to have caused; Arquilla & Ronfeldt for the shapes and the limits; Jones
& Libicki to make absorption the real danger; Paul/Clarke to keep the state from becoming a
scoreboard; and Wood's standard throughout — **there must be an action that costs everything and
returns nothing measurable, and it must be the right move.**

That is a Topology screen where the player is not optimizing a graph. The player is trying to
keep a body of people from becoming the thing the state has already written down.
