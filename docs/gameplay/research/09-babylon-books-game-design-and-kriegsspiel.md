# 09 — Babylon Books: game design, Kriegsspiel, and the topology screen

Research briefing, read-only sweep, 2026-10-25. Three design problems answered from the shelf: (1) how a
graph of collective nodes becomes a *game object* rather than a diagram, (2) why hiding the arithmetic does
not destroy the decision, (3) how to draw a contradiction that visibly reaches its limit and flips.

**Read:** Debord, *A Game of War* (Atlas Press, 2007 — rules booklet pp. 11–35, in full); Sylvester,
*Designing Games* (2013 — ch. 1, ch. 4 pp. 90–95, ch. 5 pp. 129–131, ch. 9); Salen & Zimmerman, *Rules of
Play* (2004 — ch. 3, uncertainty, ch. 18); Shneiderman, "Direct Manipulation" (*IEEE Computer*, Aug 1983);
Tufte, *Envisioning Information* (1990 — ch. 1–4) and *Visual Explanations* (1997 — ch. 1); Marx,
*Mathematical Manuscripts* (New Park, 1983 — "On the Concept of the Derivative" pp. 4–7; correspondence
pp. XXVIII–XXIX).

**Ground truth used, not re-derived:** `docs/gameplay/2026-10-25-porch-01.md` (visibility rule, five screens,
ceremony), `porch-handoff.md`, `research/02-systems-and-surfaces.md` §0 for what is live
(`OrganizerWorkspace`; verbs `inquiry`, `reinforce`, `hold`, `configure-standing-work`). §4 is **proposal**,
not shipped behaviour.

## 0. The three findings, flat

| Problem | Source | Answer in one sentence |
|---|---|---|
| The topology screen reads as a diagram | Debord | A unit's entire strength is a function of one topological fact — is it connected to an arsenal — so the lines are the game and the territory is scenery. |
| Hiding multipliers will kill the decisions | Sylvester + Salen & Zimmerman | It only kills them if you hide the *outcome*; hide the arithmetic, publish a **named qualitative state**, and the decision survives. |
| A contradiction should hit its barrier and reverse | Marx (via Engels) | `dy/dx` is not a small number but the **surviving relation** left after the finite difference is cancelled; a saturated relation passes through an indeterminate moment and returns with its roles reversed. |

Debord supplies the **vocabulary of states** (in communication, severed, dislodged, invested). Marx supplies
the **law of state change**. Tufte supplies the **display grammar**. Shneiderman supplies the **interaction
grammar**. Sylvester explains why the player will believe it.

## 1. Debord: the topology *is* the game

**The object.** A 25×20 board, two armies of fifteen fighting units, five moves per turn, one optional
attack. Combat is deterministic addition of factors in range. What the game is remembered for is this:

> "All a fighting unit's offensive and defensive value, and all its mobility, are entirely dependent on the
> necessity for that unit to remain in communication with one or another of its army's arsenals … it
> represents the organic integrity of an army." (p. 17)

A cut line does not weaken a unit. It switches it off: "the isolated unit is condemned to immobility and
stripped of all offensive and defensive capacity. It is defenceless against attack and may be destroyed at
will by any enemy unit stationed (or brought) within firing range" (p. 18). Infantry in a fort defends at 10;
cut the line and it defends at nothing and dies to one enemy unit. No attrition curve, no morale decay, no
partial penalty. The state is binary and topological. Hence:

> "strategy in this game is more often concerned with movement against the adversary's communications than
> with either offensive action directed against first one and then the other enemy arsenal, or with the
> wearing down of enemy strength." (p. 19)

and the tension that makes it a game rather than a puzzle: "an army whose battle lines become
indistinguishable from its lines of communication will quickly lose its tactical manœuvrability … and may
well find itself partly or completely surrounded" (p. 19); "flanking movements inevitably expose one's own
opposite flank: **the flanker will be flanked**" (p. 23). Every attack on a line is an exposure of a line, and
that tension is generated by the graph, not by unit stats.

**Territory is explicitly worthless:** "a war in which territory per se is of no interest: the sole concern is
with the tactical or strategic positions that are necessary to an army or deleterious to its adversary"
(p. 24). This is the sentence Babylon needs most. The physical map and the topology screen are not two views
of one thing. Holding the hex holds nothing; what matters is whether the *relation* through the hex is
intact. Debord's mountains are "an absolute barrier" that "block all lines of communication" — terrain that
exists only to shape the graph.

**Four node kinds, four rules:**

| Object | Takeable? | Rule | Babylon analogue |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Arsenal** | No — "may serve its own side only"; destroyable only | Root of all communication; lose both, lose the war | The organization's own infrastructure: print shop, legal fund, cadre school, the paper |
| **Fort** | Yes — "serves whichever side is in possession" | 6→10 defence and a permanent line; bonus passes wholly to the occupier | A workplace, union local, shop committee. Flips whole, defensive value and all |
| **Mountain pass** | Yes | 6→8, blocks cavalry charges | A chokepoint institution: the only bridge between two counties' politics |
| **Communications unit** | Destroyable, defenceless (DF 1) | Redirects the network unlimited distance; **the only unit that may move while out of contact, but it cannot relay while out of contact** | The organizer sent into an unreached county: can travel, cannot connect until arrival |

That last rule is the sharpest in the book. The relay cannot relay while disconnected. Babylon's `reinforce`
is exactly this: an organizer can *be there* and still not *transmit*. Presence changes nothing until
contact is made.

**The three-outcome threshold — the most transferable mechanic on the shelf.** Combat is a comparison with
three named outcomes, not damage: offence ≤ defence → **resists**, nothing happens; exceeds by **1** →
**dislodged**, must vacate as the first move next turn and may not contribute to any attack that turn;
exceeds by **2+** → **destroyed**; dislodged with nowhere to vacate → **destroyed ipso facto**. No hit
points, no partial damage. One point separates "nothing" from a named, visible, tempo-costing defeat, and the
attacker learns only which of three things happened. This is a complete answer to the visibility problem,
published in 1987. **Quantize the output; never publish the inputs.**

**Debord's own quantity-into-quality sentence:** "any such quantitative loss … may moreover quickly turn, on
the tactical plane, as soon as an army's total offensive strength sinks so low as to preclude all
counter-attack, into an irreversible qualitative inferiority on the battlefront" (p. 16). Loss is quantitative
*until* the army can no longer counter-attack; then the loss changes kind. A threshold, not a curve. Same
shape as §3.3.

**Attention as friction.** Fifteen units, five moves: "less than a third of the forces available at the outset
may be moved in one turn (a consideration corresponding to the 'friction' that slows down all movement in real
warfare)" (p. 21). The scarcity is not a currency to spend; it is the permanent statement that attention
binds. And "a player who would keep all will lose all" (p. 24).

**What he left out, on purpose.** He names the absences (p. 25): no weather or night; no morale or fatigue
except the instant paralysis of severed units; and "it leaves no room for doubt about the enemy's positions
and movements … exact and certain knowledge of all enemy movements is vouchsafed instantly. *L'ost sait ce
que fait l'ost*" (p. 26). He built perfect information and named it the flaw. Babylon's fog, intel ledger and
Veil of Money are the correction he could not make on a flat chequered board. Note the distinction: *his*
uncertainty is structural (you cannot see the plan), *ours* is epistemic (you cannot see the consciousness).
Babylon should never hide *where* a node is, only what it thinks. And the best one-line brief in the book:
"success or failure will depend in every case on the action (or inaction) of the adversary … though the basic
principles are certain, their application is always a matter of doubt" (p. 24).

## 2. Sylvester: the fiction carries what the arithmetic cannot

**The engine.** Mechanics generate **arousal** with no fiction at all (Pong does this). Fiction supplies the
**cognitive label** for it — the two-factor theory of emotion: "there is no physiological difference between,
say, anger and fear. We just label them differently." Immersion is then "the player's experience mirror[ing]
the character's", built from flow, then arousal, then labelling. The consequence is not "add story" but **the
fiction names the feeling the mechanics already produced.** If the mechanic produces nothing, no label sticks.

**The fiction layer is a metaphor engine, not a skin:** "the entire fiction layer of a game is a giant
metaphor … Imagine learning a complex video game if it were represented only by abstract shapes. But then we
wrap them in the appearance of a growing city or an ancient war, and every relationship and system becomes
clear … its simplest and most basic reason for existence is to help players understand the system through
metaphor" (p. 221). This is load-bearing for Babylon. The treatise voice is not atmosphere; it is the **user
manual delivered as literature.** *On Contradiction* explains a system of relations to a reader who has never
seen a graph — which is precisely the fiction layer's job. A player who can retell last period as a paragraph
of political history has understood the model. A player who can retell it as an equation has been handed a
spreadsheet.

**Apophenia: the story generator lives in the player.** "Apophenia is the human tendency to see imaginary
patterns in complex data" (p. 92). And: "the computer doesn't have to [simulate a mind]. It need only do
enough to make the player's mind interpret something in the game as being an intelligent agent … The game
itself is still just moving tokens. But in the player's mind, those moving tokens betray a deeper subtext of
intrigue and desire. The king is afraid of that pawn coming up. The knight is on a mad rampage. The rook is
bored" (pp. 92–93). Three cheap techniques follow:

- **Labeling.** "the emergent story in the player's mind did not actually happen in the game systems" (p. 93).
  *Medieval: Total War* gives generals the trait "Drunkard" instead of a low Leadership stat, so the player
  loses because the general loved his family too much. Same maths, different object.
- **Abstraction.** "Showing and telling players less creates more room for apophenia to fill in the gaps"
  (p. 94) — and "the need for abstraction is why player-spun stories most often emerge from strategy games,
  building games, economics sims" (p. 94). Babylon is in the right genre. This would not work in a
  first-person game.
- **Recordkeeping.** *Civ IV* silently snapshots borders each turn so the player can watch the map redraw at
  the end. Babylon's wiki and `observed_period`/`acquired_period` already are this; treat them as a narrative
  device, not an archive feature.

**Where he contradicts the Mao rule — and where he does not.** He is ferocious about hiding outcomes: "events
only have emotional value if players perceive and understand them. That which is never communicated might as
well never have occurred at all" (p. 219); "a useful FAQ is a warning sign. When a text file makes a game
noticeably better, that game is probably information-starved" (p. 129). And equally fierce about hiding
answers: "a decision is about seeing the correct answer implied by the information given. If the answer is
already plainly stated in the information given, there is no mental process in seeing it. The thought process
vanishes; the decision is no longer a decision" (p. 130).

So the axis is not *visible vs hidden*, it is **outcome vs answer**. The player must perceive that something
happened and roughly what it cost. The player must not be told which option yields more. The 2.25× multiplier
is not banned because it is a number; it is banned because it is an **answer**. "The local folded; forty
members lost; the county is now hostile" is information, not an answer, and Sylvester demands it be shown.
Which makes the design duty heavier than "hide the maths": every hidden multiplier must be *replaced* by a
legible surface effect, or it becomes information starvation in a Maoist costume. §4 is mostly about paying
that debt.

**Salen & Zimmerman supply the test:** "meaningful play occurs when the relationships between actions and
outcomes in a game are both **discernable** and **integrated** into the larger context of the game" (ch. 3).
*Discernable*: the result is communicated perceivably — their borrowed examples are an off-screen unit
attacked with no notification and a party member starving with no hunger display, both ending "why should the
player have to guess at such game-critical information?" *Integrated*: the action "affects the play experience
at a later point"; their counter-example is a decathlon whose footrace doesn't count, so players walk it.
Porch-01's failure mode ("spreadsheet popups, visible math, gameable multipliers") is only half the risk. The
other half is an outcome that is **not discernable**: a period where numbers moved and the player cannot say
what happened. A multiplier hidden behind nothing is not mystery, it is noise.

**Which loops must be stable, which must run away.** From their cybernetics chapter: a system is "a sensor, a
comparator, and an activator", and a game is a nesting of such loops; "negative feedback stabilizes the game.
Positive feedback destabilizes the game"; "positive feedback magnifies early successes. Negative feedback
magnifies late ones." Two mappings to hold deliberately. **State attention is negative feedback** — heat
rises, repression follows, heat falls; without it the first strong move ends the game. **Capital accumulation
is positive feedback** — Wc > Vc funds investment which raises Wc; it must be allowed to run away, because it
is the engine of the fiction. That gives the mechanical content of "a contradiction hits its barrier": **a
saturated positive loop must invert into a negative one.** Not fade, not plateau — invert. Accumulation
becomes devaluation; growth becomes crisis; the organizer becomes the accused. §3.3 is the formal shape; §4.5
is how to draw it. On uncertainty they are structural rather than decorative — "if the outcome of a game is
predetermined, the experience cannot provide meaningful play" — and they distinguish *risk* (known
distribution) from *uncertainty* (unknown), noting that chess, with zero randomness, produces more felt
uncertainty than roulette. Babylon is deterministic. That is only a problem if the player can compute the
future, which is the Mao rule argued from the other direction.

## 3. Shneiderman, Tufte, Marx: how to draw a contradiction

**Shneiderman: act on the object, not through a form.** He is reporting on why some systems produce "glowing
enthusiasm" and most produce "grudging acceptance": "the central ideas seemed to be **visibility of the object
of interest; rapid, reversible, incremental actions; and replacement of complex command language syntax by
direct manipulation of the object of interest**" (p. 57). Four principles follow (p. 60): continuous
representation of the object of interest; physical actions or labelled buttons instead of complex syntax;
rapid incremental reversible operations whose impact is immediately visible; a layered or spiral learning
path. Claimed outcome: "users gain confidence and mastery because they initiate an action, **feel in control**,
and can predict system responses."

The mechanism is the syntactic/semantic split. **Semantic** knowledge — "the concepts or functionality" — is
"well-structured, relatively stable, and meaningfully acquired." **Syntactic** knowledge — the keystroke, the
delimiter, the command name — "is arbitrary and therefore acquired by rote memorization … volatile in memory
and easily forgotten unless frequently used." This is the argument for topology over any allocation form, and
it is stronger than aesthetics. A form ("40 cadre-hours into AGITATE, 20 into ORGANIZE") makes the player hold
the model's semantics in their head while performing syntax with their hands. Clicking an edge and watching it
thicken or snap moves the semantics into the object. Two cautions from the same paper, which the porch should
keep: "a graphic icon, although meaningful to the designer, may require as much or more learning time as a
word," and "the wrong information, or a cluttered presentation, can lead to greater confusion" (p. 59). He is
not selling pictures. He is selling *directness*. A graph that needs a legend is a form with extra steps.

**Tufte: two scales in one mark, and narrative without chartjunk.** *Micro/macro*: "in all these micro/macro
designs, the same ink serves more than one informational purpose; graphical elements are multifunctioning"
(pp. 46–47). His exhibit is a Japanese train timetable — at a glance the distribution of 292 departures, read
closely every individual train time, correct. Detail and panorama in one mark, no drill-down, no second
screen. Babylon's topology needs exactly this: **a node readable as one county's dossier and as a dot in a
national pattern, in the same mark, at the same moment.** If the player must click to learn a county is hot,
the graph failed at macro scale; if they must open a table to learn whose county it is, it failed at micro
scale.

*Layering and separation* is "among the most powerful devices for reducing noise and enriching the content of
displays … visually stratifying various aspects of the data" (pp. 33–35) — necessary because of Albers' **1 + 1 =
3**: "when two elements show themselves along with assorted incidental by-products of their partnership …
always a continuing danger to data exhibits." Four overlays in one ink do not read as four things; they read
as one smudge. The prescription is not fewer layers but *distinct* ones — different weight, value and mark, so
the eye can unstack them.

*Small multiples* "move to the heart of visual reasoning — to see, distinguish, choose … their multiplied
smallness enforces local comparisons within our eyespan, relying on an active eye to select and make contrasts
rather than on bygone memories of images scattered over pages and pages" (ch. 1). This answers "how do we show
period-over-period change without an animation the player will miss": not a transition, not a diff number, but
**the same graph N times, side by side.** The change becomes a comparison the eye makes for free.

*Causal narrative*, from *Visual Explanations*: Snow's 1854 cholera map yields rules that bear on the ticker.
"Descriptive narration is not causal explanation; the passage of time is a poor explanatory variable,
practically useless in discovering a strategy of how to intervene and stop the epidemic" (p. 29). Snow had the
same data as everyone, ordered by date; he recast it **from time into space** — deaths against pumps — and the
cause became visible. Babylon's ticker is a time series; the topology is the space. Then: "the deep,
fundamental question in statistical analysis is **Compared with what?**" (p. 30). Snow's proof is not the
cluster of deaths but **the brewery** — seventy workmen who drank beer rather than the water, none dead — and
the workhouse, five deaths out of 535 where a hundred were expected. The non-victims carry the argument. So
never show a hot county without a cold one; the control is part of the display, and it is the fiction's best
rhetorical device ("why did Akron not rise?"). Lastly, "considering alternative explanations and contrary
cases … the point is to get it right, not to win the case, not to sweep under the rug all the assorted puzzles
and inconsistencies that frequently occur in collections of data" (p. 32). Snow prints the deaths that do not
fit — the cabinet-maker on Noel Street, the widow at West End who had pump water carted to her — then explains
them, and the explanation is what makes the case undeniable. That is the job of the Corporate Feed and the
Liberated Signal: an honest record that prints its own anomalies. A chronicle with no discordant entries is
propaganda; a game with no discordant outcomes is a rail.

### Marx: `0/0` as the moment the relation survives the cancellation

Care is needed. These are working notes, not finished doctrine, and modern analysis defines the derivative by
limits. What is *not* overstatement is that Marx explicitly refuses the limit reading and treats `dy/dx` as a
**qualitative result of a cancelled quantitative process**. That shape is worth stealing. Three verified
passages.

**(i) The difference really is zero.** "In order to obtain the 'derivative', x₁ must be set = x; therefore in
the strict mathematical sense x₁ − x = 0, **with no subterfuge about merely approaching infinitely [closely]**"
(p. 7).

**(ii) `0/0` is not nothing; it is a symbol whose meaning has transferred.** "Since in the expression 0/0 every
trace of its origin and its meaning has disappeared, we replace it with dy/dx, where the finite differences …
appear symbolised as **cancelled or vanished** differences" (p. 5). He calls the rival reading a chimera: "the
closely-held belief of some rationalising mathematicians that dy and dx are quantitatively actually only
infinitely small, only approaching 0/0" (p. 5).

**(iii) The vanished quantity does not vanish; it is demoted to a moment, and a new relation survives.**
"Although we set x₁ = x and therefore x₁ − x = 0, nonetheless nothing symbolic appears in the 'derivative'. The
quantity x₁, although originally obtained from the variation of x, **does not disappear; it is only reduced to
its minimum limit value = x. It remains in the original function of x as a newly introduced element** which …
finally produces the 'derivative'" (p. 7). His draft footnote gives the three stages: first "a finite
differentiation (endliche Differentiation)", giving "a preliminary derivative, which is the limit value of
Δy/Δx"; then "the process of differentiation … reduces this limit value to its absolute minimum quantity
(Minimalgrösse). The quantity x₁ introduced in the first differentiation does not disappear" (p. 7n). That is
**Aufhebung** exactly: cancel, preserve, raise. The finite difference is abolished as a magnitude and retained
as a relation.

**The reversal.** Engels states what Marx's procedure has done to the logical order: "at last we see clearly …
that **the differential-quotient is the original, the differentials dx and dy are derived**"; "to pass from the
special case to the general relationship, **the special case must be abolished (aufgehoben) as such** … x′ can
be allowed calmly to become x again; it is no longer the old x, which was variable in name only; it has passed
through **actual change**, and the result of the change remains, even if we again abolish (aufheben) it"; and
"the fundamental difference between your method and the old one is that you make x change to x′, thus making
them really vary, while the other way starts from x + h, which is always only the sum of two magnitudes, but
never the variation of a magnitude" (Engels to Marx, November 1882, pp. XXVIII–XXIX). The rate is prior to the
quantities it relates. What looks *derived* (the slope) is original; what looks *given* (the two increments) is
the shadow. A relation that survives the cancellation of its terms is more real than the terms.

*Provenance: the "differential quotient is the original" passage sits on p. XXVIII and ends "Yours FE", so it
is Engels writing to Marx, not Marx's prose. Marx's own refusal of the limit is in the manuscript at pp. 5–7.
The design reading below is mine.*

### The design translation: `0/0` is a visible state, not a hidden number

| Marx | Babylon |
|---|---|
| The original function | The standing relation between node and world: dues, reach, obligation, rent |
| Finite differentiation, Δy/Δx — "the relation as it happens in the course of real change" | The period's visible, countable flow: members gained, funds collected, votes shifted |
| x₁ = x; Δ → 0 | The tick. The period closes; the flows are settled and cancelled |
| `0/0` — every trace of origin gone | The moment the player cannot compute; last period's numbers no longer predict this one |
| `dy/dx` — the surviving relation, carrying x₁ as "a newly introduced element" | The new standing relation, which now contains the consequence of the period inside it |

The last row is the mechanism the porch has been looking for. **The period is not a calculation the player is
denied; it is the cancellation that produces a new object.** The player sees the object, never the
calculation. The *flip* is the case where the relation saturates: when accumulation runs to its limit `dy/dx`
stops being a determinate rate and passes through the indeterminate moment — any outcome is possible, the old
relation no longer governs. What emerges is a **new relation with the roles reversed**, where the accumulated
quantity is now the given and what was given is now derived. The union that accumulated membership becomes an
institution with dues and a landlord's interest; the cadre who accumulated authority becomes the accused; the
county that accumulated heat becomes the county that accumulates repression. Quantity crossed into quality, and
the crossing is *drawn*, not narrated. Debord had the board-game version: "as soon as an army's total
offensive strength sinks so low as to preclude all counter-attack, into an irreversible qualitative
inferiority" (p. 16).

## 4. Proposals

Each is a rule with a test, so it can be argued with and dropped.

**4.1 Make *reach* the first-class edge, not affiliation.** Two edge kinds only: **communication** (receives
orders, funds and line — Debord's arsenal rule) and **contact** (adjacent to something that does — his indirect
rule). Affiliation, ideology and faction belong to node fill. *Test:* can the player answer "what dies if I
lose this node?" by looking?

**4.2 Severance is a state, not a penalty.** A cut edge yields the *severed* state: the node contributes
nothing, can be taken at will, and shows it. No decay, no red percentage. *Test:* severed vs intact legible in
a one-second glance at a thumbnail.

**4.3 Replace multipliers with three named outcomes.** Every contested action resolves to **holds / yields /
breaks** (resist / dislodge / destroy), plus **trapped → breaks** when there is nowhere to retreat. Publish the
outcome and its immediate consequence; never the factors. *Test:* a player can narrate last period without
quoting a number, and cannot rank two options by expected value without inferring.

**4.4 Make cadre attention a slot count, not a budget.** N slots per period, freely assigned, visible and
*countable* — which is what the Mao rule permits ("concrete countable world-events may show"). Debord's
five-of-fifteen friction, and also the honest model of an organization. *Test:* the player feels the slots
before they feel the money.

**4.5 Draw the barrier as a shared boundary, not an animation.** When a positive loop saturates, show the
relation reaching a drawn limit and reversing *on the same mark* — thick edge becomes thin, arrowhead turns.
Small multiples make it readable without motion: same graph at t and t+1, side by side. *Test:* the uncaptioned
pair conveys "it turned".

**4.6 Write one glyph that carries three facts.** A node shows scale (how many people), state (severed /
contested / held / flipped) and confidence (known / stale / unknown) in a single mark. Tufte's
multifunctioning ink. *Test:* shrink to a thumbnail; the national pattern survives.

**4.7 Three inks, never four.** Material facts in one weight, political facts in a second, absence of
knowledge as its own mark — not a zero, not grey, not a question-mark glyph. *Test:* print in greyscale; if two
layers merge, they were one layer.

**4.8 Always ship the control.** Every "here is what happened" carries a compared-with: the county that did not
rise, the local that did not fold, the period before. Snow's brewery. *Test:* no causal claim in the intel
stream without an adjacent comparison.

**4.9 Keep the record loud.** Labels, not stats, on the persistent record: "the Akron local was isolated and
folded" beats "cohesion −0.4". *Test:* the wiki reads as history; a reader who has never seen the game can
follow it.

**4.10 Do not fog position, fog intention.** Debord's one admitted flaw was perfect information. Keep the map
honest and deny the one thing that matters. *Test:* the player is never surprised by *where*, only by *what it
means*.

## 5. Where this could be wrong

- **The quantization may be too coarse.** Debord resolved one attack per turn; Babylon resolves many
  interactions per period. Four states may read as four shades of nothing. *Settle:* greybox one period's
  outcomes and ask a player to narrate it.
- **Apophenia needs gaps, and gaps read as bugs.** Sylvester's abstraction argument and the fog design pull the
  same way, and both can be mistaken for missing data. Honest-unknown rendering is the entire mitigation; if it
  looks like a loading state, the fiction is dead. *Settle:* show the screen to someone who has not read the
  design and ask what they think is broken.
- **Marx's refusal of limits is a reading, not a theorem.** The design use is the *shape* — cancel, preserve,
  reverse — and it does not depend on the manuscripts being good mathematics.
- **"Integrated" cuts against the ceremony.** Their decathlon example wants actions to visibly feed later
  periods; the three-layer answer deliberately defers some consequences. The reconciliation is that the
  *receipt* must be integrated even when the *outcome* is deferred: the player must know a debt was opened, even
  if they cannot yet read the bill. This is the sharpest open question in the briefing.

## 7. In one paragraph

Debord proves a graph becomes a game the moment one topological fact — is this node still connected — carries
the whole value of the node, and that contact can resolve to three named outcomes instead of a number.
Sylvester proves the player will build the story out of almost nothing if the fiction names what the mechanics
did, and that hiding *answers* is right while hiding *outcomes* is fatal; Salen & Zimmerman supply the test
(discernable and integrated) that separates the two. Shneiderman says act on the object itself; Tufte says make
one mark carry two scales, separate the layers, and always show what happened *compared with what*; Marx says a
cancelled quantity leaves behind a relation that survives it, and that a saturated relation returns with its
roles reversed. Together: **the topology screen is not a diagram of the model — it is the model, drawn at the
only resolution where the player can watch a contradiction arrive, and the arithmetic stays in the machine
because the relation, not the number, is what carries forward.**
