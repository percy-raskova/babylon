## 0. Thesis: the threshold is a two-key lock, and the second key is not the player's

Every single-clause answer fails against the sources. "Call a congress" fails on Minsk 1898. "Wait for the objective moment" fails on the fact that 1905 found a party which had spent two years building a newspaper abroad, and 1917 found one that had spent fourteen. "The dialectical unity of both" is true and useless until someone says which part is the player's.

> **Key A — the act of constitution.** A body with authority declares itself the party, sets a
> boundary, and accepts what the boundary costs. This is a decision. In Babylon it is the
> player's.
>
> **Key B — recognition by the other contingents.** The declaration is ratified by the
> behaviour of everyone who is not you: rivals begin arguing in your vocabulary, the state
> reallocates repression toward you, adjacent formations fuse into you or collapse beside you.
> This is not a decision. It is an observation.

Lenin states Key B with unusual bluntness, and it is the most load-bearing sentence for the whole design:

> "For it is not enough to call ourselves the 'vanguard', the advanced contingent; we must act
> in such a way that all the other contingents recognise and are obliged to admit that we are
> marching in the vanguard." (*WITBD*, ch. III)

Corollary, and it should govern the Topology screen: **there is no `declare_vanguard()` verb.** There is `constitute_party()`, and it can fail.

## 1. Three historical answers, and what each proves

### 1.1 Minsk, March 1898 — the declaration without the apparatus

Nine delegates. The Central Committee elected there was arrested almost immediately. The *Short Course* is scrupulous about both halves:

> "In its decisions and in its Manifesto the congress announced the formation of the Russian
> Social-Democratic Labour Party. It is this formal act, which played a great revolutionary
> propagandist role, that constituted the significance of the First Congress." … "But although
> the First Congress had been held, in reality no Marxist Social-Democratic Party was as yet
> formed in Russia. The congress did not succeed in uniting the separate Marxist circles and
> organizations and welding them together organizationally. There was still no common line of
> action in the work of the local organizations, nor was there a party program, party rules or
> a single leading centre." (*History*, ch. I §4)

Lenin had filed the same verdict from Siberia in 1902: "The formation of the Party in the spring of 1898 was the most striking and at the same time the last act of the Social-Democrats of this period," followed at once by a third period of "disunity, dissolution, and vacillation" (*WITBD*, Conclusion). **A declaration that does not change capability is a rumour with a letterhead.** It still buys something real — "a great revolutionary propagandist role" — and Babylon should sell exactly that: mass legitimacy, zero capability, and a name that now has to be defended by an apparatus that does not exist.

### 1.2 London, July–August 1903 — the split is the constitution

Rule 1. Lenin's draft: a member accepts the programme, supports it financially, **and participates personally in one of the Party organisations.** Martov's counter-draft: accepts the programme, supports it financially, **and renders regular personal assistance under the direction of one of the organisations** — seconded in debate by Trotsky, then an Iskra supporter, who left the congress a Menshevik. Martov's version carried the vote.

That defeat is the founding act of Bolshevism, not a setback inside it. Lenin dates it that way: "When it came into being in 1903, Bolshevism took over the tradition of a ruthless struggle against petty-bourgeois, semi-anarchist (or dilettante-anarchist) revolutionism" (*LWC*, ch. IV). What made a *tendency* into a *party* was that the boundary was drawn and the losers were left outside it.

The difference is not pedantry. Martov's clause makes membership a **relation of assistance**; Lenin's makes it a **position inside an organisation**. The first is satisfiable by a sympathiser with a bank transfer and a good mood. Every downstream Leninist mechanism —dissolution, expulsion, purge — requires the second. **A party whose membership clause is a mood cannot expel anyone, because there is no one to expel.**

Design consequence: the 1903 fork decides whether the later forks exist. Take the assistance clause and you get a larger, faster, more legitimate organisation that can never pass Gate 2. Take the participation clause and you get a smaller organisation with a boundary, which is the precondition for all of it.

### 1.3 Prague, January 1912 — the expulsion that constitutes

The Sixth All-Russia Conference met in Prague with fewer than twenty delegates, assumed the functions of a congress, and expelled the Liquidators. Lenin's own summary, in a private letter two and a half years later, says what the act was *for*:

> "We excluded the liquidators' group from the Party in January 1912. The result? Have they
> set up a better party?? None at all. What they have is the complete break-up of the August
> bloc — aid to them by the bourgeoisie, desertion of them by the workers. Either accept our
> conditions, or no rapprochement, not to speak of unité!!" (to Inessa Armand, July 1914)

Two measurements in one paragraph. The expelled formation did not become a rival party; it became a front for the bourgeoisie and lost the workers. And the August bloc — the Trotsky-organised unity front of 1912 — "completely broke up." The unity of everyone who calls themselves Social-Democrat is not a strength; it is the thing that dissolves when a boundary is drawn through it.

The conference did not merely subtract. Its resolution on tasks demanded "intensive work to re-establish the illegal organisation of the R.S.D.L.P., which more than ever before takes advantage of all and every legal possibility" (*Prague Conference*, res. 2). **Expulsion and construction are one operation with two outputs.**

### 1.4 What the three cases jointly prove

| Case | Act | Apparatus behind the act | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minsk 1898 | declares a party | none: nine people, CC arrested, no programme, no rules, no centre | agitational value only; three years of disintegration |
| London 1903 | draws a boundary | an existing paper, an existing cadre, 43 delegates | a tendency becomes a party-in-formation; survives 1905 and 1907–10 |
| Prague 1912 | expels and rebuilds | fourteen years of cadre, an illegal apparatus to return to | an independent party; the form that meets 1917 already made |

Not "declaration then threshold," nor "threshold then declaration." **Each act is real only to the extent that an apparatus already exists to enforce it, and each act builds the apparatus that will enforce the next one.** 1898 had the act and no apparatus. 1912 had both, because 1903 had produced the cadre 1898 could not. That is the dialectical unity in question, and it has a direction: the act is always ahead of the apparatus, and the apparatus is always catching up to the last act.

## 2. Lenin's criteria, as actually stated

### 2.1 Recognition is the test, not the claim

*WITBD* ch. III supplies the whole of Key B in one image: a Social-Democrat announces to the liberal constitutionalists that his group is the vanguard, and the constitutionalist — "if he is at all intelligent" — silently laughs, because "your 'vanguard' must be made up of simpletons." The liberal already wants to draw workers into trade-unionist politics; a group that wants the same thing is not a vanguard, it is a competitor with worse funding.

Three recognition tests, none of which needs a hidden variable:

1. **Do rivals adopt your vocabulary?** When other contingents argue about your formulations instead of their own, the boundary is real. Before that you are one more position in a marketplace.
2. **Does the state move repression toward you?** The Okhrana's own assessment is the cleanest scoreboard in the corpus: Zubatov's confidential report held "there is nobody bigger than Ulyanov [Lenin] in the revolution today," and on that basis considered assassination expedient (*History*, ch. I §5). **The enemy's budget is the only non-self-reported metric of vanguard status.**
3. **Do adjacent formations fuse or collapse?** Both are recognition. Surviving comfortably beside you is neither.

### 2.2 "Party principles" is a list of four things

The only place in the corpus where Lenin enumerates, in one sentence, what must be owned before it is safe to open the doors:

> "If that occurred, the Party would be dissolved among the masses, it would cease to be the
> conscious vanguard of its class, its role would be reduced to that of a tail. … But the fact
> is that no such 'ifs' exist. … we have demanded class-consciousness from those joining the
> Party, we have insisted on the tremendous importance of continuity in the Party's development,
> we have preached discipline and demanded that every Party member be trained in one or other
> of the Party organisations. We have a firmly established Party programme … We have resolutions
> on tactics which were consistently and systematically worked out … We also have some
> organisational experience and an actual organisation." (*Reorganisation of the Party*, 1905)

Four assets: **a programme, tactical resolutions with a history, organisational experience, an actual organisation in which every member is trained.** The sentence defends opening membership to thousands of new workers in 1905 — the argument is not "grow slowly" but "grow fast *because* the four assets exist." Absent them, the same influx turns a vanguard into a tail. This is the opposite of a purity mechanic; do not implement it as one.

### 2.3 The dissolution power is the definition of a centre

*One Step Forward* part D concerns the Yuzhny Rabochy group: it "had done useful work when there was no real party, but … had become superfluous now that the work was being centralised," and "from the standpoint of the interests of the Party, its dissolution, its 'assimilation' in the Party … was essential."

The test of a centre is not that it can be elected. It is that it can **dissolve a body which has done nothing wrong** and have it stick. Anything that cannot do this is a federation with a stationery budget. It is also why the Minsk CC's arrest mattered more than its resolutions: the power to dissolve is a physical fact about who can reach whom.

### 2.4 Unity of will is an exercised capacity, not a mood

The Tenth Congress resolution names what unity is *for* — "genuine team-work that really embodies the unanimity of will of the vanguard of the proletariat" — then dissolves every platform-based group, attaches "unconditional and instant expulsion" to non-observance, and extends the power to the Central Committee's own members.

The detail that matters is *when*: March 1921, at the end of a faction fight the leadership had just lost control of, with Kronstadt in the background. Unity of will is not the party's normal state; it is a **capacity exercised at the moment a faction would otherwise become a second centre.** Model it as an action with a cooldown and a legitimacy cost, not a stat that decays.

## 3. Stalin's six features — the rubric, and the clause that does the work

*Foundations of Leninism* ch. VIII organises "The Party" under six headings. The mirror lacks the chapter, so the headings are rubric and each is anchored to a present text. The content is not Stalin's invention; the ordering is.

| # | Feature | Lenin's text | Babylon test |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Advanced detachment** — the best, most conscious part, not the whole class | *WITBD* ch. III (tribune vs. trade-union secretary); *LWC* ch. V | Is the line ahead of the most advanced workers, or behind them? |
| 2 | **Organised detachment** — membership is a position in a body | Rule 1, 1903; *Reorganisation* | Can a member be located, tasked, and expelled? |
| 3 | **Highest form** of class organisation — above unions, co-ops, parliamentary groups, which it leads | Stalin 1923 on "transmission belts" | Does the org set the unions' line, or does the unions' interest set the org's? |
| 4 | **Instrument of the dictatorship of the proletariat** — the party is not the power, it is the tool of it | *LWC* ch. V (party / class / leaders / masses) | Is the org's reproduction subordinate to the seizure of power? |
| 5 | **Unity of will**, incompatible with factions | Tenth Congress resolution | Can a faction be dissolved and stay dissolved? |
| 6 | **Strengthened by purging itself** of opportunists | Stalin 1920 quoting Lassalle: "The party becomes strong by purging itself"; *LWC* ch. IV (1908 expulsion of the Lefts) | Does an expulsion raise capability, or only cut headcount? |

### 3.1 Feature 4 is the anti-SPD clause, and it is the only discriminating one

Features 1, 2, 3, 5 and 6 were all present in the German Social-Democratic Party in 1914, in greater quantity than in the Bolsheviks: more members, a bigger press, a bigger parliamentary group, a tighter organisation, a more celebrated theoretical tradition, a longer record of expelling deviants. It failed anyway, and Lenin's 1915 account is precise about the mechanism:

> "The initiation of revolutionary activities would obviously have led to the dissolution of
> these legal organisations by the police, and the old party — from Legien to Kautsky
> inclusively — sacrificed the revolutionary aims of the proletariat for the sake of preserving
> the present legal organisations. … The proletariat's right to revolution was sold for a mess
> of pottage — organisations permitted by the present police law." (*Collapse of the Second
> International*, ch. VIII)

And the counterfactual, which is the rule Babylon needs:

> "Had the Social-Democratic group in the Reichstag done its duty on August 4, the external form
> of organisation would probably have been destroyed, but the spirit would have remained, the
> spirit that animated the Party under the Anti-Socialist Law and helped it to overcome all
> difficulties." (ibid., quoting the Niederbarnim document approvingly)

Two formulations of one law:

- **The threshold is crossed when the organisation can spend itself.** Capacity (1–3, 5) plus willingness (4) plus the practice of spending (6).
- **An organisation whose external form has become its reason to exist has already failed, whatever its size.** Its symptom is that it will not risk the shell.

The SPD's shell was not a fraud. It was built by decades of real work and delivered real goods to real workers — which is exactly why it captured the party. **The capture mechanism is success, not hypocrisy**, and Babylon must therefore render it as a slow accumulation of interests, not a corruption event.

## 4. Reconciling with the MIM(P) / MLM-TW form ceiling

Briefing 06: "No amount of correct line and no amount of crisis converts a First World organization into the vanguard of a revolution whose main force is elsewhere. The org wins by subtraction (removing props), not by addition (accumulating a majority)."

That is right, and it is not a denial of the threshold — it is a denial of a different claim. The word "vanguard" is doing two jobs in the literature and the game must not merge them.

### 4.1 T1 and T2

**T1 — Party-form.** The organisation has the six features: it can set a line, enforce a boundary, dissolve a body, survive a raid, and spend itself. *Available anywhere*, including the imperialist core. A fact about the organisation.

**T2 — Vanguard-of-the-revolution.** The organisation is the recognised leadership of the class force that will actually make the revolution, in the place where that force is. *Not available in the core*, because the main force is in the periphery and semi-colonies. A fact about the terrain.

Lenin's party crossed both, in that order, because Russia in 1912 was a terrain where both were possible. A core-country organisation can cross T1 and never cross T2. That is not a failure of line; it is a failure of geography, and geography is fixed.

### 4.2 Why MIM(P) refuses T1, and why that refusal is legitimate

Briefing 06 quotes it: "We uphold the need for a vanguard party to seize power and build socialism, but **do not fill that role ourselves.**" The reason given is not that T1 is impossible in the core but that T1's *cost* there is defeat: a centralised party competing for state power in the imperialist core invites the repression that a decentralised cell network survives. The Panthers, in MIM(P)'s own reading, "inspired many lumpen with their audacity" and paid for it.

So the core run has a strategic fork the corpus supports — neither branch correct:

| Fork | What you build | What you get | What kills you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Constitute** (T1) | a party: centre, programme, rules, organ, cadre | capability, a boundary, the ability to spend itself; a pole for a later crisis | decapitation; or the SPD path if the shell becomes the interest |
| **Institutionalise** (MIM(P)) | cells, a ministry, a press, a school, a legal apparatus | institutions that outlive their founders; subtraction capacity | irrelevance; the institution drifting into an NGO |

### 4.3 What the core run can win

If T2 is unavailable in Michigan, the 260 periods need a different terminal condition there or the American run is a guaranteed loss and briefing 06's ceiling becomes a disappointment rather than a design. The corpus supplies the alternative: **T1 is itself the win, because it is what the later crisis will need.**

Lenin's 1912 form is what met 1917. Had the form not set by 1912, February 1917 would have found circles. The party did not make the revolution by being present at it; it made the revolution possible by having become a party five years earlier, in a period when nothing was happening. Transposed to the core:

> **The 260 periods are not a countdown to insurrection. They are the window in which an
> organisation's form sets.** After it, the form is a fact of the world: either an instrument
> that can wait, or a permanent something-else.

## 5. The clock

### 5.1 Why twenty years is the right constant

The corpus's own cases cluster, which is why 260 periods is defensible rather than arbitrary:

| Formation | Founded | Terminal test | Years |
|---|---|---|---|
| SPD (Erfurt) | 1891 | August 4, 1914 | 23 — capitulated |
| Second International | 1889 | August 4, 1914 | 25 — collapsed |
| Bolsheviks, as a tendency | 1903 | October 1917 | 14 — took power |
| Bolsheviks, as an independent party | 1912 | October 1917 | 5 — took power |

Spread 5–25, centred near 20. The mechanism is generational and is in the text: *WITBD*'s conclusion complains that the leaders of 1894–98 "had not reached 'the age of thirty-five'" and "left the scene with astonishing rapidity." Twenty years is roughly the interval in which a founding cadre either becomes a leadership with a state or a staff with a pension. **The clock is the career arc of the founding generation.**

### 5.2 The five gates

Not a smooth bar: five gates, three of which the player does not schedule.

1. **Constitution (periods 1–26).** The player's decision: call the congress, or keep fusing circles. Precondition: the four assets of §2.2 plus a paper that is a supply chain, not a masthead. Called without them, you get Minsk 1898 — mass legitimacy, nothing else, and a name now defended by an apparatus that does not exist. **Failure: the paper party.**
2. **Differentiation (≈27–78).** The boundary war. Somebody to your left and somebody to your right both call themselves the party, and the question is whether the boundary holds when it costs members. Content: the 1908 expulsion of the Lefts over participation in a reactionary parliament (*LWC* ch. IV); the 1912 expulsion of the Liquidators. **Failure: the August bloc** — a unity front that grows until it must take a position, then breaks up and leaves you the wreckage and the bourgeoisie the workers.
3. **Reaction (≈79–156).** The illegal/legal combination under a state that is winning. The *Reorganisation* pamphlet is the gate: "The secret apparatus of the Party must be maintained. But at the same time it is absolutely necessary to make the widest possible use of the present relatively wider scope for our activity … to create many new legal and semi-legal Party organisations." With the trap in the same text: the party "has stagnated while working underground … it has been suffocating underground." **Failure: liquidation** — dissolving into legal work because legal work is where the members are.
4. **The turn (≈157–234).** An external catastrophe arrives and demands a position that is unpopular, costly and correct. The player does not schedule this gate; they only decide whether to take the position. This is the real Bolshevik/SPD discriminator, because both had the form and only one would spend it. **Failure: capitulation** — the shell survives, the spirit does not, and the organisation becomes an appendage of the class enemy while still calling itself socialist.
5. **Terminal (≈235–260).** The form meets the state. Where T2 is available this is dual power and insurrection, decided by whether Gates 1–4 produced an instrument. In the core the gate *stops* the clock rather than resolving it: the form sets, and the run ends with a verdict about what now exists.

### 5.3 Failure taxonomy

| Failure | Instance | Symptom in the interface |
|---|---|---|
| **Paper party** | Minsk 1898 | Topology shows nodes with no edges while the Ticker reports a founding |
| **Liquidation** | the Liquidators, 1908–12 | the illegal apparatus has a headcount of zero and nobody notices |
| **Capture** | SPD, 1914 | the org will not take an action that risks its legal shells, and has a reason each time |
| **Sectarian purity** | the Lefts of 1908, had they won | correct line, shrinking graph, expulsions that cut headcount without raising capability |

Capture and purity are the same error in opposite directions, and both are sincere. Neither is a corruption event; both are the natural result of a real asset.

### 5.4 The 1915 mechanism: institutions acquire a survival interest

The most transplantable mechanic in the corpus, and it should be explicit rather than emergent.

Every institution the player builds — paper, union local, legal aid office, school, book service, parliamentary group — produces two outputs: the good it was built for, and **a constituency whose income, status and identity depend on its continuation.** The second is invisible until Gate 4, where it becomes the decisive political fact. The German party did not betray the proletariat because its leaders were bought; it betrayed because its organisations were real, its members' livelihoods were inside them, and the Niederbarnim document states the trade-off exactly: the external form would have been destroyed, "but the spirit would have remained."

So: **give every institution a `survival_interest` that rises with size and age, and make Gate 4 a subtraction from it.** An organisation with low `survival_interest` can spend itself and keep the spirit; one with high `survival_interest` cannot, and will generate a plausible justification for not doing so — exactly what Legien said out loud: "This is an obviously anarchist idea: to wreck the organisation in order to bring about a solution of the problem by the masses."

This also complements briefing 06's independent-institutions rule. MIM(P) requires that an institution "keep producing when its founders are arrested" and that its product be a *good* rather than a claim. Add the mirror: **an institution that cannot be given up has become a claim on the party.** Both tests are needed; the first alone produces the SPD.

## 6. Nine computable conditions

Conditions 1–4 are constitution (Gate 1), 5–7 recognition (Gates 2–3), 8–9 roots (Gate 3). All nine must hold; each historical failure misses a different subset.

**Constitution** — (1) **a centre that can dissolve**, not elected but able: resolve a dissolution of a healthy subordinate body and have it stick (OSF D). (2) **a programme**, written, adopted, cited in internal argument. (3) **tactical resolutions with a history**, not a platform: a record of positions taken and defended across at least two situations. (4) **a membership clause with teeth**: personal participation in a named organisation, not a relation of assistance (Rule 1, 1903).

**Recognition** — (5) **adoption of your vocabulary by rivals**, measured as the share of other contingents' internal argument conducted in your terms (*WITBD* ch. III). (6) **reallocation of state repression toward you**; the enemy's budget is the scoreboard (Zubatov). (7) **an enforced boundary**: at least one named expulsion that raised capability rather than only cutting headcount (1908, 1912).

**Roots** — (8) **dual apparatus**: an illegal core plus legal and semi-legal shells, with the core able to survive the loss of the shells (*Reorganisation*; *Collapse of the Second International*). (9) **an all-country organ that is a supply chain**: print, smuggle, reprint, distribute — "*Only such a newspaper could link up the disunited Marxist organizations and prepare the way for the creation of a real party*" (*History* ch. I §5 on *Iskra*).

All nine present and the declaration is ratified: the clock starts at Gate 1 and the org enters the world as a party. Any one absent and the declaration still fires, producing the *paper party* state — not a soft failure but a specific, named, historically attested outcome with its own downstream consequences.

## 7. Anti-mechanics

1. **No grind-to-vanguard.** Briefing 06's ceiling forbids it, and the corpus forbids it for a second reason: each of the nine conditions is a decision plus a consequence, not an accumulated resource. Ten thousand members without dissolution power is Minsk 1898.
2. **No `declare_vanguard()`.** *WITBD* ch. III is unambiguous. Recognition is an observation.
3. **No self-reported recognition stat.** The player must infer recognition from rival behaviour, police allocation and fusion events. An interface that prints "vanguard: 73%" kills the mechanic, and removes the thing that makes it interesting: the player being surprised by their own status.
4. **No size-as-score.** Briefing 06 §8: "Size of an organization says nothing about its eventual historical impact." Membership is a composition.
5. **No purity stat.** §2.2 is an argument for *opening* membership. The failure on this axis is sectarian purity, and a "line clarity" meter that only rises produces it every time.
6. **No Trotskyist shortcut.** Unity fronts, "the party of the whole class," and permanent improvisation are the August bloc: they grow, they look like the answer, they break up at the moment of decision. The 1912 evidence is Lenin's, not a polemical overlay.
7. **Do not let the congress be the only verb.** Briefing 04 catalogues six distinct congress powers; collapsing them into "hold congress" makes 1898 and 1912 indistinguishable, and they are the two poles of the design.

## 8. Open questions for the porch

1. **Does the core run get Gate 4?** The ceiling implies the American organisation meets a war it cannot turn, because the war's main force is elsewhere. If Gate 4 arrives at all in the core, it may arrive as a *test of subtraction* — oppose the war and lose the legal shells — rather than as a road to power. That may be the single most interesting decision in the game.
2. **What is the player's relationship to T2?** If Michigan cannot cross T2, is the win "produce an instrument that outlives the run," or "recognise the ceiling and choose institution-building over party-building"? Both are corpus-supported. They are not the same game.
3. **Does `survival_interest` apply to the party itself?** At Gate 5 the party is also an institution with a constituency. The corpus's answer — the purge — resets it. Whether the player may purge the party's own apparatus is the difference between a run that can still spend itself at period 250 and one that cannot.
4. **Where does the 1903 Rule 1 fork live in the interface?** It must be legible as a choice with a cost at the time, or it becomes a hidden build-order discovered forty periods later — which reads as unfair rather than as history.

## 9. One paragraph for the designer

A party is not declared into existence; it is declared *and then* recognised, and only the first half is the player's. Minsk 1898 proves the declaration alone is a rumour. London 1903 proves a boundary drawn by an organisation that already owns a paper and a cadre creates a party-in-formation. Prague 1912 proves the final constituting act is a subtraction — expel the Liquidators, rebuild the illegal apparatus, and let the unity front break up on its own. §6 is the checkable form of that. Stalin's six features describe what it feels like from inside once it has happened, and feature 4 — the party as an *instrument* of power rather than a thing in itself — separates the Bolshevik from the SPD, which had the other five in greater quantity and sold the revolution for the legal organisations the police permitted it. The 260 periods are therefore not a countdown to insurrection. They are the window in which an organisation's form sets: after about twenty years an organisation is either an instrument that can wait, or permanently something else, and the crisis, whenever it comes, will find what is there.
