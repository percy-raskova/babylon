# 16 — Protracted People's War and Dual Power: from organizing to armed self-defense

Read-only briefing, 2026-10-25. Question: **how does an organization that cannot shoot — and whose own
corpus tells it not to shoot yet — convert organizing, independent institutions and base areas into dual
power, and then into the three stages of protracted people's war?**

`mao/works/1937/guerrilla-warfare/ch01.htm`; `mao/selected-works/volume-2/{mswv2_08,mswv2_20}.htm`;

pp. 6–16, 20–60); `mim-3rd-draft.pdf` (OCR; party-building critique only). Repo: `ai/epochs/epoch1/

## 0. Thesis

**Babylon already models the three stages as a reversible posture. What it does not model is the thing that
makes a stage change meaningful: a second administration growing inside the first one.**

Mao's three magic weapons are not three tech trees. They are a **dependency triangle with one load-bearing
vertex** — Party building — not because the party is morally prior, but because *it is the only one of the three
that can hold the other two together when they contradict each other*. Giáp supplies the sequence epoch1
compresses: political base → secret armed base → guerrilla war → partial insurrection → general insurrection →
liberated area. MIM(Prisons) supplies the only honest translation into the imperialist core, and it is
deflationary: **public opinion, independent institutions, divisions among the exploiters**, plus an explicit
refusal of armed struggle *at this time* in the United States. That refusal is a **condition statement**, and it
is the gate the game must implement rather than skip.

> The player does not "unlock armed struggle." The player builds the conditions under which armed self-defense
> stops being a flash of fury, and then the state's own loss of monopoly decides whether there is a civil war.

# Part I — The three magic weapons are a triangle

## 1.1 The load-bearing sentences

*Introducing The Communist* (4 October 1939) names the weapons and states their grammar:

> "the united front, armed struggle and Party building are the Chinese Communist Party's three 'magic weapons'."
> … "The united front is a united front for carrying on armed struggle. And the Party is the heroic warrior
> wielding the two weapons, the united front and the armed struggle, to storm and shatter the enemy's positions.
> That is how the three are related to each other."

Three mechanics follow. **The front exists *for* the armed struggle** — a front whose purpose is legislative
survival is not a weak version of Mao's front, it is a different object. **The party is a wielder, not a bonus**
— if it is absent or degenerated the other two stop being weapons and become, respectively, an electoral
coalition and an armed gang. **Each can be correct while the pair is wrong** — Chen Tu-hsiu's Right opportunism
(front without independence), Li Li-san's "Left" opportunism (force without base), Chang Kuo-tao's Right
opportunism (base without centre). Three named errors, three different symptoms.

## 1.2 Party building: line over organization

> "the more bolshevized the Party, the more correctly can it decide upon its political line and handle the
> questions of the united front and armed struggle."

The inverse is what the game must price: *the less consolidated the party, the more the front and the armed force
each drift toward their own class content.* `mim-3rd-draft.pdf` supplies the degenerate form — cohesion by
personality cult, "heroes are THE ANSWER" — which reads on-screen as high discipline and high unity while being
the exact opposite of bolshevization. **Cult cohesion is a substitute good:** it raises coordination now and
discounts the cost of every future line error, because a cult cannot lose an argument.

## 1.3 United front: composition, not coalition

Mao's six laws of the front are a composition table; the sharpest is law (4): the ally "stubbornly opposes any
ideological, political and organizational development of the proletariat and the proletarian party … moreover,
it does all this to prepare for capitulating to the enemy and splitting the united front." That is a **named
adversary action inside the front**, not a risk modifier: the front is a node whose internal contradiction has an
agenda and a funder.

MIM(Prisons) gives the core version and its test, in the United Front for Peace in Prisons:

> "The basis of any real unity comes from an agreement on certain key ideas. This statement does not grant
> authority to any party over any other party. We are mutually accountable to each other… Members don't have to
> agree with MIM(Prisons)'s cardinal points … but they can't consciously disagree with any of them."

**Design reading.** Unity is measured by *what the front will not knowingly contradict*, not by membership count
or a shared demand sheet. Cheap, checkable, non-sigmoid — and it produces the right failure: a formation that can
"live with" the line has just imported the capitulation it was meant to prevent.

## 1.4 Armed struggle: last, and never independent

> "These guerrilla operations must not be considered as an independent form of warfare. They are but one step in
> the total war, one aspect of the revolutionary struggle." … "they, lacking the quality of independence, are of
> themselves incapable of providing a solution to the struggle."
>
> "Because guerrilla warfare basically derives from the masses and is supported by them, it can neither exist nor
> flourish if it separates itself from their sympathies and co-operation."

Mao states the political preconditions of any armed phase as an ordered list — and the order is the mechanic:

> "1. Arousing and organizing the people. 2. Achieving internal unification politically. 3. Establishing bases.
> 4. Equipping forces. 5. Recovering national strength. 6. Destroying enemy's national strength. 7. Regaining
> lost territories."

**Design reading: this is a set of preconditions that can be run in reverse.** A player who arms at step 4 with
1–3 incomplete does not get a weaker army; they get a *different social object* — an armed group whose
reproduction depends on the armed group. That is the foco.

## 1.5 Three decaying capacities plus one wielder

| Weapon | On Topology | What decays it | Cannot do alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Party building** | A centre with authority to dissolve subordinate bodies; a line that survives its authors | Succession (cult), cadre absorbed into office, loss of the paper chain | Fight — it has no mass weight |
| **United front** | Composition of nodes bound by "cannot consciously disagree" | Ally's capitulation agenda; dilution to a demand sheet; repression aimed at the moderate wing | Decide — it cannot choose the moment |
| **Armed struggle** | Coercive capacity, initially self-defense only | Any operation without a base; casualties without replacement; criminal drift | Reproduce — it eats its own base |
| **The wielder** | The party's *line* — which of the three is currently the main danger | All of the above at once | Exist without the other three |

**Rule:** no screen shows a "magic weapons" score. The player sees a front that stopped growing, a cadre who took
a county job, a defense committee that now owns a building. The triangle is engine-private bookkeeping; the
visibility rule already forbids rendering it, and the corpus already supplies the visible symptoms.

# Part II — The three stages are a posture, and the posture is reversible

## 2.1 Six specific problems, one of which is the base area

*Problems of Strategy in Guerrilla War Against Japan* (May 1938) lists the whole programme:

> "(1) the use of initiative, flexibility and planning in conducting offensives within the defensive, battles of
> quick decision within protracted war, and exterior-line operations within interior-line operations;
> (2) co-ordination with regular warfare; (3) establishment of base areas; (4) the strategic defensive and the
> strategic offensive; (5) the development of guerrilla warfare into mobile warfare; and (6) correct relationship
> of command."

Item (1) is the important shape: **offense inside defense, quick decision inside protractedness, exterior lines
inside interior lines.** The strategic defensive is not passivity; it is a container for local offensives.
Epoch1 has the container ("Guerrilla warfare, supplemented by mobile warfare") but not the interior structure —
which is what makes a defensive period playable rather than a waiting screen. Under all six sits the base
principle: "to strive to the utmost to preserve one's own strength and destroy that of the enemy," where
"partial and temporary 'non-preservation' (sacrifice, or paying the price) is necessary for the sake of general
and permanent preservation."

## 2.2 Stage is derived, never stored

`territorial-schema.yaml` states the doctrine correctly and at length: "DEFENSIVE <------> EQUILIBRIUM <------>
OFFENSIVE" … "Conditions determine phase, not time" … "You are never 'safe' in a higher stage. The enemy can push
you back," with the Long March, Vietnam's thirty years and Moncada as proofs.

**This is the most important design line in the briefing:** the stage must be a *derived label*, not a stored
variable. The engine holds four quantities and renders one of three words. If the player can see the variable
they will farm it; if it is stored, a designer will eventually write `stage += 1`.

| Engine-private quantity | What the participant sees instead |
|---|---|
| Enemy offensive capacity relative to commitments | Raids that stop coming; a QRF that arrives late; a county that stops asking for help |
| Base areas that survive a repression cycle | A clinic still open the month after three founders were arrested |
| Mass base outside the base area | A jury pool, a school board, a church that will not inform |
| Armed force's replacement rate | A defense committee the same size as last year |

## 2.3 Giáp's ladder — the sequence Babylon is missing

> "wherever the masses are, political bases and revolutionary organizations must be set up. From those political
> bases … to conduct armed propaganda and pay more attention to political action than to military activities –
> our Party strove to organize secret armed bases, and boost political action in co-ordination with ever more
> perfected armed struggle. Then it launched guerrilla warfare and partial armed insurrections, set up the Viet
> Bac liberated area and guerilla bases in other regions, while powerfully expanding political bases in the whole
> country … In that way, our people finally launched a general insurrection, won power all over the country…"

| Rung | Giáp's term | Babylon object | Must already exist |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | political base | committee, front, institution producing a good | a line and a cadre |
| 1 | secret armed base | unlisted node with a *non-military* function | a political base worth defending |
| 2 | guerrilla war / partial insurrection | local coercion used *inside* an existing base | the base's population, not the fighters |
| 3 | liberated area | administration replaced, durably | contiguity + a state that cannot re-enter cheaply |
| 4 | general insurrection | simultaneous, all-country | the state's interior-line problem becomes unsolvable |
| 5 | people's state | downstream (epoch 3) | — |

Two structural claims are also mechanics. **Three categories of troops** — regulars, regionals, militia — exist
because "the people's militia must be ubiquitous and strong, the regional and regular troops must be elite ones
and in sufficient numbers": a *distribution* rule, not a quantity. The militia makes a base area expensive to
enter; the regulars make a campaign decisive. And **political forces are prior to armed forces**: "If it were not
for the revolutionary people, their immense political forces … there could never be powerful popular armed
forces."

**Reversibility, from Giáp's own career:** the Nghe-Tinh soviets (1930–31) are his starting point — worker-peasant
power in a number of rural areas, and the first self-defense squads — and they did not survive French repression.
The ladder was climbed again from a *legal* democratic campaign (1936–39) with no soviets at all. A movement can
lose its base areas and be the same movement two years later.

## 2.4 The ten principles belong to a people's army — the state cannot buy them

The ten principles from *The Present Situation and Our Tasks* (25 December 1947) are in `red-book/ch08.htm` and
end with the sentence that decides implementation:

> "**our strategy and tactics are based on a people's war; no army opposed to the people can use our strategy and
> tactics.**"

The principles — attack dispersed and isolated first; small and medium cities and rural areas before big cities;
wiping out effective strength rather than holding places; concentrate two to six times the enemy's strength; no
unprepared battle; replenish from the enemy; rest and consolidate between campaigns — are therefore not a combat
model to bolt onto any actor. They are the *output* of a political relation. A state that tries to use them gets
the correlated bad practices for free, which is the argument `research/08` already makes from *Victory Has a
Thousand Fathers*.

**Design reading.** The ten principles belong to the parked tactical study, with one exception that belongs here:
**principle (3), "make wiping out the enemy's effective strength our main objective; do not make holding or
seizing a city or place our main objective."** That is strategic, and it is the antidote to the player's most
natural instinct — treating the Physical Map as paint-by-hex conquest. Territory must be *costly to hold and
cheap to deny*, so that the player who paints the map loses the war.

And the base area's actual defense: "when we talk of the people in the base area as a factor, we mean that we
have an armed people. That is the main reason why the enemy is afraid to approach our base area." Not walls, not
a militia roster: **an armed population**, measured by how many people show up when the raid comes.

## 2.5 Regression is normal, and each direction costs something different

| Regression | Cause signature | Lost | Kept |
|---|---|---|---|
| Offensive → Equilibrium | premature offensive; enemy rallies; external support to enemy | cadre, arms, the belief that the offensive was ready | base areas, line, front |
| Equilibrium → Defensive | base area crushed; leadership captured; intelligence breach | contiguous territory, safe houses, the militia | line (if written down), mass base |
| Defensive → destruction | base areas overrun + leadership killed + mass defection | everything | the record, on the Wiki |

The Long March is the schema's own example and the right one: a retreat that *preserved the line* and therefore
permitted a later ascent. **A regression that keeps the line is a strategic retreat; one that loses the line is a
defeat regardless of how many people survive.**

# Part III — What a base area is inside the United States

## 3.1 MIM's institution, and the test that separates it from a mailing list

The standing task, repeated rather than sloganised:

> "…to build public opinion and **the independent institutions of the oppressed to seize power**."

Behind it, program point 5: "the oppressed need independent institutions to provide land, bread, housing,
education, medical care, clothing, justice and peace." And the operational test, which `research/06` extracted
and this briefing adopts verbatim:

> **An institution keeps producing when its founders are arrested, and its product is a *good* — a book, a
> lawyer, a bed, a name — rather than a claim. That is the difference between dual power and a mailing list.**

The United Front for Peace in Prisons adds the second test, *independence* as a spatial fact: "We build our own
institutions and programs independent of the United $tates government and all its branches, **right down to the
local police**." A clinic that accepts police referral is not dual power; it is a subcontract.

## 3.2 The Weatherman proof: focoism is a commitment error before a military one

`weatherman.crit` orders its three points by importance.

**(i) The correct target.** Weatherman's strength was "the primacy of confronting national chauvinism and
racism" — organizing whites around their *own* perceived oppression "is bound to lead in a racist and chauvinist
direction." MIM's line on the Black nation is compatible: "The struggle of black people—as a colony—is for
self-determination, freedom, and liberation from U.S. imperialism."

**(ii) Focoism, named and diagnosed.**

> "Focoism holds that small cells of armed revolutionaries can create the conditions of revolution through their
> actions. Weatherman's sensational tactics—bombing police stations and banks, starting riots—ignored the
> principles of attacking with strategic confidence and building mass support for violent action. The gamble that
> the masses would be sparked to revolt by focoists' heroic endeavors never paid off. If the focoists survive the
> battles, they burn out and sell out. The focoist 'revolution' is never more than a flash of fury."

**(iii) Why individual commitment cannot substitute.** "Without taking time to build mass support for Maoism, the
commitment of the individual cannot overcome the commitment of the bourgeoisie… Carrying the revolution forward
on the shoulders of individuals is impossible."

**Design reading.** Focoism is not "armed struggle with too few people." It is **armed struggle used as a
substitute for the base**, and its signature is that every cost is paid in a currency the organization has
finitely much of: cadre, audacity, legitimacy. Implement it as a doctrine converting Cadre Labor into Heat at a
terrible rate, plus a **legitimacy transfer to the state** — the flash is bright enough that the Corporate Feed
gets a free story.

## 3.3 The Marighella contrast: discipline does not fix the missing base

The *Minimanual* is the same error with better tradecraft. Marighella is careful about the criminal distinction —
"The criminal benefits personally from his actions … The urban guerrilla follows a political goal" — and precise
about the aim: "to distract, to wear down, to demoralize the military regime and its repressive forces." But the
decisive sentence is an epistemology:

> "It is better to err acting than to do nothing for fear of making a mistake."

That is focoism stated as a virtue. It inverts "fight no battle unprepared, fight no battle you are not sure of
winning," and it inverts the base principle, because action-without-base has a negative replacement rate by
construction. **The urban-guerrilla doctrine should be available and should look competent** — small, real,
deniable wins — while its long-run curve subtracts from Cadre Labor and from the front's willingness to be seen
with it.

## 3.4 What the corpus actually says about armed struggle in the core now

The sentence the game must not soften, because it is the corpus's own condition statement:

> "MIM(Prisons) and its publications **explicitly oppose the use of armed struggle at this time in the
> imperialist countries (including the United $tates)**. … We encourage prisoners to join these battles while
> explicitly discouraging them from engaging in any violence or illegal acts."

Against the triangle this is neither moral nor permanent. It is the claim that **steps 1–3 of Mao's list are
incomplete**, so an armed phase entered now would be a foco phase. Encode it as a *gate*, not a prohibition: the
doctrine is selectable, the conditions are checkable, and choosing it early has the historical consequence.

## 3.5 Four candidate base-area forms in the core

| Form | Why it is a base | Structural weakness | Referent |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prison** | The state's administration *is* the terrain; a united population replaces it (peace, unity, growth, internationalism, independence). Product is a good. | No exterior line, no retreat. Crushable by one QRF operation; the loss is total. Highest value, highest variance. | MIM(P) USW / UFPP; Attica; BPP prison work |
| **Contiguous internal semi-colony** | National oppression is territorial (Black Belt/New Afrika, Aztlán, First Nations). The population is not recruited *into* the base — it *is* the base. | The cheapest state response is concession plus police, not invasion; reform is available, so absorption is the real danger. | Haywood; BPP ten points; `research/06` §1 |
| **Municipal / industrial zone** | When Φ cracks and Θ saturates, a city can stop delivering services and a union can stop delivering freight. Both are goods. | Depends on the ballot and the bond market; capital strike and preemption are the first two moves. | Seattle 1919; sit-down era; porch-02 Non-Cooperation |
| **Indigenous territory** | Sovereignty is pre-existing and legal, not seized; the claim to independence is older than the state's claim to it. | The player is not the protagonist and cannot lead it; wrong posture is a named trap (RED_OGV). | Standing Rock; treaty rights |

These are not four flavours of one building. They differ in **what the state must spend to end them**: prison →
one assault and it is over; semi-colony → concession plus occupation, and it can last decades; municipality →
money plus injunction, reversible at the next budget; Indigenous territory → cannot be ended, only violated, which
is why it is the only permanent political fact rather than a position. That difference is the base area's real
stat, and it shows up as **how the state chooses to respond.**

## 3.6 Dual power before civil war: the QRF economics

No "declare dual power" button, for the same reason `research/12` finds no `declare_vanguard()` verb. Dual power
is a **condition on the state's cost function**:

> **A zone is dual power when ordinary police cannot enter it without a mobile reserve operation, and when the
> goods the zone needs are produced inside it.**

**(a) The interior-line problem.** Mao explains why a stronger enemy loses the initiative by *troop shortage*: the
enemy "is numerically small" while occupying, so it must choose where to be. In the core, police are numerous but
**non-tactical** — presence, revenue and surveillance, not maneuver. What is scarce is the **mobile reserve**: the
tactical team, the joint task force, the Guard call-up, the federal deployment needing a legal predicate and a
political price. Dual power is therefore not "we have more guns here"; it is **"the cheapest way in is a QRF
operation, and a QRF costs the state something it does not want to spend this month."**

**(b) The goods clause.** A zone that needs the state's clinic, court and school is not dual power no matter who
patrols it. Hence MIM's unglamorous list: land, bread, housing, education, medical care, clothing, **justice**,
peace. Justice is on it — which is why independent courts and jury pools matter as much as defense committees, and
why the BPP's ninth point (peer juries) and tenth (UN-supervised plebiscite in the Black colony) are *dual-power*
demands. They ask the state to stop being the administration.

**(c) The crossover.** Civil war is not declared; it is the period in which the QRF calculus has failed in more
than one region at once and the zones begin to coordinate. That is epoch1's `stage_3` condition and epoch3's
balkanization entry point simultaneously, with `warlord-trajectory.yaml` downstream. The player does not choose
civil war; the player's base areas make the state's arithmetic fail.

**(d) The state is not the player's instrument.** State agencies are non-player actors advancing on declared local
rules over the same graph — the constitution requires that "player and non-player actors act through the same
declared causal machinery." The player manipulates their **inputs** (Heat, Θ allocation, legitimacy, the cost of a
legal predicate), never their policy. A base area is a change to the *environment the non-player agencies compute
in*, not a captured unit.

# Part IV — The six screens

Each states the decision question the screen answers. No display shows a stage number.

## 4.1 Topology — "where is there a second administration?"

Dual power renders as **two graphs over the same nodes**: the state's (jurisdiction, funding, referral) and the
movement's (institution, product, obligation). The gap is what the player is building.

- **Node property:** *produces a good* vs. *makes a claim.* A tenants' assembly that negotiates is a claim; one
  that runs the boiler is a good.
- **Survival test, visible in play:** after a repression event, which nodes still produce? A countable world-event,
  so the visibility rule permits it.
- **Base area** = contiguous nodes where the movement's graph is denser than the state's *and* the goods clause
  holds. Contiguity is a Physical Map fact, density a Topology fact; both must hold.
- **The state's counter is not a raid, it is a referral.** Absorption appears as an edge from a movement node into
  a state agency — the clinic that takes Medicaid, the defense committee that becomes a legal-aid contractor, the
  cadre who takes the county job. Drawn, not narrated.

## 4.2 Physical Map — "how much does it cost the state to come in here?"

- Base areas are **H3/county polygons with a response-cost attribute**, not ownership colours. A zone can be
  *denied* without being *held*.
- Terrain enters as Mao says: "conditions of terrain, climate, and society in general offer obstacles to his
  progress." Prison (no exterior line), urban grid (many approaches, high surveillance), mountain/rural (low
  surveillance, low density), reservation (legal sovereignty as terrain).
- **The QRF ring** is the one legible state artifact here: a distance/response overlay the player can read and
  cannot control. Watching it thin is the visible sign the strategic posture is changing.
- Principle (3) lives here as a rule: ground the state can retake cheaply is a negative. The map should make the
  player feel that.

## 4.3 Electoral — "is the seat a weapon, a shield, or a hook?"

The screen already has the right structure (Clout vs. Votes; six office verbs; refusal at incidence). Dual power
adds the third answer.

- **Weapon:** Non-Cooperation (sanctuary, carceral freeze) and Budget can *defund the state's entry* into a zone —
  a QRF-cost intervention by other means.
- **Shield:** the prosecutor who will not charge, the sheriff who will not lend tactical support.
- **Hook:** the same office converts a cadre into an office-holder whose reproduction now depends on the state.
  This is the Hilliard problem in institutional form: in `hilliardclass.html` the Panther legacy is retold as "own
  apartment buildings," "we want to own some," voting, with the ten-point program called "reformist" and "nothing
  revolutionary." **That is absorption seen from inside**, the best-documented failure of the closest American
  analogue to a base area.
- **Refusal at incidence:** the seat is never a trap at the door. The player wins it, uses it, and discovers the
  five material barriers are the actual terrain. Dual power is the answer this screen cannot give, which is why
  the two screens must be read together.

## 4.4 Ticker — "what does reproduction cost here now?"

- Dual power is **local price and time relief without Φ moving.** The clinic, the food institution, the defense
  committee change the household's week: $E = (T_{\text{wage}} + T_{\text{repro}} + T_{\text{commute}})/168$.
- The tell that a base area is real: **the Corporate Feed stops printing a price for something.** A good that has
  left the market is a political fact, and the three-author narrator knows how to render it.
- The tell that it is fake: prices unchanged, only rhetoric moved. A reformist institution the player should be
  able to lose a year to.
- **Φ's floor is still the periphery** (`research/06` §9). A base area does not raise American wages; it changes
  who allocates the local surplus and what the household must buy. It is a political strategy that buys time, not
  an economic strategy against imperialism.

## 4.5 Wiki — "what is this period's task, in a verb?"

- **Chapters titled by the dominant task** (porch-02 §4.5), with the Mao-treatise test governing what appears: a
  base area's founding congress — yes; its cohesion modifier — no.
- **The base area's own constitution is a document.** Independent courts, jury pools, defense committees and
  clinics produce paper: rules, verdicts, intake forms, expelled members. That paper is the Wiki's best material
  and the strongest argument the institution is real, because a mailing list has no minutes.
- **Vigilance case files** for both traps: focoism (operations multiplying faster than institutions; Heat rising
  faster than base; cadre losses without replacement) and absorption (cadre in office; institution on state
  funding; the line quietly amended to fit a funder).
- **Backdated discovery.** `porch-01`'s `observed_period` vs `acquired_period` means a base area crushed in period
  40 can be *discovered* in period 52 from a document the player only then obtains. That is how a regression is
  felt rather than announced.

## 4.6 Line / Doctrine — "which of the three weapons is the main danger now?"

A branching map of *errors*, not a tech tree.

- **Three strands, three decay modes.** Party building (cult / liquidation), united front (capitulation /
  closed-doorism), armed struggle (focoism / militarism). Mao names both poles of each: "Left" closed-doorism and
  Right opportunism on the front; Li Li-san and Chang Kuo-tao on force and base.
- **The focoism node is selectable and looks brave** — reachable by a player who has read *Minimanual* and not
  *On Guerrilla Warfare*. Its consequence is the corpus's own: "never more than a flash of fury," plus a
  legitimacy transfer to the state.
- **The armed-self-defense threshold is a Congress ceremony, not an unlock.** It requires a document stating what
  the force is *for* and what it will not do — Giáp's "armed propaganda … more attention to political action than
  to military activities," and exactly what MIM(P)'s refusal protects. A force without that document is a gang,
  and the engine should treat it as one.
- **No Trotsky, no putsch.** The urban-insurrectionist line is a doctrine node whose historical result the engine
  computes: it wins a week and loses the front.

# Part V — Failure traps, with symptoms

| Trap | Corpus name | Symptom on screen | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Focoism** | "a flash of fury" (`weatherman.crit`) | operations ↑, institutions flat, Heat ↑↑, cadre ↓, state legitimacy ↑ | Stand down; rebuild base; the line may not survive |

# Part VII — What is deliberately parked

Low-level tactical resolution — the hex/battalion/campaign layer, force ratios, supply, attrition, the ten
principles as combat modifiers — is a separate study combining Stellaris, HOI4, Victoria 3 and Risk. This
briefing supplies only the inputs that study must not contradict:

1. **Terrain is a social fact before it is a modifier.** "An armed people" is the base area's defense; the terrain
   modifier renders that, it does not replace it.
2. **Effective strength, not ground, is the objective** (principle 3). Any tactical model rewarding held hexes has
   broken the strategic layer above it.
3. **The ten principles are not purchasable by the state.** If a non-player state actor can select them, the model
   has misread `red-book/ch08.htm`'s closing sentence.
4. **Loss must be payable in cadre and legitimacy, not only units.** Otherwise focoism is cheap — the one thing
   the corpus forbids.
