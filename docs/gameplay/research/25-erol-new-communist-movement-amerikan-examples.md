# 25 — EROL: contemporary Amerikan examples

Read-only briefing, 2026-10-25. Question: **what do the U.S. anti-revisionist organizations of
1969–1990 actually teach Babylon's decision layer — not as history to illustrate, but as failure
modes the engine can price?**

**Source.** Local MIA mirror, cited below as `E/` =
`/media/user/data/old-hdd/old-hdd/www.marxists.org/history/erol/`. Folders read:
`1956-1960/`, `1960-1970/`, `ncm-1/`, `ncm-1a/`, `ncm-2/`, `ncm-3/`, `ncm-4/`, `ncm-5/`,
`ncm-6/`, `ncm-7/`, `ncm-8/`. 1,284 HTML/text files (~38 MB of text after tag-stripping) were
indexed and searched; roughly seventy documents are cited.

**Limits.**

- EROL is a partisan archive. It preserves what the movement wrote about itself *and* what its
  enemies wrote about it. Both are evidence of **what a line produced**; neither is a verdict.
  Hostile sources are marked.
- 308 PDFs in these folders were not read (scanned; OCR cost). No claim below rests on one.
- Some index entries have no text in the mirror — Klonsky's "The White Question", the RU draft
  programme appendix on the gay question, Haywood excerpts in `ncm-8/oc-racism`. They are listed
  as absent, never paraphrased.
- Trotskyist sources (`Young Spartacus`, `Workers Vanguard`) appear only where they quote RCP/RSB
  primary language verbatim. No Trotskyist mechanic enters.
- The **Bernard Dawson** fact — the ATF/CI informant who guided the Klan caravan at Greensboro —
  is *not* in EROL. What EROL contains is the pattern without the name (§2.2). The name comes from
  the 1980 federal civil-rights trial record, outside this corpus, and is flagged where used.

## 0. Thesis in five sentences

1. **The repression gap is not "the state is strong."** It is that the state's cheapest move is to
   let your enemy's auxiliaries do the killing and then seize the evidence — Greensboro is the
   clean instance, and the movement's own account supplies every step.
2. **The labor-aristocracy trap is not "you like white workers."** It is that you accept the
   actually-existing white workers' movement as *the* working class at the one moment when the
   oppressed nation's movement is the more advanced pole — and the tell is that your slogan becomes
   indistinguishable from the enemy's.
3. **The national question gap is a wager about whether a nation exists**, and the wager has
   organizational consequences before it has theoretical ones: whichever way you bet, one of your
   two mass bases stops being addressable in its own name.
4. **The party-building gap is not "call the congress."** PRRWO called one and had to un-call it;
   CPML called one and nobody came; CWP called one and was weeks from a massacre; OCIC refused
   to call one and could not expel anybody. The two-key lock is real and each key fails differently.
5. **The persistence gap is the one nobody plans for**: an organization can survive its errors and
   die of its survival — CPML emptied into dissolution within three years; LRS formally renounced
   vanguardism, the dictatorship of the proletariat, and Marxism-Leninism as the leading ideology
   in 1990.

# Part I — Catalogue

## 1.1 Factory colonization and "industrial concentration"

The archive is unusually honest here, because the organizations wrote their own post-mortems.

| Case | Where | What happened | Failure mode it names |
|---|---|---|---|

Two details worth keeping because they are *mechanical*, not decorative.

**(a) The blacklist is a cross-firm edge, not a random event.** General Baker was fired from Dodge
Main in 1968 for an "unlawful" work stoppage, helped found DRUM, and was then locked out of the
industry. Hired at Ford Sterling in 1971, he was fired after 30 days because "Ford Motor Co. had
secured his employment history from Chrysler Corp." The history included: "known black militant,"
"has communist leanings and visited Communist Cuba," "this subject is believed to be the leader of
the DRUM Movement." He was hired at Ford Rouge under an alias in September 1973 and fired in April
1975 for "securing employment under the use of an alias" — a discharge that violated the UAW
contract's own 12-month limitation, which is why a year-long legal battle put him back to work
(`E/ncm-3/baker-1-down.htm`). *The alias bought 19 months and cost a legal case.*

**(b) The center of gravity degrades from a political line into a commute.** RCP's own rectification
report names the economist tendency it had to fight: "**the center of gravity and industrial
concentration are everything**." RWH's account of the same campaign records what it became in
practice: "Concentrate in the struggles of the workers at the center of gravity has come more and
more to mean only a physical concentration, '**get jobs in the big plants**,' rather than a
political line and ideological question." And the gimmick that replaced line: "**wear the NUWO
T-shirt when you're fighting at the center of gravity, that'll get 'em.**"
(`E/ncm-5/rcp-split/rectification.htm`, `E/ncm-5/rp-8/campaign.htm`)

## 1.2 The multi-national / national question splits

| Formation | Position taken | What it cost |
|---|---|---|
| **RU/RCP** | The Black nation has been dissolved into the multi-national working class; the struggle is class, not national. | Boston (§1.3); a permanent charge against them in every unity negotiation; and the spectacle of a "vanguard" that had no mass organization in the Black movement. |
| **OL/CPML, Carl Davidson** | The Afro-American nation still exists in the Black Belt; right of self-determination up to secession. Davidson's 1975–76 *Guardian* series argues the nation persists *because* it is oppressed — "the feature that stands out is not the breaking up of the Negro concentration in the Black Belt, but its stubborn persistence" (`E/ncm-3/davidson/d-12.htm`). | Correct on the nation, and pushed into the "two movements, kept equal" formula that WVO called "the cornerstone of right opportunism" — because keeping them equal means holding the Black movement back (`E/ncm-1a/greensboro/part2.htm`). |
| **August 29th Movement** (Chicano) | Chicano nation / Aztlán; the "Revolutionary Wing" with PRRWO, RWL, WVO; then fusion with IWK and CAP/RCL into the League of Revolutionary Struggle (1978). | Its own self-criticism is the sharpest artifact in the archive: ATM had called OL "right opportunist," and recants — "we incorrectly placed them in the camp of the enemy" (`E/ncm-5/atm-unity.htm`). |
| **PRRWO** (Puerto Rican) | Out of the Young Lords Party. "Divided nation" theory first; corrected to "part of the multi-national proletariat"; convened a congress to proclaim itself the **Puerto Rican Revolutionary Party** and had to turn the congress into a committee for a multi-national party instead — after a split (`E/ncm-1/prrwo-history.htm`). | Minsk 1898 in the Caribbean. The congress had to be *un*-called, which is more expensive than not calling it. |
| **IWK / Wei Min She** (Asian) | IWK: open revolution in Chinatown, International Hotel, defense of Harry Wong. WMS (RU-backed): the masses are "too backward," use the social-welfare agencies, and do not raise the war. | The RU's Asian work produced an organization that picketed *for* the Holiday Inn's hiring of Chinese doormen "to lend 'atmosphere'" (`E/ncm-1a/iwk-wms-history.htm`). |
| **RU and OL on sexuality** | RU: "homosexuality in the US today is an individual response to the intensification of the contradictions brought about by decaying imperialism." RCP/RSB, as quoted by *Young Spartacus*: homosexuality "is anti-working class and counterrevolutionary," i.e. "sick" (hostile source, quoting primary language). | A cadre layer that must lie about its own life is a cadre layer the state can turn. The LA Research Group's rebuttal is the only document in the archive that treats the question methodologically (`E/ncm-3/gay-question/`). |
| **OCIC and white chauvinism** | The federation's own 1980 document admits that its form excluded the people it claimed to unite: "Federationism is racist and anti-working class." | See §2.4; this is the bridge between the national question and party-building. |

**The white-skin-privilege wager, and why it is a third position rather than the correct one.**
Noel Ignatin and Ted Allen's "White Blindspot" (mid-1960s; Ignatin later a founder of the
Sojourner Truth Organization) argued that the ruling class had struck a "deal" with the misleaders of
American labor and, through them, with "the masses of white workers": world conquest in exchange for
"a monopoly of the skilled jobs," cushioning against the economic cycle, and privileges "befitting
your white skin." On that basis the fight against white supremacy becomes "the central immediate task
of the entire working class," and white workers are called on to "repudiate" their privileges (all
quoted in `E/ncm-2/proletarian-cause/article12.htm`).

The critique in the same file is the useful part, because it is *quantitative*: only 25 per cent of
the workforce was unionized; a deal with misleaders is not a deal with the rank and file; and "to
pose the question of the masses of white workers in a deal is to obscure the real situation and to
impose upon the masses of white workers the material situation of only a small upper stratum."

So the shelf holds **three** positions, not two: RU's liquidation (no national question, no bribe),
the privilege theory (every white worker bribed), and the differentiated one (a craft/aristocracy
stratum bribed, the rest not). Babylon's `bribe` attribute must be set per node, never by
nationality — and the two errors have *opposite* symptoms. Liquidation makes you unable to see the
bribe at all; privilege theory makes you unable to see anyone who is not bribed.

The single most reusable sentence in this whole shelf is from the OCIC's own anti-racism paper,
quoting a comrade arguing for organizational representation at the founding conference: "**What's a
communist without an organization?**" (`E/ncm-8/oc-racism/racism-ocic/federationism.htm`). The
paper's reading is that the question already assumes the answer: anyone outside a circle is an
opportunist, so the circles' whiteness is the independents' fault.

**One case in full, because "whether a nation exists" sounds abstract until it decides who attends
the congress.** ATM's *Fan the Flames* held that "Chicanos constitute an oppressed nation within the
boundaries of the U.S.," with a program of land expropriation, "state unity of the Southwest," and
"the right of political secession." OL's reply in *The Call* (14 March 1977) held that Chicanos are
"part of the Mexican nation," annexed into the U.S. as a national minority, and that ATM's "Chicano
nation" was an idealist confusion of nation with territory. **Both cite Stalin.** The organizational
consequence is not academic: an oppressed nation with secession rights implies one kind of party and
one kind of alliance; a national minority implies another. The two positions then landed on opposite
sides of the 1978 fusion — ATM into LRS, OL into CPML — which is the cleanest demonstration on the
shelf that the national-question wager *is* a coalition decision, made before the theory is settled
(`E/ncm-3/ol-atm-2.htm`).

## 1.3 Boston busing as the archetype

Five organizations wrote about Boston and their accounts do not agree. That disagreement is
the artifact.

**The RU's public defense** (`E/ncm-2/ru-ol-busing.htm`, *Revolution*, Dec 1974) is that the plan
"is being consciously used by the ruling class to stir up antagonisms between white, Black and Latin
working people," that it improves education for nobody, and that calling racism the main issue
"boils down to pointing a finger at the white workers as the enemy of Black people, and really
amounts to tailing behind the line pushed by the liberal bourgeoisie like Kennedy." The RU opposed
the school boycott *and* opposed the plan.

**The RU's internal summation** (`E/ncm-3/ru-boston.htm`, "Class Stand Key In Boston Busing
Struggle") is a different document. It records, in the organization's own words:

- The first slogan: "**People Must Unite to Smash the Boston Busing Plan.**"
- The oscillation: "for a while it was 'freak out city,' as our old 'friends' from the moral
  movement mounted their attacks on the working class and on our line. The roar from the left that
  'the RU and the working class is racist' caused us to look over our shoulders to our petty
  bourgeois past."
- The wrong diagnosis: "Our main error in this period was to look to the bourgeois nationalists for
  leadership."
- The confession with the mechanism in it: "**Our Bundism came out in a tendency to see Black
  workers as Black, but not as workers.**"
- And the collapse of the position: "We dropped 'Fight the Boston Busing Plan' as a slogan for the
  contingent we were leading. Then we didn't even bring signs or banners putting out the slogan at
  all. This was mainly out of fear that we would be attacked by the nationalists and their movement
  allies."

Read that sequence as a state machine and it is exact: **correct analysis → maximal slogan →
hostile reception → loss of nerve → slogan dropped → no independent position → alliance with
nobody.** Both poles of the oscillation are errors, and the org pays for both.

**The CWP arrived at the same substantive line from a different direction.** Jerry Tung's busing
position, per CWP's own 1981 theoretical article, was that "as a whole busing has nothing to do with
improving the education either working class white children or black children. It is a scheme by
the bourgeoisie to divide the working class and usher in fascism," derived from the party's practice
in New York's School District One / Canarsie (`E/ncm-5/cwp-practice.htm`). **This matters for
design: the trap is structural, not a personal defect of the RU.** Two organizations with different
base areas, different nationalities, and mutual contempt reached the same place because the same
incentive gradient was in front of both.

**The New Voice's version** (OL-adjacent, `E/ncm-2/nv-boston-busing.htm`) supplies the material
facts the moral argument usually omits: Phase II closed 25 schools and ended 600 teaching jobs while
the administration spent "an estimated 23 million dollars this year on the diversionary racial
balancing scheme, including at least $7.6 million on forced busing alone." The white parents'
grievance was **real and material**; that is precisely why a chauvinist frame could capture it.

**The CLP's version** (`E/ncm-2/clp-busing-1.htm`) is the most alarmist and the most quotable:
"Liberalism as the Breeding Ground for Fascism," Boston as "the center for the imperialists'
generation of a fascist movement among the white section of the U.S.N.A. working class."

**The PUL's history** (`E/ncm-1a/pul-bus/`) is the only account that treats the anti-busing movement
as a *democratic* movement with a reactionary wing, and it names the liberal counter-moves —
"neutral sites" as "educational 'strategic hamlets'", metropolitanization, the Masters' Plan — and
one fact no other account has: "The only force in the courtroom to oppose the Masters' Plan was the
Hispanic parents," who were fighting the dispersion of bilingual education.

**The consequence nobody in 1974 could see.** CPML's recruiting advantage over RCP was partly
*this*: OL won adherents "because of the manner in which it differed ideologically with the RCP: its
more open stance toward reform movements, **its support for the Boston school integration
struggle**" (`E/ncm-3/index.htm`). Your line error becomes your rival's recruiting asset. That is a
mechanic worth having: **a doctrine choice should write MASS_LINK into somebody else's node.**

## 1.4 Premature party-building and its four failure modes

| Threshold attempt | Date | What was claimed | What actually happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PRRWO congress** | July 1972 | To proclaim the Puerto Rican Revolutionary Party. | Cadres studied Stalin on the national question mid-preparation, concluded they were not a party, turned the congress into "create the conditions for the formation of the multinational party," and split two months before it met (`E/ncm-1/prrwo-history.htm`). |
| **RCP founding** | 1975 | The party, out of RU plus allies, after the National Liaison Committee collapsed. | By the 3rd Plenum the CC was repudiating a "**second center**" and a "bourgeois headquarters" led by Comrade Mickey Jarvis, whose operating norms were "if you have more workers you have the right to speak; if you have fewer, then you really don't" and "it is easier to steer a truck once it's moving" (`E/ncm-5/rcp-split/rectification.htm`). |
| **CPML founding** | July 1977 | October League's party, via an Organizing Committee for a Marxist-Leninist Party. | "No other national formations in the New Communist Movement chose to join." ATM+IWK+CAP/RCL then merged into LRS "essentially in opposition to the CPML's claim to vanguard status" (`E/ncm-3/index.htm`). Three years later the chair, the editor, and the CC labor lead had all left; *The Call* went weekly → monthly; a section of the leadership advocated dissolution (`E/ncm-7/what-went-wrong/one-intro.htm`). |
| **CWP founding** | Oct 1979 | The party, formed "without even bothering to put out a program" (hostile: `E/ncm-5/greensboro.htm`). | Weeks later, five members were dead in Greensboro. The party's response was a membership drive named after the dead (`E/ncm-5/cwp-killing.htm`). |
| **OCIC** | Feb 1978 | Not a party: an *ideological center*, 18 Principles of Unity, Point 18 as the line of demarcation. | One year in: "several hundred members," a functioning national steering committee, and a published admission that its own form reproduced the whiteness it deplored (`E/ncm-6/oc-1st-year.htm`, `E/ncm-8/oc-racism/`). |

**The declaration is not the party, and the archive has the receipts.** PRRWO's own list of what
study revealed — that they could not "call a Congress to declare ourselves the Puerto Rican Party,"
that the central task was to "educate and organize the working class and to build the party of the
proletariat" — is the Minsk lesson learned at the cost of a congress and a split.

**And the terminal state is not defeat; it is dissolution into the milieu.** The League of
Revolutionary Struggle's 1990 dissolution statement rejects, by number: that Marxism-Leninism is
"the sole and leading ideology for social change"; "Lenin's view of the vanguard communist party";
and "the goal of the 'dictatorship of the proletariat'" (`E/ncm-7/lrs-last-congress-2/draft.htm`).
This is the same organization that in 1978 was the most multinational formation on the left. It did
not lose a fight. It **won** a place in the mass movement and then discovered that the place did not
require the doctrine.

## 2.1 Gap 2 — Boston maps onto the PatSoc / Labor-Aristocracy Chaser

The live `doctrine-tree.yaml` PatSoc pipeline runs: legitimate anti-imperial start → national
rhetoric raises `MASS_LINK` cheaply → `CLASS_ANALYSIS` maintenance gets expensive →
`NATIONAL_CHAUVINISM` crosses `CLASS_ANALYSIS` → allies change → point of no return. Boston is the
same shape with the sign of the nationalist term reversed, and that is the useful discovery: **the
pipeline has a mirror image.** Chauvinism does not have to be patriotic; it can be
*class-solidarist*.

Write it as a five-move sequence the engine can score:

1. **Correct read.** Busing is a bourgeois maneuver that redistributes cuts. (`CLASS_ANALYSIS` high,
   `MASS_LINK` low because you are in nobody's meeting.)
2. **Choose the vehicle.** The only organized mass force in South Boston is ROAR/ABM, with a real
   material grievance: 25 schools closed, 600 teaching jobs gone, $7.6M on buses. To be *in* the
   movement you must appear at its meetings.
3. **Rename the subject.** "The working class" is now the people in that hall. Black Boston is
   either "the nationalists" or an abstraction. `NATIONAL_CHAUVINISM` +2 per period of association,
   `MASS_LINK` +3.
4. **Slogan convergence.** "Fight the Boston Busing Plan" is now the same demand as the School
   Committee's, minus the racism. The engine should detect this as **slogan collision**: your demand
   string is a substring of the enemy's. That is the mechanical tell, and it is far better than a
   hidden alignment bar.
5. **Loss of nerve / isolation.** Attacked from the left, you drop the slogan rather than defend a
   better one. You keep the cadre who joined for the fight; you lose the ability to stand in the
   same march as the oppressed nation. `MASS_LINK -4`, and one ally edge is deleted permanently.

**What Babylon should count, concretely.**

| Quantity | Source in the case | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| `slogan_collision` | RU's "Smash the Boston Busing Plan" vs BSC's position | 0–1; >0.7 means your demand is the enemy's demand with better grammar |
| `subject_of_the_demand` | Who is named as the bearer: "the people," "the workers," "Black workers," "the nation" | categorical, not numeric; changing it is a Congress-level act |
| `ally_edges_deleted` | NAACP/Owens, Black parents' group at Hyde Park | permanent; the archive's "Bundism" line is the receipt |
| `rival_gain` | CPML's recruiting advantage from supporting integration | written to the *rival's* node, not yours |

**The receipt** should read like the RU's own summation, because the RU wrote it better than a
designer could: *"Our Bundism came out in a tendency to see Black workers as Black, but not as
workers."* And then the correction the engine must not grant for free: the RU's stated main error —
"to look to the bourgeois nationalists for leadership" — **is not the error the document describes.**
The document describes tailing the *white* movement and fearing the Black one. A rectification that
lowers the wrong variable should leave `NATIONAL_CHAUVINISM` untouched and charge full price in
Cadre Labor. That is the "self-criticism as misdirection" mechanic, and Boston is its proof text.

## 2.2 Gap 1 (+17) — Greensboro maps onto Fascist Militias + State Informant Collusion

The event is over-determined and the CWP's own account supplies the sequence. Read it as a script:

1. **Disarm by ordinance.** "They had a special ordinance passed prohibiting us from having weapons
   there" (`E/ncm-5/cwp-killing.htm`). A city council act, facially neutral, removes a capability
   from one node for one period. In `repression-logic.yaml` terms this is the legitimacy tier being
   spent to change the rules of engagement rather than to police.
2. **Let the auxiliary mass.** A caravan of "about eight vehicles (as contrasted to the reported
   two)" — lead car fires into the air to draw the march forward, rear car fires into the crowd.
   The Klan is cheap; the state does not have to be present to be decisive.
3. **Withdraw the police before contact.** "The fact that the local police left the area just before
   the gun battle began shows that they were tipped off about the planned assassinations." This is
   the mechanic the briefing-17 model already has: **withdrawal is cheap, deniable, and
   indistinguishable from incompetence.** No `REPRESS` order is issued; a patrol simply isn't there.
4. **Decapitate.** "Four of the five comrades murdered were leading members of the Party, one a
   member of the Central Committee," shot "either right in the head or in the heart." High-centrality
   nodes are the target; the state's map is better than the movement's.
5. **An unnamed supplier.** Grand Dragon Joe Grady, in the *Winston-Salem Journal*: "one person, who
   is not a Klansman and whom he did not name, brought most of the weapons to Greensboro." **This is
   the informant's signature: capability delivered, identity withheld.** EROL does not name him.
   The federal record does (Dawson). The game does not need the name; it needs the shape.
6. **Seize the evidence.** "A team of at least two dozen FBI agents were dispatched directly from the
   White House to seize the evidence and organize the cover-up." The player's Archive loses the
   primary record; the state's file becomes the only file. This is the `dossier capture` inverse in
   briefing 17 — the *state* captures the dossier.
7. **Witch hunt as second wave.** The Governor "called for the infiltration of extremist groups" and
   the SBI complied. Cadre who had been covert went open to "avenge the CWP 5" and lost their jobs.
   One shop steward was removed because "the union threatened to strike if the company did not remove
   her" (`E/ncm-1a/greensboro/part2.htm`). **The union is a second repression channel**, and this is
   the single most under-modelled fact on the shelf: the transmission belt runs both directions.

**Player-visible surface.** Not a "Heat: 74%" bar. Three things:

- **A named list of what you no longer know**, because the FBI took it.
- **The formation diagram** of the march, because the caravan's geometry determined who died.
- **One sentence from a member who has stopped taking your calls**, because that is what a
  post-massacre witch hunt actually produces (`part2` records exactly this: a recruit who raised
  criticism was told he was influenced by the bourgeois press and "ceased dealing with them").

**The counter-play the archive actually demonstrates.** CWP's pre-massacre practice was the
opposite of its own later myth: openly taunting the Klan ("cowards," "punks"), inviting them to a
"showdown," with the slogan "Death to the Klan." A competing organizer's phrase for the alternative
is the best line in the corpus: "**In the language of the streets, it's called 'selling wolf
tickets'**" (`E/ncm-1a/greensboro/part2.htm`). And the same document supplies the doctrine: "Mao
Zedong once commented to some U.S. communists that under U.S. conditions he would suggest a **90/10
ratio of secret to open work**."

So the decision is not "fight or don't fight." It is: *with what proportion of your leadership in the
street, under whose legal protection, with what withdrawal route, and with which of your capabilities
announced in advance.*

## 2.3 Gap 3 + Topology — DRUM / LRBW vs the UAW in Wayne County

This is the best-documented node-split cascade in the archive, and it is local.

**The cascade, with dates the sources pin:**

| Period | Node event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1968 | Wildcat at Dodge Main over speedup; General Baker fired for an "unlawful" work stoppage; DRUM founded. | `E/ncm-3/baker-1-down.htm` |
| 1969 | RUMs spread to other plants; **League of Revolutionary Black Workers** formed. Its General Policy Statement: "dedicated to waging a relentless struggle against racism, capitalism and imperialism." | `E/ncm-2/ru-nlc.htm`; `E/ncm-3/rcl-history/intro.htm` |
| 1970 | Motor City Coalition's in-plant task force (later MCLL) supports the Eldon Gear & Axle safety strike; runs educationals *under LRBW leadership* on a "whites organize whites" basis. | `E/ncm-2/mcll-1/intro.htm` |
| 1971 | BWC formed out of the League milieu. | `E/ncm-3/rcl-history/intro.htm` |
| 1972 | MCLL splits: Cockrel, Ravitz, Murphy leave as "right opportunists." By 1974 Ravitz is a judge who rules plant safety "irrelevant" in the Mack trial; Cockrel defends Mundy — a union bureaucrat who **shot a worker** protesting the 1973 contract — and calls Leonard Woodcock as a character witness. | `E/ncm-2/mcll-1/intro.htm` |
| 1973 | Three Chrysler wildcats (Jefferson, Forge, Mack) — MCLL's turning point. | same |
| 1975 | BWC splits four ways. Baker blacklisted out of Ford; runs for state representative on the Communist Labor Party ticket. | `E/ncm-3/index.htm`; `E/ncm-3/baker-1-down.htm` |
| 1978–80 | OCIC founded in Detroit; **DMLO and DSC** — two all-but-one-white circles — spend a year fighting each other instead of building a local center. A Black independent, Madie, has to break the circle game. | `E/ncm-8/oc-racism/racism-ocic/federationism.htm` |

The form travelled beyond auto: the BWC's own list of Black worker caucuses names "DRUM (Detroit),
the United Black Workers (Newark), Concerned Transit Workers (Chicago), **HRUM (New York)**, Fight
Back (N.Y.)" (`E/ncm-8/bwc-1/section9.htm`). HRUM is the hospital case — the same RUM structure in
a different industry — but EROL names it without documenting it, so it is a *shape*, not a case
study. Hospital work otherwise appears in this shelf as CWP's professional cadre base and as a
recruiting ground for the party, not as an industrial concentration with its own post-mortem.

**The UAW's counter-move is the mechanic.** From `E/ncm-7/rwh-blm.htm`:

> "After the 1967 rebellion and the birth of militant Black rank and file formations like DRUM in
> 1968, the auto companies and the UAW fell all over themselves getting Blacks into low- and
> middle-level management jobs. One effect of all this was to create a promise of mobility and a
> **buffer class** of more privileged Blacks which cushioned the system from the poor and working
> class masses."

Call it **absorption**, and make it the cheap default rather than repression. Repression costs
legitimacy and produces martyrs. Absorption costs a payroll line and produces a *former organizer
with a mortgage*. It also has a tell the game can show honestly: the node's most eloquent speaker
stops coming to meetings and starts explaining the company's position in a reasonable voice.

**Three split types the engine should distinguish, because EROL distinguishes them:**

1. **Split** (divisible membership): DRUM from the UAW local. The bargaining certificate stays with
   the parent; the new node gets the militants and a hostile edge. Neither can destroy the other.
2. **Flip** (indivisible seat): Ravitz's judgeship, Cockrel's practice. One person, one seat, and
   the seat's logic is now the state's. Nobody "betrayed" in a legible moment; the node changed
   class content by changing *occupation*. The MCLL's own reading — "Judge Ravitz covered himself
   with Marxist phrases, and blew the line of a common liberal" — is a class-content flip, not a
   split.
3. **Cascade** (multiplicative): BWC → four. When a node with high degree and low discipline
   resolves its contradiction, the split is not binary. It is a *fragmentation*, and each fragment
   keeps the name, which is why the archive has four organizations with near-identical programs.

**And the mirror error, which is the reason this belongs in Gap 3 rather than Gap 2.** The Socialist
Organizing Committee's trade-union document evaluates exactly this history and concludes that
DRUM-style formations, "though the anger behind them was understandable and did result in some
eventual gains, … **would have been far more effective channeled into a rank-and-file movement that
could struggle to win the support of all progressive workers**" (`E/ncm-1a/soc-tu.htm`). That is the
left's own version of absorption: it wants the militancy, rejects the national form, and offers the
militants a committee. In game terms it is a `merge` proposal that raises short-run coherence and
deletes the node's independent capacity. **The engine should offer it, price it attractively, and
let it be wrong.**

The same wager appears one level down in §1.2, and the three positions there map onto three
assignments of one attribute: `bribe = 0` everywhere (RU), `bribe = 1` for every white worker
(Ignatin), or `bribe` distributed by craft and sector. Only the third produces a plan rather than a
verdict — which is the difference between a doctrine screen and a doctrine wall.

## 2.4 Gap 4 — OCIC maps onto the Two-Key Party Lock

The two keys are: **A**, the act of constitution (programme, rules, a centre with the power to
dissolve subordinate bodies, a paper that is a supply chain); **B**, recognition by the other
contingents (rivals argue in your vocabulary; the state reallocates repression toward you; adjacent
fronts fracture or fuse into you).

OCIC is the cleanest natural experiment on record for **what a federation can and cannot buy**,
because it tried to buy Key B directly and refused Key A on principle.

**What it built** (`E/ncm-6/oc-1st-year.htm`): 18 Principles of Unity; a Point 18 ("US imperialism
is the main enemy of the peoples of the world") adopted 1 June 1978 as the line of demarcation
(`E/ncm-6/pt-18/theses.htm`); a national steering committee; local centers in two areas; "several
hundred members." Real achievements, honestly reported, including the failures.

**What it could not do.** Three structural facts, each of which is a missing verb:

1. **No dissolution power.** A federation's members are sovereign. Every attempt to pass a binding
   line returns `NO_AUTHORITY`. The available verbs are *debate*, *circulate*, *convene*. The OC's
   own rules are the proof: "No binding instructions" and "minority position representation" — both
   designed to protect members from the centre, which is exactly what makes the centre unable to
   act.
2. **The biggest circle becomes the centre without legitimacy.** PWOC's self-criticism: "A more
   serious error has been the view that in Philadelphia the OCIC is the PWOC … in practice this is
   how the work has been taken up." The federation reproduces hierarchy while forbidding itself the
   means to legitimate it.
3. **A federation of organizations structurally excludes the people with no organization.** This is
   the paper's central finding: independent Black Marxists in Detroit were reachable only as
   individuals, and the rules said organizations sent delegates. "Federationism is racist and
   anti-working class" is not a moral slogan in that document; it is a statement about a schema.

**And the trap inside the trap: it very nearly won Key B.** OCIC's vocabulary did become the
movement's vocabulary — "ultra-left," "circle spirit," "line of demarcation," "anti-left tendency."
If Babylon's Key B check is *rivals adopt your vocabulary*, OCIC passes and should not. So Key B
needs three sub-checks with different weights, and the archive supplies the ordering:

| Key B sub-check | Weight | OCIC result | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rivals argue in your vocabulary | **low** (0.2) | **pass** | "ultra-left" became the movement's term |
| The state reallocates repression toward you | **high** (0.5) | **fail** | the state's attention stayed on CPML and RCP; OCIC was not raided |
| Adjacent fronts fracture or fuse into you | **high** (0.3) | **fail** | the Club Network refused; "let a thousand centers bloom"; LRS formed *against* CPML, not with OCIC |

That is a genuinely non-obvious design conclusion: **a study federation can win the argument and
still not be a party, because nobody has to obey the winner.**

**The decision at the founding conference is ceremony-grade.** The motion on the floor: should the
nominations committee present "a slate of **organizations**" or should individuals be elected? It was
voted down, then reversed after struggle, and the reversal is why Madie — who had resigned from DMLO
over its refusal to fight racism — was in the room at all. In game terms the player chooses the
*electoral schema of the centre*, and that choice silently determines which of their mass contacts
are represented for the next twenty periods. No bar, no number: a rule.

## 2.5 Gap 5 — the persistence trap, and why "collapse" is the wrong word

Three distinct terminal states, all in the archive:

- **Emptying** (CPML): chair, editor, and CC labor lead gone; weekly paper becomes monthly; a
  section of the leadership advocates dissolution into a broad socialist organization
  (`E/ncm-7/what-went-wrong/one-intro.htm`).
- **Renunciation** (LRS, 1990): formal dissolution, with numbered rejections of vanguardism, the
  dictatorship of the proletariat, and the leading role of Marxism-Leninism
  (`E/ncm-7/lrs-last-congress-2/draft.htm`).
- **Franchise** (the sect): the organization persists indefinitely with a paper, a line, and no
  possibility of either victory or dissolution. EROL's very existence is evidence for this state —
  an encyclopedia of organizations that mostly outlived their own reasons.

For the `survival_interest` clock, the archive's contribution is **the shape of the slope**. CPML
went from founding (1977) to leadership exodus (1980) in three years — not twenty. LRS went twelve
(1978–1990). The Leninist 20-year window is real for a party with legal institutions; it is *not*
the timeline for a party with a paper and a claim. Suggested split:

- `survival_interest` accrues from **legal institutions** (halls, papers, seats, staff) — slow.
- `claim_interest` accrues from **the vanguard claim itself** — fast, because a claim must be
  defended daily and each defense narrows what the organization can say.
- Terminal states trigger on different ratios: emptying at high `claim_interest` + low `CL`;
  renunciation at high `survival_interest` + low `CLASS_ANALYSIS`; franchise at high
  `claim_interest` + stable `CL` + zero `MASS_LINK` growth.

# Part III — Five Decision Ceremonies

Format follows porch-01: split-pane, options side by side, honest summaries of what each *means*,
never what each *yields*; the click is the gavel. Each scene below is grounded in a specific
document, and each ends with what the game honestly does not know.

### 1. "The Sign in the Contingent" — Boston, 14 December 1974

**Room.** A church basement in Roxbury. Fifty people, mostly not yours. A stack of cardboard and a
can of black paint. Somebody has already painted one sign: SMASH THE BOSTON BUSING PLAN. The March
Against Racism is in four days and your contingent has to decide what it carries.

**The three options on the pane.**

- **Carry the painted sign.** *Means:* your contingent enters the march as the tendency that opposes
  the plan. You will be photographed beside the people who threw rocks at the buses.
- **Paint a new one: DECENT AND EQUAL EDUCATION FOR ALL.** *Means:* you keep the demand and lose the
  edge. The parents at Hyde Park will read it as support for the plan, because that is what the
  NAACP's lawyers say too.
- **Do not march. Take the shop gate.** *Means:* you spend the day where your class actually is and
  concede the terrain to everyone else.

**Staked, visibly.** Twelve Cadre Labor, one ally edge (the Hyde Park parents' group), your standing
with the "moral movement" that will decide whether you are invited anywhere in 1975.

**Unknown, marked unknown.** "Whether Black Boston will come out." *The archive's answer, which the
player does not get:* 800–1,000 people at the October violence, and the December march "failed to
bring out Black people from Boston" (`E/ncm-3/ru-boston.htm`).

**The seal.** *The contingent forms up behind the sign.* Music: a school bell.

**Reckoning, three periods later.** The sign is in the *Globe*. The October League's recruiter in
your city gets a better meeting than he had last month, and the game does not tell you why.

### 2. "The T-Shirt" — Cleveland, February 1977

**Room.** A rented hall, first national meeting of NUWO organizers. The line from the center is on
the mimeograph: build the organization "in its own right" and "in the course of struggle." Somebody
has brought a box of shirts.

**Options.**

- **Wear the shirt at the center of gravity.** *Means:* every militant you talk to at the gate learns
  the organization's name before they learn its program.
- **Spend the period on the safety demand; bring the organization in after the first win.** *Means:*
  slower, and the cadre who came for the convention will ask what they were convened for.
- **Postpone the founding convention one period.** *Means:* you admit you have no line, in public, to
  people who have been promised one.

**Staked.** 1,500 workers' travel and a hall deposit; the credibility of the center's "model
leadership"; roughly 40 Cadre Labor.

**Unknown.** "Whether anybody will still be in the room in a year." *The archive's answer:*
"what was built was not an organization but a big meeting" (`E/ncm-5/rp-8/campaign.htm`).

**The seal.** *The convention is called to order.* Gavel. No music.

### 3. "The Ordinance" — Greensboro, late October 1979

**Room.** A kitchen. Six people. The city has passed an ordinance prohibiting weapons at the
November 3 rally. The Klan has been invited, in writing, in a leaflet that called them cowards.

**Options.**

- **Hold the route, unarmed, and put the leadership in front.** *Means:* the party's most visible
  members become the most exposed people in the county, and the law has already been changed to
  ensure they cannot answer.
- **Move the rally indoors, adjacent, and keep armed self-defense.** *Means:* you lose the march and
  keep the cadre; the Klan keeps the street.
- **Cancel, and put the period into the mill drive.** *Means:* you lose the confrontation, the
  recruits who came for it, and the argument that you are the party that fights.

**Staked.** Five named people whose faces the player has seen. The mill organizing committee. The
party's legal existence.

**Unknown, honestly.** "Whether the police will be present." "Who is in the caravan." "Who supplied
the weapons." The game marks all three unknown and does **not** reveal them after the fact in a
tooltip; the Archive backfills only what a participant could learn — the Grand Dragon's statement
that an unnamed non-Klansman brought the guns, and the fact that the police left.

**The seal.** *The route is confirmed.* No music. A car door.

**Reckoning.** The state's file is now the only file. Cadre are visited at home. Several lose their
jobs for having gone open. One shop steward is removed from her plant because her own union
threatens to strike if she stays.

### 4. "The Second Centre" — RCP Central Committee plenum, 1977–78

**Room.** A long table. The Chair has a paper. Comrade Jarvis has a shorter paper, delivered without
warning. Two lines on China; behind them, two centres that have existed since before the founding.

**Options.**

- **Rectification campaign.** *Means:* you keep the party and spend two periods arguing about
  pragmatism, departmentalism, and who has the right to speak.
- **Let the faction go.** *Means:* you lose a region's worth of cadre and gain a rival who will
  describe you accurately for the next decade.
- **Postpone: study, circulate, decide next plenum.** *Means:* the second centre gets a year to
  become the first one.

**Staked.** The industrial concentration line; the norm that "if you have more workers you have the
right to speak"; roughly a third of the CC.

**Unknown.** "Whether the faction is a line or a personality." *The archive's answer, in the report's
own words:* "a line and ideological outlook opposed to that of the Party," operating "under the
banner of carrying out the Party's line."

**The seal.** *The report is adopted, 2 to 1.* A stamp.

### 5. "The Slate" — OCIC founding conference, Detroit, February 1978

**Room.** A union hall. Delegates from a dozen circles, plus independents who are not delegates and
know it. The Committee of Five proposes that the nominations committee present "a slate of
**organizations**" for the steering committee.

**Options.**

- **Organizations elect the steering committee.** *Means:* the centre is a conference of circles, and
  the people who are not in circles are represented by whoever happens to be married to one.
- **Individuals elect the steering committee.** *Means:* the largest circle loses its proportional
  advantage, and its leaders will say you are handing the centre to people with no base.
- **Seat both: organizational delegates plus an individual roll.** *Means:* two franchises in one
  body, and every subsequent vote is a constitutional crisis.

**Staked.** Whether the independent Black Marxists in the room — including the woman who resigned
from an all-white circle over its refusal to fight racism — are inside the centre or outside it.

**Unknown.** "Whether this becomes a centre or a debating society." *The archive's answer:* both. It
produced the movement's vocabulary and could not expel anyone.

**The seal.** *The motion is put.* Voices. A recount.

# Part V — What this does not license

- **No adventurism as a correct line.** The ACWM's 1970 Cleveland march — 25 communists through 150
  hard-hats, "Five police were injured; four of our comrades were injured and eight arrested," and
  the headline verdict "a tremendous victory" (`E/ncm-1/acwm-hard-hats.htm`) — is a *receipt*, not a
  model. It belongs in the Wiki's vigilance file, not in the player's option set.
- **No Trotsky.** The Spartacist material is used only where it preserves RCP's own words. Nothing
  in the mapping depends on a Trotskyist category.
- **No "the archive proves the line was wrong."** EROL preserves both a line and its enemies. The
  engine's job is to make the *consequences* legible, not to score the theory. Where two accounts
  conflict (RU vs PUL on Boston; CWP vs Bolshevik League on Greensboro), the briefing records the
  conflict rather than resolving it.
- **No claim that these organizations were only failures.** WVO's textile drive, IWK's International
  Hotel, the Black parents at Hyde Park, the Hispanic parents in the Masters' Plan hearing — these
  are the moments when the correct pole existed and was *not* taken. That is the whole reason the
  material is playable: the player should be able to see the turn and miss it anyway.
