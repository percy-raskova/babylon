# The opening of the national economy: design

Date: 20 November 2026. Issue: PER-371 (and the repair of PER-370). Status: approved by the
Director on 20 November 2026 ("Approve: plan and build"). Decision record: ADR313.

## 1. What was asked

The Director, on 20 November 2026, after reading the record of the nationwide collapse:

> this sounds like we need some kind of financial system and credit and loans and a financial
> market and all that stuff, volumes 2 and 3

> then consumers need to be demand, as do foreign corporations because unequal exchange means that
> the imperialist core sells back capital to the periphery, high labor inputs from periphery, high
> capital intensive exports from core. also households are part of the consumer economy. workers
> spend their wages. there also needs to be capital for expansion like investments, construction,
> production, ramping up production, etc.

> make it interesting enough for a video game, the goal is entertainment not scientific
> explanation, the goal is to make the game engaging and dynamic and emergent

The order agreed that day: (1) the supply repair and the price of a short market; (2) households
spend what they earn; (3) capital for expansion, with debt and bank credit; (4) trade by kind of
product, with different techniques in core and periphery; then household debt, tradable claims and
public debt. This design is steps 1 and 2: an opening that runs, and households that spend. It
prepares step 3 and does not build it.

The Director's answers that this design carries out:

| Asked | Answered |
|---|---|
| Money left after needs | Spend it, tier by tier; only what is left after the last tier is kept |
| The consumer tier | A new product, consumer goods, and a luxury product |
| Wages | Each trade in each place pays its observed average wage |
| Recipes and prices | From the national accounts: every observed job needed, every trade covers its cost |
| Makers of the new products | Two new trades cut from the jobs data |
| How much each band spends | Get the federal consumer expenditure survey |
| A short product | One market, money decides: a comfort can outbid a need |
| Households with no earner | "public benefits, cut when taxes fall, the benefits and state services and disability pension etc can become a site of struggle eventually and be modified, tweaked, increased, etc thru the political/electoral system" |
| The luxury trade | Leisure and hospitality |
| Earners outside Wayne | Split every place by band |
| A laid-off worker | Unemployment benefit for 26 weeks |

## 2. What the player sees

**Twelve trades, each paying what it pays in that place.** In Wayne: the car plants (consumer
goods, about 53,000 jobs), hospitals and schools (household services, 182,000), offices (business
services, 149,000), stores and warehouses (distribution, 127,000), restaurants and hotels (leisure
and hospitality, 71,000 at under $20 an hour), and the rest.

**Every household at one of four standards, every period.**

| Standard | What it means |
|---|---|
| Going without | A need was not met |
| Getting by | Needs met; comforts not complete |
| Comfortable | Comforts complete; luxuries not complete |
| Affluent | Every tier complete; what is left is kept |

**A shock that travels, and can be watched.** Car sales are a comfort, so they are the first thing
a household drops. A plant lays off. Its households fall from comfortable to getting by. Stores,
restaurants and services lose customers and lay off in turn. Tax receipts fall, and with them
public services and benefits. The County Review meets over a budget that is really shrinking.

**Standards move before hunger does.** A downturn shows first as households leaving the
comfortable standard. That is the player's early warning, and their opening to organize.

**A layoff has two steps.** A laid-off worker's household lives on about half its pay for 26
weeks. Then the benefit stops. Both steps can be seen coming.

**Classes in every place.** The bottom half, the next 40% and the owners of each place are
separate accounts, each with its own standard.

**Prices that were paid.** With the price of a short market (ADR312), a short product shows the
price paid beside the posted one, and who was priced out.

**Public money that is spent.** Each public body's receipts, the services it pays for and the
benefits it pays out, and what was cut this period.

## 3. Today

Measured on 20 November 2026 (`reports/economy-repair-2026-11-20/`):

- By period 5 households across the country lack 80% of their food. Twelve candidate repairs of
  the scenario's numbers were tried over 34 periods. None holds: the best still leaves about 85% of
  food unmet at period 34, with 33 million at work of 161 million.
- **The designed recipes need about a quarter of the workforce.** Over 110 million are laid off in
  period 2.
- **Households with no earner have no income.** They are 22.5% of households (27.6% in Wayne).
- **A household buys one fixed basket whatever it earns.** Money above it is never spent.
- **Every job pays $24 an hour.** Observed pay runs from about $16 in leisure and hospitality to
  about $60 in extraction and energy, and averages $35.86.
- **Public money piles up.** Public bodies' cash grows from $1.3 trillion to $7.6 trillion over
  the 34 periods. A body's payments are capped at the opening's figure, and its receipts are not.
- **A business counts an order stuck behind a full route as arriving,** stops ordering at home,
  and its home supplier lays off.
- Ten trades, one product each. Six needs. Nine recipes. Those counts are fixed in about fifteen
  places of the opening.

**What this design stands on.** The price of a short market (ADR312) is built first, through its
Task 7. Its Task 5 settles the stuck orders: what the shipping pass after a sale does not send is
cancelled and its money returned. The rebuilt scenario is the one that asks for that rule.

## 4. The rules

**O1. Twelve trades.** The ten of today, and two cut out of them from the jobs data:

| Trade | Product | Made of |
|---|---|---|
| Consumer goods (new) | consumer goods, a good | Apparel, footwear, furniture, textile products, miscellaneous goods (from manufacturing); motor vehicles, bodies and parts, audio and video equipment, household appliances (from capital goods) |
| Leisure and hospitality (new) | leisure, a service | Arts, entertainment and recreation; restaurants and hotels (from household services) |
| Household services | as today | Education, health care and other services |

The other nine keep their industries. Each trade still makes one product. The designed claim of
Japan's owners on Wayne's plants, and of Wayne's owners on Canada's, follows the car plants into
consumer goods.

**O2. Pay.** A workplace pays the observed average wage of its trade in its place: annual pay over
jobs over 2,080 hours, from the jobs data. Where the figure is withheld, it takes its state's wage
for the trade, then the nation's. A place that stands for several counties pays their summed pay
over their summed jobs. A foreign region pays a designed fraction of the United States wage of the
same trade. Two figures that read today's single wage get their own: the County Review's going wage
is the county's average pay, and the City PD's payroll is the pay of Wayne's public workplaces.

Where a county's jobs in a finer industry are withheld and the industry above it is published, the
withheld industries share what is left of the published figure, jobs and pay, by their number of
establishments. That sets the pay of the trade in that county; where the figure above is withheld
too, the state's wage stands in, then the nation's. Where residents work is decided as today. (At
the finer cut most counties' car-plant jobs are withheld. Wayne's 53,000 are the published part.)

**O3. The accounts.** One committed table gives, for the twelve trades, what each buys from each,
what it sells to households, to public bodies and abroad, what it pays in wages and what it keeps.
It is built from the 2024 national input-output tables and the jobs data, both already held. A
builder turns the table into the scenario's numbers: recipes, opening prices, the households'
baskets, the hours of retail, the shares of trade and the tax rates. The table is coarse and the
numbers are round. They set the opening once. Nothing in play reads the table.

**O4. Every observed job is needed at the opening.** A trade's hours for a unit of its product are
its observed hours over what the game's buyers take from it at the opening. What a trade sells in
reality to a buyer the game does not have is spread over the buyers it does have.

**O5. Every trade covers its cost.** A product opens at its cost price plus its trade's margin.
A workplace's own opening price follows its own wage, so services cost more where pay is higher. A
scenario in which a private product opens under its cost at the nation's average wage is refused.

**O6. Four balances at zero.** Money is conserved and nothing can be owed, so what one part of the
economy keeps, another loses. At the opening:

1. households spend what they receive;
2. public bodies pay out what they collect;
3. what is sold abroad pays for what is bought abroad;
4. what businesses keep of their profit is what they spend on equipment.

The builder sets the tax rates, the share of profit paid out, the volume of exports and the size of
the tiers so that each balance closes within one percent of its flow.

**O7. Households buy in tiers.** A household's list is its needs, then its comforts, then its
luxuries. Each tier is a basket:

| Tier | Holds | Size, of the average spending of a person |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Needs | The least of every necessity | 40% |
| 2. Comforts | Consumer goods; more housing, services, food and energy | the next 60% |
| 3. Luxuries | Leisure; more of everything again | the next 150% |

Money left after the last tier is kept. The sizes are designed numbers read from one table of the
survey. A need that is not met is going without. A comfort or a luxury that is not reached is not.

**O8. Comforts and luxuries bid in the same markets as needs.** A household bids for a thing with
the money it does not need, at posted prices, for the things ahead of it in its list that it has
still to buy this period. So its own needs come before its own comforts. So does the tax it owes on
the period's wages: that tax is collected after the buying, and a comfort bought with its money
would leave it unpaid. Between households, money decides: in a short market a better-off
household's comfort can outbid a poorer household's need, at one price for all (ADR312).

This amends ADR312 in two places. Its P2 gave a buyer one bid in a market; a household's bid now has
a rung for each tier, each with the money that tier may use. Its P7 named seven products in the
order of paying; consumer goods and leisure join the order, and a household's list goes by tier
first and by that order inside a tier. One rule holds for every household of the campaign, abroad
as at home.

**O9. Distribution is the shop.** The distribution trade's workers are the hours of retail on the
goods households buy. Freight as a trade of its own is left to the transport program.

**O10. Building and software are bought as inputs.** A trade's recipe carries the premises and the
business services that, in the accounts, it buys as investment. Only equipment is bought as
equipment.

**O11. Trade by product.** Each product has its own share bought abroad and its own volume sold
abroad, from the accounts. A foreign region's mix of jobs is computed, so that the region is in
balance by construction. Techniques that differ between core and periphery are step 4.

**O12. Benefits.** There are two kinds at the opening, each a named row of the scenario.

*For a household with no earner.* It is paid a benefit each period for each of its
persons: enough, at the opening's prices, for the needs of tier 1. People in a collective residence
who hold no job are paid the same. One national fund pays it, out of its share of the wage tax and
of the tax on operating income collected in every place. Each kind of benefit is a named row of the
scenario, with its own rate and its own people, so that politics can change it later. The opening
has one kind. A household is covered by what it was at the opening, owner or not, and the payment
follows the number of its persons as they are born and die. The fund is the United States' own:
a foreign region's households are one account that holds its workers and its other people
together, and the dependencies stay as they are today.

*For a worker who is laid off.* Whoever is looking for work now is paid half the pay of the job
they lost, for 26 weeks: six periods in full and half of the seventh. Then it stops. The national
fund pays it. Those who were already looking for work at the opening keep today's county support;
the benefit is for a job lost in play. When work returns, the last laid off are taken back first.

**O17. Bands in every place.** In every place the households that earn are three accounts, where
today they are two: the bottom half, the next 40%, and the owners, about the top tenth. A place's
jobs are ranked by their pay. The owners' earners hold the best-paid, the next 40% the next, the
bottom half the rest, each band with jobs in proportion to its households. So a band's income
differs by the pay of its trades, and a plant's layoff lands on the band that holds its workers.
These are pooled accounts. Wayne's household units, when they resume, are finer and follow the same
bands.

**O13. Public money is spent, and cut when it runs short.** A public body pays its programs in
full while it has the cash. When it is short, the programs of one rank are cut in the same
proportion. (Today payments go by rank and, inside a rank, in the order of an identifier: the
first is paid in full and the last gets nothing.) It opens with two periods of its payments in
reserve, as today, so a fall in receipts first drains the reserve and then cuts. A place's own
body keeps what it pays today: public provision, the County Review's relief list and the City PD.
The County Review does not rank benefits. The builder sets the two tax rates and the fund's share
of each.

**O14. Equipment wears out a little every period.** A workplace opens with machines of every age,
so that about one in a hundred is replaced each period across the economy, and the makers of
capital goods have customers from the first period. (Today a quarter of the workplaces replace
everything at once in period 25, and nobody buys before.) It also opens with machines for its whole
workforce, so that the opening has no expansion to do. Capital for expansion is step 3.

**O15. The map's places stay.** The 187 places are made from today's food recipe and food need.
Those figures are written down as the map's own basis, so recipes can change without moving a
place. The room of each kind of terminal stays a designed figure of the transport policy. Airports
are raised so that the opening's flows fit, and the record of health lists every route that is
full.

**O16. On asking.** The rebuilt scenario is `schema_version = 8`. It carries the order of paying,
and compiles as `national-world-v13`: the shipped world changes its compiler once, for the price
rule and the rebuilt opening together. Household units, when they resume, are `national-world-v14`.
A scenario of an earlier schema compiles to the bytes it compiles to today. ADR312 recorded the
Director's earlier choice that wages stay during the repair; the rulings of section 1 replace it.

## 5. What it does not do

- Credit, banks, interest, debt (step 3). Until then money that is kept lies idle, so the tiers
  are sized for the top to spend nearly all it earns at the opening.
- Different techniques in core and periphery (step 4).
- Homes and ownership (the households lane). Every household still buys its housing.
- Freight as a trade; public ownership of schools and hospitals as a rule of its own.
- Politics over benefits and services. The rates are built so that politics can change them.
- Durable goods that last. A car is bought as a flow.

## 6. What would make it a failure

- A calm economy in which nothing moves.
- A model tuned to reproduce statistics that the player never feels.
- A clock that runs down whatever the player does.

## 7. Approaches

1. **Change the numbers of today's table.** Measured: twelve candidates, none holds.
2. **Keep the designed recipes; price each product at cost plus one rate of profit.** Every trade
   covers its cost, and the layoffs stay.
3. **Rebuild the opening from the accounts (chosen by the Director).** Every job is needed and
   every trade covers its cost by construction.
4. **The same at the accounts' full detail of 71 industries.** Six times the trades, and nothing
   the player would see for it.

## 8. How we know it works

1. **The builder's balance sheet.** For each trade: what the game's buyers take against what its
   workers can make, and its cost against its price. And the four balances of O6.
2. **The opening's own report,** read from the compiled opening before any period runs: jobs needed
   against jobs present, by trade and place; the standard each household account opens at; each
   public body's receipts against its payments; the sellers that open under their cost.
3. **A quiet run of 34 periods across the country,** with no player and no shock, recorded as the
   health of the economy is recorded today. At every period: at least 90% of the opening's jobs; at
   least 95% of every need met; no product under its cost at more than one seller in twenty; money
   conserved. The foreign regions are held to the same bands, because what the country buys
   abroad depends on them.
4. **Three runs that are pushed,** each a variant of the opening: benefits at three quarters; one
   foreign region's routes at a tenth of their room; consumer goods bought abroad at twice the
   share. Each must differ from the quiet run where the player would look (standards, layoffs,
   prices), spread beyond where it began, and not end in collapse: at least 80% of every need met
   at period 34.
5. **Play.** Wayne, twelve periods, in the real client.

If the quiet run cannot hold its bands, the cause is found first. Stabilizers are then measured one
at a time and brought to the Director with their numbers, never added unasked. The first two
candidates: a company pays out the cash it holds beyond its working need; a public body's reserve
is deeper than two periods.

## 9. Calls made here, for the Director to check

1. **A tier is a basket,** not one product (section 4, O7). Without it, housing and health care
   have far more workers than customers.
2. **The sizes of the tiers:** 40%, the next 60%, the next 150% of a person's average spending. Read
   from the survey's incomes per person by tenth: the second and third tenths stand at 0.41 and 0.50
   of the average, the seventh and eighth at 0.95 and 1.10, the top tenth at 2.41.
3. **Wages are pay, without the employer's benefits.** The jobs data gives pay. In the accounts,
   employers also pay about 30% on top in health insurance and pensions. In the game that money
   stays with the business, and reaches households as profit paid out and as public benefits. So
   the game's incomes are less equal than the country's.
4. **Before banks, the top spends nearly all it earns at the opening.** Money that is kept is a
   drain until it can be lent.
5. **The whole distribution trade is retail** (O9), and investment in buildings and software is
   bought as inputs (O10).
6. **The rent that home owners are counted as paying themselves is left out.** Every household
   still buys housing, at a price that covers building, upkeep and an ordinary margin.
7. **Public schools and hospitals stay with household services,** and are paid for by households.
   Tax-paid schooling and health care would be a rule of their own.
8. **Trade is balanced in money at the opening.** The country's real deficit needs the lending
   that step 3 and after will bring.
9. **A workplace's price follows its own wage** (O5).
10. **One national fund pays benefits** (O12). If each place paid its own, poor places would cut
    from the first period and rich ones would hoard.
11. **The map's places are pinned** (O15), and machines open at every age and for the whole
    workforce, so nothing expands at the opening (O14).
12. **Withheld jobs** are placed from what is left of the published figure above them, to set the
    pay of a trade in its county (O2). Where residents work is decided as today.
13. **Abroad:** the tiers apply, the benefits do not (O8, O12).
14. **A household with no earner is covered whether or not it owns business shares** (O12).
15. **Each band holds jobs in proportion to its households** (O17). Bands then differ by the pay of
    their trades, not by how many of their people work.
16. **The unemployment benefit is for a job lost in play.** Those looking for work at the opening
    keep today's county support (O12).
17. **Bands are dealt county by county** (O17). Inside each county its jobs are ranked by pay and
    dealt to that county's three bands; a place's accounts are the sums over its counties.
18. **The County Review's floor follows the wage** (O2): two hours at Wayne's average pay, about
    $74, where the earlier ruling named $48 (two hours at $24).
19. **The City PD pays the wage of its one payroll workplace** (O2).
20. **The wage tax is ahead of comforts and luxuries** (O8). Measured on the small world with a
    fund: with the tax collected after the buying, households spent its money on comforts, 280 of
    640 was collected, and the fund was short from the third period. A need still bids with the
    whole purse, as today.

## 10. Build order

Each stage leaves the game running and every earlier compiler's bytes unchanged. The price rule's
Tasks 5 to 7 come first.

0. **A small world that holds.** A hand-built economy of three trades, in balance, run for 34
   periods in the engine's own tests, then pushed. It shows what the engine's rules do to a
   balanced economy before the country is rebuilt on them.
1. **The two reference tables:** the jobs and pay of the twelve trades by place, and the accounts
   of the twelve trades. Builders, sources, contracts. (A first cut of the accounts exists. It was
   made before leisure took in restaurants and hotels, and it still counts the rent that home owners
   are counted as paying themselves; this stage cuts it again.)
2. **Twelve trades** in the kernel, the opening and the client, pay by trade and place, and the
   bands of every place.
3. **Tiers** in the engine: the list of comforts and luxuries, the bid with several rungs, the four
   standards, their records.
4. **Benefits and the public budget:** the national fund and its share of the taxes, the two
   kinds of benefit, the record of who was laid off when, and the cut in proportion.
5. **The builder and the new numbers:** recipes, prices, baskets, retail hours, trade by product,
   tax rates; the opening's report; the map's places kept.
6. **Measure, tune, play.** The quiet run, the three pushed runs, Wayne in the client, the whole
   gates, and the report to the Director.
