# The Pay Fight: Design

Date: 2026-11-24. Decision record: ADR323. Status: approved by the Director on 24 November, to be
built after stored hours and before the bridge. The Director's ten answers of that day are below,
word for word. The rest is the lead's, written once and checked once by a second reader, whose
findings are folded in. Two parts were written after the approval, from answers 7 and 8, and
await the Director's reading: founding (PF18) and the demand for work (PF6, PF12). They were
checked once, and that check is folded in too. The lead's calls are listed for the Director to
check. Corrections are small amendments while the build runs.

## What was asked

On 22 November the Director ruled on the slump that comes years after a failed harvest has
returned: "Play, ended by struggle" (ADR319). It does not fail the test of the economy. "It lasts
until workers win pay." Organized workers who answer a squeeze were to be designed next as the way
out, and how that fight looks was the Director's to rule (ADR319, J5). This is that design.

Four records meet in it. All four are the Director's.

**The approved rule of November (ADR314, A16).** "Each trade in each place opens with its observed
share of union members. A union is an organization with a fund from dues. It answers a cut with a
stoppage, and asks for a rise where hands are short, when its fund would outlast the employer's
money. In a stoppage its members give no hours and draw strike pay; the employer loses output and
may take on seekers if there are any. The accounts end it as they change: the employer gives way
when the stoppage has cost it more than the cut would save in a year; the union gives way when its
fund and its members' money run out. A loan to the employer, hands that cross the line, or money
sent to the strikers can each turn it. These unions defend their own members' pay, not the class."
With it A15 (pay moves by steps), A22 (a stoppage withholds hours, and the economy takes it as a
push like any other, whoever starts it) and A23 (every act leaves a record).

**The strike design of August (the Neel integration design of 25 August, sections 4, 5, 8, 9, 15
and 16; ADR232, ADR234, ADR237).** A strike is a proposal, not an order. "Only a materially
connected worker organization may propose a strike at a labor process. A proposal never commands
worker participation." Every affected group of workers decides for itself, every period: it "may
join, continue, reduce, abstain, or leave". It may weigh only attributed facts: "grievance, prior
practice, directed reliability evidence, provision, strike funds, replacement risk, lost income,
repression, household needs, concessions, and observed participation". "Unknown facts remain
unknown." There is no strike stage: an episode begins when hours are first withheld and ends when
none are. The organization "may recommend, withdraw its recommendation, negotiate, provision,
communicate, or defend. It cannot directly end worker withholding." "The strike's only immediate
material carrier is available labor at the target process." A practice leaves competence,
repertoire, trust evidence and ties, and "Co-participation alone cannot mint a pairwise clique or
generic solidarity." It cannot write membership, solidarity or victory. One episode founds no
organization: that takes "a shared decision relation that persists beyond one episode", with three
other conditions, and every participant's own decision. The game's code holds the first half: a
strike proposal is admitted, and every affected group is left "awaiting its own governed
participation resolver" (`babylon-practice-contract`, `strike.rs`). The resolver, and the labor
that is withheld, are not built. This design is that second half.

**The strike rulings of July (ADR176, 11 to 14).** The strike is the player's verb: `Mobilize`,
sub-mode `strike`, and nine keys hold (12). What a strike leaves behind is organization (13).
Funding is a surface of the organization's line; ship simple at the start (14). Ruling 11 said:
"THE BRIBE, NOT THE FIGHT, SETS THE CORE WAGE: a won core wage strike yields organization,
tension, and material cost — zero wage gain while Phi holds. Economism is not punished by a
debuff; it simply does not work. Narrator + decision-zone legibility required."

**The form every fight takes (the homes design, H16 and H18; Amendment AM).** H16: "A household
joins an action when what the action offers it within a year is more than what joining puts within
reach of the law's steps in that year, both in its own dollars. A household with a larger stake
needs a larger offer; nothing happens at any fixed amount." H18: "Each fight is decided by a race
between two sets of accounts". Its call 13: "The year is the horizon of the weighing, of a holding
in a strike and of an employer in a stoppage (ADR314 A16)." Amendment AM: a rule decides for one
member by comparing amounts the circuit holds for that member, and no constant sets where a
response turns.

## What the player sees

The figures marked *recorded* are from one run of the bench as it stands today, where no fight
exists: the failed harvest that returns, in the world with a fund, Place A (`fund-P2` of the
closest form's reference set). What a workplace paid its owners is worked out from the run's
sales, prices and hours. Everything else is a sketch of the rules, not a measurement.

What can be seen at any time:

- **The measures**, for the people of every workplace: a period's pay beside the cost of their own
  needs at posted prices; what the period's work paid the owners beside what it paid them; the
  owners' take beside the people laid off and any cut of pay. They are read from receipts. They
  start nothing.
- **The news**, with the cause of each item, and **an event** where your organization could act.
  An event carries a forecast made from the figures: what a stoppage would cost each side a
  period, how long each could hold, who would take the places, what holding them would cost.
- **Organizations**: each as a hatched ring around its member households (a set's drawing,
  ADR317), with its fund, its people at work and its people who look for work. A fund that people
  are keeping, before it is an organization, is a thinner ring.
- **A stoppage**: the people at the gate, no hours going in, money leaving a fund for the people
  out and for the people it holds back, the struck work offered and who takes it.
- **A contract**, on the workplace: its pay, its terms and its last period.
- **The payer**, after every win: what the owners did in the six periods that followed, who bought
  at a raised price, and what the winners get back in hours, before and after.

The player's decision, in one sentence: you decide what your organization proposes and whom it
pays; every household decides for itself what it does.

## Today

- **Pay is fixed.** A rate of pay is a figure on each household's place at a workplace
  (`EmploymentTerms.compensation`, in the engine's state). No code changes it during a run. The
  workplaces of the small world pay $10 an hour (a clinic pays $11 under one push).
- **Nobody can withhold hours.** A person on the rolls is on the clock for a period's hours; only
  the employer's plan and its money decide what is worked and paid.
- **Workers hold nothing.** On the bench a period's pay equals the cost of a household's needs at
  the opening prices, and worker households open with no money. Round eight said so: "every wage
  opens equal to its household's need". A household is `GoingWithout` (the engine's word) as soon
  as one need is unmet.
- **The laid off.** Where there is a fund, a person laid off is paid half the pay of the job lost
  for 26 weeks, six periods in full and half of the seventh. Where there is none, nothing. People
  looking for work are taken on by any workplace of their place, the best-paying first: "A move
  is for good." A workplace calls back its own people before it takes others.
- **One household owns a place's four workplaces**, takes all their profit every period as the
  period's last payment, and covers no running loss. A workplace keeps about one period of its
  outlay in money.
- **An organization can already hold money.** The engine has accounts for organizations and moves
  money between a household and an organization as mutual aid (collections and aid, built for the
  organizer of the real game). The small worlds open no organization, and the scene's reading
  refuses such a payment.
- **The strike exists as a proposal only** (see above). The real game's organizer can already
  collect money, give aid in food and money, and spend its people's hours, on the one screen that
  counts as play (the Organizer Workspace). The small world's scene is an administrative view and
  counts for no gameplay milestone.
- **The later slump, as round ten counted it.** Care runs short and its price rises; the gain goes
  to the owners; the owners bid for food; the shop's price climbs from $35 to about $95; "workers
  who pay that much for food cannot pay for care", and hours end at 70% to 82%. Its cause line:
  "no worker's pay follows the cost of living". Organized workers have never been on the bench.

**What the bench can hold, counted on 24 November.** The closest form's reference sets on today's
engine, 25 runs of 400 periods at each set of figures (`pc-down-400`, `pc-down-nat-400`). A
workplace's take is worked out from sales, prices and hours (one unit of power for each unit of
farm food and of care; the shop buys the farm's food), after the fund world's tax; it gives the
owners' known income in the calm run. In two runs a clinic's higher pay is not in it.

owners have more to lose by a stoppage than the people have. It is the shop, then the farm. The
utility's people, half the workers of a place, are short as often as anyone, and their own
workplace almost never earns more than its payroll: they can win only because a place without
power makes nothing (PF7). In nine cases of ten there are people looking for work in the place.

## The design

Eighteen rules. PF1 to PF4 give the economy what it lacks. PF5 to PF8 are the weighings: each is
a rule for one household or one employer, comparing its own amounts. PF9 to PF13 are
organizations, the win and the loss. PF18 is founding. PF14 to PF17 are what is shown and who
decides.

### What the economy gains

The engine learns five material acts: a rate that changes; hours withheld; withheld hours filled
above the rolls; named people taken on, with a floor on the rolls; payments to and from a fund. It
takes them as orders of a period, checks them and applies them. It decides none of them. Each
order is canonical input of its period: it is in the state's digest and in the stored form, for a
world that asks. The decisions are made outside the engine: in this build by the small worlds'
runner, which is the scene's stand-in for the two resolvers that the August design gives to BSL,
of participation and of founding, and later by the tick.

**PF1. Pay lives on the wage relation and can change at a period's boundary.** A change of pay is
an order with a cause and a receipt: an offer accepted (PF11), a date of a contract (PF12) or,
where its switch is on, the employer's own step (PF4). The engine applies it at a boundary, and
the new rate holds from the first hour of the next period. A workplace has one rate: an order
changes it for everyone who works there. Every rule that reads a rate (the bound on hiring, the
stop rule, the order in which workplaces are served, the benefit of the laid off) reads the new
one from that period.

**PF2. Hours can be withheld, and withheld hours can be filled.** Each household's place at a
workplace carries the share of its hours there that it withholds in a period, as the August
design has it. Hours withheld are not on the clock, are not paid and make nothing. The people stay
on the rolls. This is the only thing a stoppage writes: output, shortage, prices and profit follow
in the circuit (A22). From the period after a stoppage begins the employer may fill withheld hours
with people who look for work, above its rolls: that is a second order. A struck workplace is not
a stopped one: the stop rule, the resting price and the owner's restart do not act on hours that
are withheld.

**PF3. Organizations hold money, and dues, strike pay and relief are payments of the circuit.** A
world that asks for the fight opens, at its opening, an account for each organization it asks
for and the accounts it keeps for organizations founded in play (PF18); none is made later.
*Dues* are a share of a member household's pay, reckoned on its pay net of the tax to come, taken
when wages are paid, and go to its organization's fund. *Strike pay* goes from a fund to a member
household for hours it withholds. *Relief* goes from a fund to any household, for nothing in
return. All of it is conserved money, drawn in gold like any payment and read by the hours ledger
as support, never as pay for work (ADR322, H1). A world that does not ask keeps its bytes.

**PF4. Employers move pay by A15, behind a switch of its own.** A cut when the workplace loses
money and people who look for work could replace its own; a raise when it cannot fill its hours;
the benefit as the floor. It is built last, from the prototype of round eight, and measured again
on today's engine. The fight does not need it: a squeeze with pay standing still is enough.

### The weighings

"A household" is the unit that holds one purse and decides: a household of the small world, a
household group of the country (ADR317, R4). It decides for each workplace where it has people.
One year, thirteen periods, is the horizon on both sides, as approved. No rule compares anything
with a constant that sets where the answer turns.

**PF5. A household decides each period whether its people at a workplace stop.** It reads its own
accounts and what has been put to it, and nothing else: not the owners' accounts, not what other
households mean to do, not who might take its places. It stops, or stays out, while all three
hold.

1. *There is something to stop for.* An organization with members among the workers there has put
   a call to the people of that workplace and written what is asked. Or, with no call, the
   household's own measure holds (PF14, a or b), and then what is asked is what would end it: the
   cut withdrawn, or the fewest steps of pay that make a period's pay cover its needs again.
2. *It can go without.* It has money of its own, or a fund pays it; A16 has a side give way "when
   its fund and its members' money run out". Or it goes without already, at full work: its pay no
   longer covers its needs.
3. *The fight is still worth it.* What stoppages there have cost it within the last year, and
   would cost it this period, is less than a year of what is asked on its own hours, both in its
   own dollars. The cost is the pay it gives up, less what funds pay it.

It stops for all its hours there. It decides again every period. It goes back when a condition
fails or when what is asked is offered (PF7). People of its own whose places are taken (PF13) are
from then among its people who look for work; for the rest it decides as before.

**PF6. A household decides each period whether its people who look for work take struck work.**
It takes work that is offered when both hold: without it the household goes without, and the work
would leave it more to live on this period than it has without it. What it has without it counts
in full: the benefit, which a person keeps while refusing struck work (PF13), relief from any
fund, the pay of its other people. A household does not take work its own people have stopped. A
household that takes work its own organization's people have stopped leaves that organization. In
one period a household decides first for its people at work, workplace by workplace, and then for
its people who look for work.

*The same demand.* Where what is asked names a household's people who look for work, to be taken
on (PF12), that household is in the fight like the people inside, and decides by PF5: it refuses
the struck work while it can go without and the fight is still worth it. What is asked on its own
hours is a year of the pay of the places named for it. What the fight costs it is the pay of the
struck work it passes up, less what funds pay it. A household with people inside and people named
adds both asks and both costs, and decides once for that workplace.

**PF7. The employer decides each period, by A16.** The employer is the company that owns the
struck workplace or, where there is none, as in the small worlds, its owners. From the period
after a stoppage begins it offers the withheld hours to the place's people who look for work, and
keeps whoever comes. It gives way when stoppages there have cost it, within the last year, more
than a year of what is asked for the workplace: the rise, on the hours the workplace worked in
the period before the stoppage, and the pay of any people it is asked to take on. The cost of a
struck period is read from its receipts: what all its workplaces paid it for a period, on the mean
of the last thirteen periods in which no hours were withheld there (or of the periods there are),
less what they pay it in the struck period, and never less than nothing. Giving way is an offer of
all that is asked. Both counts, the employer's and each household's, run over the last year and
start again when what is asked is won: an offer accepted, or the people gone back to one.

**What follows from PF5 to PF7.** A fight is a race between two sets of accounts. Each period out
costs the people their pay, less what funds pay them, and costs the owners what the work earned
them. Whoever's loss, within a year, first passes a year of what is asked gives way.

- Over a year, each side gives way only if a period out costs it more than a period of what is
  asked. An employer that loses less a period than is asked of it a period never gives way. The
  forecast shows this test.
- Periods are whole. With no fund, people who ask for one step win only if the first period costs
  the owners a year of that step.
- Strike pay and relief do not start a fight. They lengthen it, and a longer fight is one the
  owners can lose. A fund that pays people as much as their pay keeps them out for as long as it
  lasts, whoever is winning.
- People with no organization walk out blind. They do not know what the owners can bear or who
  will take their places. Organizations do (PF16). That is why a fight seldom wins without one.
  A walkout that fails is not repeated at once: what it cost still counts for a year, on both
  sides.
- There are two ways to hold the people who look for work. Pay them: to hold a household costs a
  fund what the struck work would have added to it, or what it lacks for its needs, whichever is
  less. Or ask for work for them: then they hold by their own weighing, the employer has their pay
  to lose as well, and the people inside need strike pay to last. A card with nothing paid and
  nothing asked holds nobody who goes without.
- The employer's count is over all its workplaces. A stoppage that leaves its other workplaces
  without power or food costs it those too.
- An employer that was paid nothing in the year before loses nothing by a stoppage and never gives
  way. So a cut made by a workplace that loses money cannot be answered by a stoppage in this
  economy, until a struck workplace has costs that run on: rent and interest, with the banks.

**PF8. Membership is each household's own decision.** A household joins an organization that has
invited it when what membership would bring it within a year is more than a year of its dues. It
leaves when a year of its dues is more than what the organization has brought it within the last
year. What an organization brings a household is counted from receipts: what its fund paid the
household; the rise of a contract it negotiated, on the household's own hours; and the pay of a
place that its open demand names for the household, which an invitation may offer. A household
with no pay owes no dues. A household that takes work the organization's people have stopped
leaves it. No rule writes a membership for a household.

### The order inside a period

Every decision takes effect in the next period (the Constitution, Article III; the August design,
section 5). For a period N:

1. *Close of N.* The engine closes the period. The measures and the news are read from its
   receipts. Each employer with hours withheld in N applies PF7: it gives way, or it offers the
   withheld hours for N+1. A contract's dates fall here. A fund kept for three periods running
   founds its organization here (PF18).
2. *Organizations decide for N+1*, in the order of their ids: accept an offer, call or withdraw,
   take up a stoppage, whom to pay, whom to invite. They read N's receipts and what they know.
   Yours decides here: the scene waits at an event.
3. *Households decide for N+1*, each from what is committed by then: stop, stay out or go back
   (PF5); take struck work (PF6); join or leave (PF8); pay into a fund (PF18). No household reads
   another's decision of this step.
4. *N+1 runs with these orders.* Funds pay at its start, as the benefit is paid. Withheld hours
   are off the clock. Dues and payments into a fund are taken when wages are paid.

So an offer made at the close of N is weighed before N+1, and the new rate holds from N+1: no
period is lost after an offer. And a stoppage has one period before anyone can take its places.

### Organizations, the win and the loss

**PF9. What a stoppage leaves.** As August has it, a practice leaves competence, repertoire, trust
evidence and ties, and no rule writes membership, solidarity or victory. So a stoppage leaves:

- its record: who stopped, period by period, what each weighed, who paid whom, who took whose
  place, how it ended;
- ties, in August's sense: between an organization and each household it paid or called, read
  from the second period after. People who only stopped at the same workplace are not tied by
  that;
- the members who joined while it ran (PF8).

No organization is founded by a stoppage. The people of a fight can join an organization that paid
them and invited them; with members there, that organization can call there the next time. Or
they can found one of their own, by keeping something up after it (PF18). This is how July's
ruling 13 is kept: what a stoppage leaves behind is organization, as members, funds, ties, a
record, and what its people go on to found.

**PF10. What an organization may do, and what it costs.** Where it has members among a workplace's
workers it may *call*, which puts a stoppage before every household with people there; *negotiate*,
which writes what is asked as the organization's own record and accepts an offer; *take up* a
stoppage that its members began with no call, which makes their ask its own, with the terms its
rule names; and *withdraw* its call. What is asked may be a rise, terms of a contract, and work
for its own people who look for work in the place (PF12). The call itself carries no figure
(ADR234): the household's weighing reads the record of what is asked as a fact. Anywhere, it may
pay relief to any household, pay strike pay to its members who are out, and invite households to
join. It cannot make anyone stop, go back or join, and it cannot call where it has no members
(ADR237). So an organization from outside begins by paying. That is how a union moves in, and how
yours does.

A call costs what it commits: strike pay, by the organization's own rule, to every member who
stops, each period, until they go back or the call is withdrawn. One demand of an organization is
open at a workplace at a time. In the real game a call also costs its organizers' hours. Dues
follow from members' pay and are never an act (ADR187). A union, a rival and yours differ in their
rules of decision and their funds, not in what the economy lets them do.

**PF11. A win is a rate, or a contract with an end.** When the employer gives way, what was asked
is the rate from the next period, and the people who stopped go back to their places. People taken
on in the fight were above the rolls: they stay only while the workplace has hours for them.
Where an organization negotiated, the win is a contract between it and the employer: the pay, any
other terms (PF12) and a last period. It covers the workplace: everyone who works there is paid by
it, member or not. Until its last period the organization calls no stoppage there and pays no
strike pay there, and the employer does not cut. Bargaining opens before the last period, with
news and an event: the organization may write a new demand, and may call once the contract has
ended. If nobody asks for anything it is renewed as it stands. Where nobody negotiated, the win is
a rate and nothing more: nothing holds the employer to it.

A household may still stop against a contract that covers it, by PF5 with no call, when its
measure holds. No fund of the contract's organization pays it. That is the opening the Director
named.

**PF12. Terms a contract may carry beyond pay**: the three the Director saw, and the one ruled in
answer 8, whose floor on the rolls is the lead's (call 29). The Director strikes or adds terms.

- *Pay follows prices.* On set dates the contract's rate steps up by the fewest steps that make a
  period's pay cover a covered household's needs again at posted prices. Upward only.
- *The laid off taken back first.* It is already how a workplace hires: it calls back its own
  before it takes others. Under a contract the people whose places were taken in a fight are its
  own (PF13).
- *Dues collected by the employer.* The employer takes the dues of the organization's members from
  their pay and pays them to its fund. It makes nobody a member.
- *Work for the people who look for it.* The workplace takes on the people named, the
  organization's own who look for work in the place, at the contract's pay, from the period after
  it gives way. Until the contract's last period its rolls do not fall below the people on its
  rolls when it gave way and the people named. Hours it has no work for are paid, and are drawn
  apart as idle hours on the clock (ADR317, R17). Its wages are paid from its money as always. A
  workplace with no work for the people named runs at a loss; when its money is gone the floor
  gives way to the stop rule, which lays off in its own order. The forecast shows what the people
  named would cost a period beside what the workplace earns its owners.

**PF13. A loss, by the state's law.** A stoppage is lost when the people go back with nothing
offered, or their places are taken. What follows is the law as it stands today: a row of law that
struggle can change, as the homes design treats the law's steps. The people taken on keep the
work. The people who stopped are not dismissed: those whose places are taken join the workplace's
own laid off and are called back first as places open. Where fewer come than there are struck
places, places are taken in the order in which the engine lays off (ADR302: first from the
households with the most at work). For this case only, the people who stopped take the place of
that order's choice of whom to lay off. While a fight lasts the people out draw no benefit
(Michigan, MCL 421.29(8)). A person on the benefit may refuse struck work and keep it (26 U.S.C.
3304(a)(5)). When the fight is over, those not taken back are laid off like anyone else. People
named in a demand that is lost are still looking for work. Left out: a stoppage against an
employer's unlawful act, where the law lets nobody's place be taken for good.

### Founding

**PF18. People who made a fight together found an organization by keeping a fund after it.** One
stoppage is not enough: "something must be kept up after it". What is kept up is one act, paying
into a fund in common, period after period; August's four conditions are read from it, and each
household decides for itself. The runner's founding rule is the only place an organization is
made, as the scene's stand-in for August's founding resolver.

- *Who.* The households that took part in a stoppage at a workplace, by withholding hours there or
  by holding out under its demand, and that belong to no organization when they pay.
- *A function kept up.* From the period after the stoppage has ended, each of them may pay into a
  fund in common, period by period, at the rate of dues. The fund has one rule, which is what it
  is for: it pays the households that keep it strike pay when they are out and relief when they
  are laid off, from its first period.
- *Accountability.* Every payment into the fund and out of it is a record that each of its payers
  can read.
- *Resources committed.* What a household has paid in is the fund's, not its own.
- *A decision in common that lasts.* The fund and its rule are held by its payers together, and
  outlast the fight that began them.

A household pays in by its own weighing, in the form of PF8: when what such a fund would have
paid it in the last year, strike pay for its periods out and relief for its periods laid off, is
more than a year of paying in. Who paid it in that year does not enter: a household that your
fund carried through the fight weighs the same as one that nobody paid.

When the fund has been paid into for three periods running, the households that paid in each of
them have founded an organization. Its members are those households, its fund is theirs, and it
acts from the next period. What a household that missed a period paid in goes back to it. If a
period passes in which nobody pays in, there is no organization: what was paid in goes back to its
payers, in the shares they paid, read from the receipts. The three periods are a Designed window,
shown on the screen. Nothing stores a stage: the rule reads the receipts of the last three
periods.

- An organization founded so decides for itself, as a union does, by A16 (PF16). It is not yours,
  though you paid. You cannot found it and you cannot stop it. While a fight lasts you can invite
  its people into yours instead: a household that has joined an organization founds no other.
- In the scene the runner gives a founded organization A16's rule at once. August has a new
  organization wait until its people commit who may propose for it (its section 7); the bridge
  adds that step.
- A tie runs from each payer to the fund, and then to the organization, as provision. No tie runs
  between payers unless a receipt shows that one paid or told another.
- On the bench a workplace has one worker household, with any household whose people moved there
  or were named in its demand. So there "a local of the farm" is often one household. The rule is
  written for the country, where a workplace has many.
- The accounts kept for founding are a ceiling of the build: twice the world's workplaces, a
  Designed figure, raised if any counted run comes near it. One fund more is a fault of the
  build: the run stops and says so. It is never news and never a rule of the world. A dead
  organization's account is not used again in the same run.
- An organization with no members and no money is dead, by arithmetic.

### What is shown, and who decides

**PF14. Four measures, read from receipts.** For the people of each workplace: (a) a period's pay
against the cost of their own needs at posted prices; (b) a cut of their pay; (c) hours the
workplace asked for and could not fill; (d) the employer's take growing while it lays people off
or cuts pay. With them, what the period's work paid the owners and what it paid the people. No
figure divides money by hours (ADR317, R1). A measure that begins is news. A measure does nothing
by itself. Measures (a) and (b) are also a household's own grounds to stop with no call (PF5);
measure (a) reads pay before dues. Measure (c) is news, and a union's occasion. Measure (d) is
news and a nudge: what would end it is work for the people let go, which an organization's demand
can ask (PF12).

**PF15. News, events and the forecast.** Every act of this design leaves a record with its cause
(A23): a call, what is asked, each household's decision and what it weighed, hours withheld,
strike pay, relief, struck work offered and taken, an offer, a contract, each step of pay under
it, a tie, a membership begun or ended. An event is news your organization could act on. It
carries a forecast: what a stoppage would cost the people a period and, at least, the owners; how
long each could hold; who looks for work in the place and what each household has to live on; what
holding them would cost your fund. "At least", because a forecast counts the struck workplace's
own take and the receipts will count all the employer's workplaces. In the scene every figure is
known. In the real game a forecast is made only from what your organization has earned (ADR182).
Nothing new moves on the screen outside the three beats of a period (ADR317, R13): a stoppage is
hours that do not go in, and what a fund pays is money that comes back.

**PF16. Who decides.**

- *You* decide what yours calls, asks, takes up and pays, and whom it invites, when you choose. A
  fight you cannot win is yours to start. Only yours asks for work for people who look for it.
- *A union* decides by A16. Its occasions are a cut, hands short (hours asked for and not filled),
  and the end of a contract when its members' pay no longer covers their needs. It calls, or takes
  up a stoppage its members began, only when, on the accounts it can read, its members would
  outlast the employer: at the struck workplace's own take of the year before, the owners' loss
  would pass a year of what is asked before its members' loss does; its fund covers the strike pay
  for that long; and nobody in the place who looks for work would take the places (PF6). It asks
  for the cut withdrawn, for one step where hands are short, and otherwise for the steps that make
  pay cover its members' needs. With pay it asks for the two terms it holds to: pay that follows
  prices, and dues collected by the employer. It asks for no work for anyone: that is "the demand
  a union that defends only its own members never makes". It pays its own members and nobody
  else: "These unions defend their own members' pay, not the class."
- *A union moves in* where its accounts say a fight can be won and nobody has members. It offers
  the people there membership and its strike pay. It ranks such workplaces by what a year of the
  fight would bring for each dollar of its fund and goes down the list while the fund lasts
  (ADR184, R3).
- *Workers with no organization* decide by PF5 alone, on their own accounts.
- *An organization founded in play* (PF18) decides as a union does, by A16. It is not yours.
- *Other organizations* are a place kept. Their rules of decision come with their lines.

**PF17. The payer.** After a win, from receipts:

- what the employer did in the six periods that followed: profit paid out, posted prices, hours
  asked for, people on the rolls, any stop or restart; in the country also work moved abroad and
  the price of its parts;
- who bought at a raised price, and what each of those households went without;
- what the winners get back, in hours: hours on the clock, hours back for work, and hours beyond
  the clock, which is the bribe as ruled (ADR322, H4). Each is a mean for one person on the clock
  over six periods: the six before the stoppage began, and the six after the win. The ledger
  counts over a span and not period by period, because pay for one period's hours can be spent in
  a later one.

The line under them reads the winners' side, as the Director ruled (answer 9), and it is split at
the clock. For the part of the change below the clock: "They get back less than they give. The win
took back 1 hour." For the part beyond it: "The bribe grew by 1 hour.", whoever paid. A win that
crosses the clock gets both sentences. "Give" is read as hours on the clock (ADR322, H4). Who paid
stands beside it as a fact: "Paid from: the owners' profit.", or the buyers, or work moved abroad.
The line names the largest of the three, read from the six periods' receipts, with each one's
amount under it.

No rule says where a strike can win. Whether anything is beyond the clock in the small world is
counted after each win, not assumed. The stored-hours design tests that no group's hours back for
work pass its hours on the clock; that test holds for runs with no fight and is amended for runs
with a win. These panels and the news carry what July's ruling 11 required of the screen: that the
player can read the result.

## The line

The game takes MIM's line as its own. Six places where this design meets it, each with MIM's own
words, for the Director to check. Sources and lines are in the notes of the build.

1. **A fight for pay is not the class struggle.** "Only the utterly garbage pseudo-Marxists with
   a very bad case of economism insist that the only valid class struggle is purely economic
   conflict" (MIM, January 2008). So this design gives a stoppage its material results and no
   others: pay, money, hours, a contract, ties. It writes no outlook, no solidarity and no victory.
2. **Why strikes win.** "the big labor unions could get bigger wins from striking because there
   weren't as many unemployed workers to be hired as replacements" (MIM Notes 225, 2001). PF6 and
   PF7 are that sentence as rules.
3. **What a win is.** MIM holds that "imperialist country workers are paid more than the value
   of their labor" (MIM Notes 129, 1997), and that the fight for their pay "leads unavoidably to
   reformism, a simple redistribution of super-profits" (MIM, 2008). A redistribution is one
   whichever pocket it left. The hours ledger reads it so: a group's hours back for work rise with
   its pay when it spends it on goods that reach it, and the count is shown, not assumed. No rule
   here says which workers are bribed. "Farmworkers in the U$ are among the few exploited
   professions in this country" (MIM Notes 134, 1997): the ledger shows it group by group, or it
   does not. The line the screen draws is ruled: by the winners' hours (answer 9).
4. **Who paid.** When a farm workers' union asked for a price rise to pay for a raise, "MIM asks
   why the proletariat should not demand that the imperialists take the money out of their own
   pockets to give to the workers" (MIM Notes 134, 1997). PF17 shows which it was: the price, and
   who bought at it, or the owners' pockets.
5. **The people who take struck work.** "Mexican "scabs" are workers too." (MIM, 1995-96). Here a
   household takes struck work because it would otherwise go without. No word on the screen calls
   it an enemy; the record says who took whose place and what each had to live on.
6. **What is asked.** "a strike worth actively supporting is a strike with political demands led
   by the proletariat" (MIM, 1996). In this build a stoppage asks for pay and for terms of a
   contract, nothing else. That is economism by construction, and it is the lead's cut, not a
   position: the small world has no war, no border and no nation for a demand to be about. What is
   asked is a record with kinds, so that other demands come with the country.

## What it does not do

- Police, courts, injunctions and guards at a gate. The state's answer comes with its own design
  (the August design, section 14). "Force at the gate" was offered and not picked.
- Nation, and where the people who look for work come from. No rule here reads a household's
  nation (ADR315, H2). In the country the people who can take struck work include people who come
  from elsewhere, and MIM puts the reserve army of the core abroad. That is the border's design.
- The lines of organizations: what a reformist union, a settler socialist group or a nationalist
  organization asks for, whom it takes in, where its money comes from and what that money does to
  it (ADR176, 14 and 38; ADR180, R8). A place is kept for each.
- What a win or a loss does to how people see the world. That is the design of outlook, which will
  read this design's record: who stopped, who paid, who took whose place, and the winners' hours.
  This design writes none of its results. "Tension", which July's ruling 11 named as a product of
  a strike, is not written by a stoppage here.
- Blockades, occupations, boycotts and rent strikes. Each has its own carrier (the August design,
  section 10; the homes design). A general strike is several stoppages seen together, not a
  button.
- A loan to a struck employer, and work moved abroad: with the banks and with capital that moves.
- Organizers as people who give hours. The real game has them; the small world does not.
- Fog. The scene shows everything.

## What would make it a failure

- A stoppage that a rule starts, ends or decides: accounts and each household's weighing do.
- A household that stops, goes back, takes work or joins because someone told it to.
- A number placed to set where a response turns. A fixed cut-off put in on purpose must make a
  test fail.
- Money or hours made or lost. A fund, dues, strike pay and relief are conserved every period.
- A choice with no cost, or one that is always best. Paying costs the fund every period; asking
  for more makes the fight longer for both sides; a call commits the fund.
- A win with no payer shown.
- A word on the screen that makes an enemy of a household that took struck work.
- A rule that says a strike in the core cannot win, or that it must.
- A shipped world, or a reference run of the bench, that changes a byte.

## Approaches

- *A stoppage as a labelled shock*, struck from the rail like the failed harvest. Cheap, and it
  would show what withheld hours do. Nobody decides in it and no accounts end it. Against answers
  1 and 5 and against August.
- *The fight in the real game's tick first.* The tick refuses this economy until the bridge is
  built, and the scene could not show it.
- *Chosen: the material acts in the engine, the decisions in the small worlds' runner, now.* The
  engine learns five acts, as orders. The runner holds the organizations and resolves each
  household's weighing, in the shapes the August contracts name (a proposal, an independent
  resolution for each group, an episode, a record). The bridge then carries them into the tick,
  where the strike proposal's admission already waits.

## How we know it works

- **Conservation.** Money and people are exact every period of every run, with funds, dues, strike
  pay and relief.
- **Nothing moves by itself.** With every new switch off, every reference run of the bench is
  equal byte for byte (the fourteen long tests). With organizations present and no measure
  holding, the calm world stays still.
- **Each weighing, shown for one member** (Amendment AM).

  | Rule | The member | The amounts it compares | The population | What differs between members |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | PF5 | a household with people at a workplace | its pay there, its money, what funds pay it, the cost of its needs, what stoppages there have cost it within the year, a year of what is asked on its hours | the households with people at that workplace | money held, pay of its other people, people at other workplaces, strike pay; later, rent or a loan on its home |
  | PF6 | a household with people who look for work | what it has to live on without the work, the cost of its needs, the pay of the struck work | the households of the place with people looking | the benefit and how long it has left, pay of its other people, money, relief |
  | PF6, the same demand | a household with people named in what is asked | the struck pay it passes up, what funds pay it, a year of the pay of the places named for it | the households named | how many of its people are named, people inside, money, relief |
  | PF8 | a household that was invited, or is a member | a year of its dues, what the organization would bring or has brought it within a year | the households invited; the members | pay, whether it is out, whether it looks for work, a contract over its hours |
  | PF18 | a household that took part in a stoppage and belongs to no organization | a year of paying in, what such a fund would have paid it in the last year | the households that took part | periods out, periods laid off, pay |

  PF7 is one employer's own accounts, not a response of a population. On the bench a workplace
  has one worker household, and any household whose people moved there or were named, so there the
  response of a workplace, and of a founding, is close to a switch. The test that removes the
  difference between households, and must change the response, runs on a test world with three
  households of unlike money at one workplace. A second test plants a fixed cut-off and must fail.
  In the country a household group of many households decides as one: differences inside a group
  do not enter these weighings.
- **Founding, four tests.** A stoppage alone founds nothing. Three periods of paying in found an
  organization of exactly the households that paid in each. A period in which nobody pays in ends
  it and pays back every dollar. One fund more than the world's kept accounts is refused, with
  its news.
- **Three counted comparisons, on 400 periods at both sets of figures**, from reports and
  receipts, never from a picture:
  1. *The reserve army.* The same squeeze with hands short, with people looking for work whom
     nobody pays, with them paid, and with work asked for them. Who stops, who takes whose place,
     who gives way, and what each way cost.
  2. *The later slump.* The run that decided the verdict of round ten, with no organization, with
     a union that pays only its own, and with the people who look for work paid. Does work come
     back, and who paid? If won rises do not end the slump on today's engine, the rows go to the
     Director before anything else is built on it.
  3. *The payer.* After each win: the six periods of the employer's receipts, the buyers, and the
     hours ledger, with the count of anything beyond the clock.
- **The lead's look**, at every new window, beside the sketch, shown to the Director.

## Calls made here, for the Director to check

1. Whether people stop is theirs. The player and a union decide the call, what is asked and what
   the fund pays. This is August, against a plain reading of "a player decision" and of A16's
   "its members give no hours".
2. A16 says a union starts "when its fund would outlast the employer's money". A struck
   workplace's money does not run down in this economy: it pays no wages, and there is no rent or
   interest yet. So the test is read with A16's own ending: its members would outlast the
   employer. With banks and rent, a struck employer's money will run down too.
3. The employer's cost is counted from its receipts, over all its workplaces, against the mean of
   the last thirteen periods with no hours withheld there. So the people of a utility can win
   though the utility earns little. A forecast counts only the struck workplace, and says "at
   least".
4. A workplace that loses money cannot be hurt by a stoppage here, so its cut cannot be answered
   by one (PF7). This is where A15 cuts. It changes with the banks.
5. A household's own test is the mirror of the employer's: it goes back when the fight has cost
   it more than a year of what is asked. A16 has only "its fund and its members' money run out".
   Both counts run over the last year and start again when what is asked is won. So, over a
   year, a side gives way only if a period out costs it more than a period of what is asked.
6. People with no organization stop on their own accounts and know nothing of the owners' or of
   who will take their places. So a stoppage can be broken: August's "Unknown facts remain
   unknown", and the Standard's "the reserve army breaks strikes".
7. A household that already goes without at full work may stop with no money (PF5, 2). On this
   bench workers hold no money, so without this nobody could walk out without a fund. Such a

   it more than it is worth.
8. People stop for the whole of a period's hours. August lets a group reduce; a share is stored,

## Build order

About thirty-two tasks: each piece of the engine or the runner with its piece of the scene, as the
six rules were built. After stored hours, before the bridge.

1. The orders of a period, as canonical input; organizations and the accounts kept for founding,
   in a world that asks; dues, strike pay and relief as payments; the scene reads them and draws
   them.
2. Pay changes at a boundary; shown for each workplace.
3. Hours withheld, through staffing and the close; the stop rule, the resting price and the
   restart leave struck hours alone; the people at the gate.
4. Withheld hours filled above the rolls; whose places are taken; the order of a loss; what
   happens to the people taken on after a win.
5. The measures and the news.
6. The household's weighing and the ask with no call; the weighing of the people who look for
   work; the two tests of Amendment AM.
7. The employer's rule, the offer, both counts over the last year.
8. The order inside a period; organizations as actors; yours: pay, invite, call, negotiate, take
   up; the event and the forecast.
9. Membership, joining and leaving; ties and the record.
10. Founding: the fund kept up, the organization founded, the money sent back; its four tests;
    the thinner ring.
11. The contract, its calendar and its terms: pay follows prices, dues collected.
12. The demand for work: named people taken on, the floor on the rolls and its yielding to the
    stop rule, idle hours paid, the weighing of the people named.
13. A union present, by A16; a union moving in; "ask me".
14. The payer: the employer's receipts, the buyers, the hours ledger over two spans, and the line
    under a win.
15. The three counted comparisons, the lead's look and the page for the Director.
16. A15 behind its switch, measured again.
