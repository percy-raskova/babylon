# What brings the small economy back: round two

Measured 21 November 2026 in the same small world as round one: four workplaces (utility, farm, clinic, shop)
in one place, 40 people, 240 hours of work a period, in two worlds, "plain" (no taxes) and "fund" (20% taxes
and a benefits fund). Round two adds four rules, h, i, j and k, six sets of rules together, and runs of 200
periods beside the 100 of round one. Every figure here is read from a run's summary or its rows. Where an
agent's account and the rows differ, the rows are used and the difference is named.

The pushes are round one's: **P2** a poor harvest (9 food a batch, not 10), **P4** higher pay at the clinic
($11 an hour, not $10), **P5** a cut in the fund's tax rates (fund world only). **Quiet**, **P1** and **P3**
are the calm runs. Unmet is food/utility/care, out of 40 each. "Holds a band" means hours swing inside a band
wider than 2 hours with no trend. "Still moving" means a run that has not come to rest at its last period.
Neither is called settled.

## 1. The answer

- **Nothing measured brings every slump fully back.** No rule alone, from either round, does it, and no set
  brings either poor harvest back to its target (240 hours, 4.44 care unmet).
- **The best set run for 200 periods is f at a half share + d2** (set 6): a workplace with spare cash plans
  for half of what buyers asked and could not pay for, and owners top up a workplace short of cash for its
  wages and inputs. It holds every run for 200 periods. Fund P4 and fund P5 come back to target. Both P2s and
  plain P4 hold bands 4-9 hours short on average (228-238 hours). No workplace closes, the calm runs stay
  exactly still, and no money is lost.
- **Whether rule a (lent workers) is needed is open.** Round one's f + a + d2 ran for 100 periods only. Over
  p81-100, dropping a narrows the poor-harvest bands (plain P2 228-234 against 209-240, fund P2 230-235
  against 226-240) and cuts top-ups (plain P2 $322 against $1,409), but costs about 4 hours in plain P4
  (235.6 against 240.0) and fund P2 (233.4 against 237.8); plain P2 gains 3.85 hours.
- **Order to build: f, then d2.** f alone wrecks the fund world's poor harvest (round one: the shop closes
  from p65); d2 refills the few shortfalls f opens. Then measure f + d2 again at the national figures (price
  steps of 2.5%, two periods of working cash), and f + a + d2 for 200 periods beside it, before a is ruled
  out. Neither run was made. Do not build h, i, j or k as cures.
- **What it costs: the owner.** Over p181-200 the owner holds $154-319 in both P2s (baseline plain P2 over
  p81-100: $1,823-2,037), goes without all its care in both P2s and plain P4, and carries most of the unmet
  food and utility (4 food and 6-9 utility in both P2s). Owners' top-ups are $76-488 a run over 200 periods.
- **How sure:** the small world repeats exactly, and every set reproduces its parts when a rule is switched
  off. But 200 periods turned three of the baseline's 100-period verdicts into collapse, so a band at p200 is
  not proof of rest.

The first 100 periods of each 200-period run equal the 100-period run, row for row. Quiet, P1 and P3 stay
exactly still for 200 periods in both worlds. **Run long enough, every push ruins both worlds.** Fund P4, near
target at p100, loses its shop and farm at p177-178.

**The cause, in plain words** (found in round one; no round-two rule removed it):

1. A workplace's plan can only go down. When buyers want more than the plan, the price steps up and some go
   without. When they want less, the plan is cut. A supplier plans from last period's demand, so a plan that
   rises downstream meets short inputs a period later and pays idle hours.
2. Pay follows the plan hour by hour, so every cut in a plan is at once a cut in what households can spend.
3. A worker household's pay is exactly the cost of its needs, so one price step makes it drop a unit of the
   last thing it buys, care.
4. A price moves a whole dollar a step: 8.3% of the utility's price, 2.6% of the shop's. The national scenario
   steps 2.5% of each opening price.
5. A workplace opens with one period of working cash; the national scenario opens with two.

Round two found a sixth. **6. The shop restocks to a fixed 40 food.** Any extra food wanted (a richer owner,
benefits paid in full) leaves the food market a unit short, and the shop's price climbs a step nearly every
period. In the P2 runs of sets 1-5 it is $62-114 over p81-100 and $102-193 over p181-200, against $38 at the
opening.

## 5. What each recommended rule needs to become real

Only f and d2 are recommended, and only once they are measured again at the national figures. Rule a is not
recommended and not ruled out: it waits on f + a + d2 run for 200 periods.

- **f (plan for part of the unpaid asking, from spare cash).** *Remember:* a share and a cash reserve per
  workplace, as a policy row, in place of the spike's `SPIKE_F_SHARE` in `src/recurring/firms.rs` (with
  changes in `model.rs`, `recurring.rs` and `transition/state.rs`). *Record:* the extension on each plan's
  receipt (the spike reports it only as `flow_f_extra_units_<site>` and `flow_f_extra_cost_<site>`). Unpaid
  asking must count want tiers, and the utility must see the clinic's extension in the same period. *The
  player sees:* a business with money in the bank keeping staff on through a slump, and a care price that
  falls.
- **d2 (owners put in working cash).** *Remember:* a policy row (owner, workplace, periods of outlay), capped
  at the hours a workplace can staff, and a way to pay it back. *Record:* a capital contribution receipt;
  today it lives only in the test harness (`tests/small_world/support.rs`, flows `flow_contrib_paid` and
  `flow_contrib_paid_farm`). *The player sees:* owners putting money into their businesses and spending less
  on themselves.
- **i and j are not rules** but national figures already in `content/scenarios/national/defines.toml`
  (`price_step_micros` at 2.5% of each opening price, `working_capital_periods = 2`). The small world's
  opening (`tests/small_world/support.rs`) should take them before f + d2 is measured again.
- **k, only if f fails at the national figures.** *Remember:* last period's demand per market and handling per
  merchant, on the wire and in the state digest (the spike's `DemandMemory` in `src/recurring/model.rs` is
  held in memory only). *Record:* the remembered figure and what it added, on the plan receipt. *The player
  sees:* a business riding out one bad period.

## 6. What NOT to add, over both rounds

- **h, pay the whole job:** closes the clinic from p15-16 in both P2s (baseline p74, or never); $8,440-12,990
  of pay beyond the plan over 100 periods makes nothing.
- **k alone:** runs fund P4 down to 40 hours by p100 (baseline 238) and closes the fund P2 clinic from p11.
- **i or j alone:** at 2.5% every push ends in ruin by p200; j only delays the drains and adds $4,360 of new
  money.
- **h with k (set 4):** both spend the same cash on idle hours; fund P4 falls from 240 at p100 to 186 at p200.
- **a, lent workers, without f + d2:** alone (round one) the farm closes from p16 in both P2s; in set 5 the
  farm closes in three of five pushes by p200, paying borrowed hours idle while power comes late. With f + d2
  it is not ruled out (section 1).
- **b, pay that rises when staff are short:** fund P4 runs away to 116 hours by p100 (baseline 238).
- **d1, spare cash to the owner:** both quiet runs collapse (40 and 92 hours at p100).
- **g, a deeper fund reserve:** lowers every pushed fund run (fund P4 averages 178.80 against 236.45).
- **c, idle wages in the price:** fund P4's shop closes from p78 and its farm from p80.
- **e as written** moves both quiet runs; **e-kept** undoes f's plain P2 recovery (the clinic closes from
  p63).
- **f alone:** the fund world's poor harvest closes its shop (p65) and farm (p66).

## 7. What is still unexplained, and what was not measured

- **f + d2 at the national figures was not run.** With two periods of cash, f's "cash above one period" test
  would act from p1. This is the next run to make; if it fails, f + d2 + k at the national figures is the one
  after.
- **f + a + d2 for 200 periods was not run.** It leads set 6 by about 4 hours in plain P4 and fund P2 at p100,
  but swings wider in both P2s (still moving); whether it holds to p200 is unknown, so a stays open.
- **f + d2 beyond 200 periods.** Its bands held from p100 to p200, but the baseline's fund P4 held 234-239
  until about p170 and then collapsed. 300 or more periods were not run.
- **The poor harvest's lost output.** No rule closes the P2 gap: the farm's people make 36 food however it
  plans, its stock drains, and the utility plans a period late. Lent hands reached the farm and drained it. A
  farm that can plan its power a period ahead, or buy it elsewhere, was not measured.
- **Only one setting of each rule was combined** (f 50%, i 2.5%, j two periods, k on). f's share did not act
  steadily in round one (plain P4 ran away at 10% and held at 50%); whether set 6 rests on the half share was
  not tested.
- **The owner's lost care.** In set 6 the owner's money ends as workers' pay. A top-up that is paid back was
  not run.
- **The shop's fixed restock (cause 6)** was found in round two; no rule aimed at it was built.
