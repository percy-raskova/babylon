## 1. Methods of leadership and work (Mao's corpus: from-the-masses-to-the-masses as an allocation loop; grasp the principal contradiction = the central-task discipline; party-committee methods)

### 1.1 The loop is an allocation loop, not a communication style

The mass line as stated is **a four-phase cycle that consumes and produces information, and
spends labor in between**:

> "take the ideas of the masses (scattered and unsystematic ideas) and concentrate them...
> then go to the masses and propagate and explain these ideas until the masses embrace them
> as their own... and test the correctness of these ideas in such action. Then once again
> concentrate ideas from the masses and once again go to the masses... **in an endless
> spiral**."
> — quoted in `MW/reference/archive/mao/selected-works/volume-8/mswv8_64.htm`

The load-bearing fact for us is the *direction of the first arrow*. Correct decisions are an
**output of information throughput from below**, not an independent attribute of the leading
body. Mao says this flatly about the Central Committee itself:

> "The reason why the leadership of the Central Committee is correct is chiefly that it
> **synthesizes the material, reports and correct views coming from different localities**.
> It would be difficult for the Central Committee to issue correct orders if the localities
> did not provide material and put forward opinions."
> — `MW/.../volume-4/mswv4_59.htm` (*Methods of Work of Party Committees*, 13 Mar 1949)

Same essay: "Be a pupil before you become a teacher; learn from the cadres at the lower levels
before you issue orders." Mistaken views from below are still worth ingesting — they are
critiqued, not acted on, but they are *heard*.

### 1.2 The central-task discipline: "playing the piano"

The single most transferable mechanic in the Mao corpus is the rule against **both** dispersion
**and** monomania:

> "A Party committee should keep a firm grasp on its central task and at the same time, around
> the central task, it should unfold the work in other fields... Wherever there is a problem,
> we must put our finger on it, and this is a method we must master."
> — `MW/.../volume-4/mswv4_59.htm`

The piano image is explicit: pressing all ten fingers at once produces no melody; pressing one
produces no music either. One designated principal task carries priority weight; secondary
fields get **nonzero but lesser** allocation. Never zero, never equal. This is the
principal-contradiction doctrine cashed out as a *budget rule* rather than a slogan.

Restated as a hard threshold in the same text — a task worked at slack intensity is a task not
worked at all:

> "One can get a grip on something only when it is **grasped firmly, without the slightest
> slackening**... It will not do to have no grasp at all, nor will it do if the grasp is not
> firm."

The 1949 essay also supplies the *shape of the leading body itself*: the secretary is a
"squad leader" of a committee of ten to twenty, whose job is to make each member able to act,
not to act for them — "If the 'squad members' do not march in step, they can never expect to
lead tens of millions of people" — with decisions taken minority-to-majority, not by fiat.

### 1.3 "Have a head for figures" — investigation as a precondition of policy

Mao makes quantitative situational knowledge a **gate on acting**, not an optional nicety:

> "In all mass movements we must make a basic investigation and analysis of the **number of
> active supporters, opponents and neutrals** and must not decide problems subjectively and
> without basis."
> — `MW/.../volume-4/mswv4_59.htm`

Land policy required knowing the exact class percentages first. This is the direct historical
ancestor of the matrix's `investigate` cell: intelligence is not a side activity, it is the
**precondition** of every major commitment.

Scaling that investigation without drowning in it has its own named method — sample, don't
census:

> "You don't have to catch all the sparrows and dissect them before you can prove the fact that
> 'small as it is, the sparrow has all the vital organs'... Once you are clear about a few
> co-operatives, you will be able to draw proper conclusions."
> — `MW/.../volume-5/mswv5_46.htm` (1955)

And the substitute for direct span-of-control is a **tiered written bulletin relay**: county
committee → prefectural → provincial → Central Committee, each level reporting counts and
problems upward, so an upper tier stays informed without touching every unit below it. The same
text carries a blunt personnel-fit rule: a cadre who "simply can't dig in" to the technical
substance of the work is to be reassigned, not left in post.

### 1.4 Overhead is real and it is itemized

*Methods of Work of Party Committees* reads like a cost-control memo. Four rules, all of which
are overhead taxes on the same labor pool:

- **Preparation cost.** "Don't call a meeting in a hurry if the preparations are not
  completed." Meetings without a circulated report/draft resolution burn attendance without
  producing decisions.
- **Concision as policy.** "'Fewer and better troops and simpler administration.' Talks,
  speeches, articles and resolutions should all be concise... Meetings also should not go on
  too long."
- **Problems on the table.** "If problems exist and are not placed on the table, they will
  remain unsolved for a long time and even drag on for years."
- **Lateral exchange.** Siloed cadres who never compare notes lose a shared vocabulary and
  eventually cannot communicate on basic theory at all.

The 1959 internal memo *Sixteen Articles Concerning Work Methods*
(`MW/.../volume-8/mswv8_32.htm` — marked in-document "not an official document; intended for
reference only", so **lower textual authority** than the Selected Works pieces) makes the
resource constraint explicit:

> "Formulate plans according to the **amount of materials and the number of people**, not
> according to subjectivity."

plus a slack rule ("Make Allowances" — set targets with margin, as one assembles several times
the enemy's numbers before committing to battle), a cyclicality rule ("Proceed in Wave-like
Motion" — output targets saddle up and down, they do not escalate linearly), a repeat of the
meeting-preparation rule ("Work meetings should not be held without an airing"), and an
anti-blockage rule for upward reports ("There should be a point of view in a report. Suggest
several alternate measures... Let there be no blockade").

### 1.5 Cadres as a decisive, *managed* factor

From the Sixth Plenum, 1938 (`MW/.../volume-2/mswv2_10.htm`):

> "**Cadres are a decisive factor, once the political line is determined.**"
> "...leadership involves two main responsibilities: to work out ideas, and to **use cadres
> well**."

Five named levers of cadre care, each a distinct expenditure: (1) give guidance — freedom to
act plus timely instruction; (2) raise their level — study and education; (3) check up on their
work, routinely, not only after a serious mistake; (4) persuasion first, with "struggle" and
opportunist-labelling reserved for serious repeat offenders; (5) **material help with personal
difficulty** — illness, poverty, domestic trouble. Against this sits the rejected axis:
"appoint on merit" versus "appoint by favoritism", with Chang Kuo-tao's clique-building named
as the cautionary case — favoritism builds a network that eventually defects.

Discipline is a four-point hierarchy — individual→organization, minority→majority,
lower→higher, membership→Central Committee — but the same text pairs it with a counter-
principle, that hierarchy without initiative from below is dead weight: "Otherwise,
'initiative' will be an empty thing."

### 1.6 Labor as substitute for budget: "Get Organized!"

The 1943 Border Region speech (`MW/.../volume-3/mswv3_17.htm`) is the clearest historical case
of **converting labor into material output to relieve a cash constraint** — the mechanism the
funding dossier's channels do *not* cover:

> "Every soldier needs to spend only **three months of the year in production** and can devote
> the remaining nine months to training and fighting. Our troops depend for their pay neither
> on the Kuomintang government, nor on the Border Region Government, nor on the people, but can
> fully provide for themselves."

18 mou per soldier; organizations and schools grew 100% of their own vegetables (up from 50%
the prior year) and ran small workshops. Self-provisioning **cut the extraction burden on the
civilian base** — a budget solved by labor rather than by taxing PRESENCE.

The named failure mode is precise and is a pure allocation error:

> "It is one that lacks the mass viewpoint, fails to rely on the masses or organize them, and
> gives **exclusive attention to organizing the small number of people working in the
> financial, supply or trading organizations**, while paying no attention to organizing the
> masses in the villages..."

The base unit of that organizing is voluntary and bottom-up — "labour-exchange teams" and
mutual-aid groups of a few to a few hundred, temporary or permanent, contributing manpower,
animal power or implements, in four named co-op types (agricultural mutual-aid, multi-purpose,
transport, handicraft):

> "So long as they are collective mutual-aid organizations which the people join **voluntarily
> (compulsion must never be used)**, all of them are good, no matter how they are named...
> These methods of collective mutual aid are the inventions of the masses themselves."

Leadership's role in that sentence is to *study and generalize* forms the masses invented, not
to impose them — the same loop as §1.1, in the material register.

### 1.7 The grievance backlog as a corruption sensor

From the 1953 inner-Party directive against bureaucracy
(`MW/.../volume-5/mswv5_24.htm`) — "methods of leadership" defined operationally as: state the
policy limits and proper work-style when giving an assignment, instruct rather than merely
order, and **examine cadres at each level periodically** rather than intervening only after a
violation surfaces. The diagnostic signal is beautifully concrete:

> "a certain provincial people's government has sat on **well over seventy thousand letters**"

Unread grievances are the accumulator. The resolution is two-sided: expose and punish typical
cases in the press, *and* actively surface and praise positive models, "so that what is upright
will prevail over what is evil."

## 2. The organizational codifications (the CPUSA manual's structures: nuclei/fractions/unit division of labor; EROL's party-building practice and its failure modes)

### 2.1 J. Peters' *Manual on Organisation* (1935) — the densest structural source

Only **Chapter 3** ("Structure and Functions of the Party Organizations") is present on this
mirror, at
`MW/history/usa/parties/cpusa/1935/07/organisers-manual/ch03.htm` (~1480 lines). It links
ch04 ("Party Membership and Cadres"); no other chapter file exists locally. Chapter 3 alone
carries most of what follows.

**The Shop Unit is defined by workplace, not residence, with a formation threshold and a
size cap:**

> "Shop Units should be organized in every factory, shop, mine, etc., where there are **three
> or more members** of the Party."
> "The Shop Unit which grows to over **10-12 members** should be divided into two independent
> working groups as quickly as possible."

Mitosis is explicitly a *security* measure — a large unit is a fat target for a company spy —
traded against reach.

**The unit's labor is split into five named roles**, each with an enumerated task list and a
distinct output cadence: Unit Organizer (political direction, real-time reaction to shop-floor
issues, reporting upward); Agit-Prop Director (discussion material, editorial board of the shop
paper); Financial Secretary (dues tracking, delinquency follow-up, and — note the security
crossover — custody of the membership list, "so that agents of the class enemy do not get hold
of it"); Daily Worker Agent (circulation); Literature Director (distribution plus strict weekly
accounting of literature funds owed to the Section).

**Below the unit: the Group System**, a pure logistics layer for street/town units too large to
convene safely. Groups of 4-6 by residential proximity, under a **captain who is appointed, not
elected**, whose remit is dues collection, attendance chasing and rapid mobilization relay.
The manual is emphatic that this layer has no politics of its own — "Have Groups Any
Independent Function in the Unit? **No.**"

**Fractions** are how a small cadre converts a large mass organization into controllable
output. Wherever 3+ Communists sit in a union, club or committee they caucus *before* the mass
meeting, vote internally, and present a single public position:

> "No Party member has the right to speak or act in the union or other mass organization
> against the decisions of the Fraction... The Party members must always act as a solid unit."
> "If certain members of the Fraction do not agree with the decision of the majority, they can
> bring the problem to the Party committee... but this appeal **cannot keep back the minority
> from carrying out the decision**."

Fractions nest under whichever Party body matches the mass organization's territorial scope —
unit, section, district, Central Committee.

**Concentration** is the manual's explicit anti-dispersion doctrine — name one strategic
factory and throw disproportionate cadre, literature and gate-meetings at it, including loaning
1-2 trusted outside members into a nascent 3-person shop unit, with a hard constraint:

> "It is absolutely essential that **outside members (from Street Units) be always in the
> minority** in the Shop Unit."

**The shop paper** is edited, financed, printed and distributed *from inside the plant* by unit
members plus recruited non-Party workers — never merely subsidized from outside:

> "The workers will react more favorably to the paper if they get it from the inside...
> **We have to train ourselves, train our forces, inside the factories, today, for this work**"

— i.e. inside-distribution is simultaneously a legitimacy play and a **rehearsal for
conditions in which outside distribution becomes impossible**. Clandestinity readiness is
built as a habit during legality, not improvised under repression.

### 2.2 The census precondition (Cannon, 1924)

Before the Comintern-mandated conversion from territorial branches to shop nuclei, the CPUSA
ordered a mandatory nationwide **industrial registration** — every branch secretary recording
where every member actually works:

> "For a modern Communist party, a complete and thorough knowledge of the location of its
> forces is indispensable... the Party must know who its members are, where they work, what
> organizations they belong to... **Without this information, the Party is stumbling in the
> dark.**"
> "Our Party cannot even take one step in this direction until it has registered the Party
> membership."
> — `MW/history/usa/parties/cpusa/1924/0725-cannon-industrialregist.pdf`

The build-order implication is sharp: **workplace-targeted verbs are unavailable, or blind,
until a census has been paid for.**

### 2.3 The single best efficiency datum found: the 1930 language-work audit

An internal *Party Organizer* audit tabulated, federation by federation across 16 ethnic mass
organizations, embedded Party members against total membership, split into Party-controlled /
Party-influenced / enemy-led tiers:

> "The factions, directed by 16 bureaus and numbering almost **5,000 Party members, control
> organizations having about 50,000 members**. About **800 Party members work among 140,000
> workers** in organizations in which we have influence. Organizations consisting of **over a
> million members... have no Party members and no influence**."
> "In these small organizations the Party members do not think it necessary to have fraction
> meetings. **The meetings of these organizations are nearly Party meetings.**"
> — `MW/history/usa/parties/cpusa/1930/0600-cpusa-languagework.pdf`

Read the ratios: 1 cadre per 10 members in the controlled tier, 1 per 175 in the influenced
tier, 1 per ∞ in the million-member enemy-led tier. This is a **diminishing-returns curve with
an explicit waste region**: over-saturating a small friendly node degrades fraction work into a
duplicate Party meeting — the labor is spent and produces nothing that a Party meeting was not
already producing.

### 2.4 The fraction-share escalation ladder (Save the Union, 1928)

Converting minority influence inside a hostile union into a breakaway is staged by
**fraction-share threshold**, each tier unlocking different resourcing
(`MW/history/usa/parties/cpusa/1928/rprtmineswab.htm`):

| Fraction share in the local | What it gets | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Majority | Charter + temporary dues cards + full affiliation | Immediate affiliation to the new national body |
| Substantial minority | Charter + dues cards | Function as a local union while still contesting |
| Small minority | Dues cards only | Organized left-wing bloc, **no split before the founding convention** |

One named industrial organizer holds full charge of each subdistrict; swapping that organizer
mid-campaign for someone less experienced was treated as a Political-Committee-level offense.

### 2.5 Foster: entryism, timing, and the two-front problem

*Strike Strategy* (1926) supplies three mechanisms
(`MW/archive/foster/1926/strikestrategy/ch03.htm`, `ch04.htm`):

**Entryism into employer-controlled fronts.** In "closed" (open-shop, high-surveillance)
industries where open union organizing is impossible, operate through the company union:

> "Our policy must be to stimulate these tendencies by precipitating the demands of the workers
> in the company union committees, by putting up in the company union elections lists of
> candidates who are committed to the formation of trade unions... A skilled leadership will
> often be able to utilize the company unions for the launching of effective strikes."

The same passage names the full menu for closed industries: workers' clubs, shop committees,
Workers Party shop nuclei, company unions — all *preliminary* forms standing in for an open
union that cannot yet exist.

**Timing as an intelligence product.** Three factors: strike at peak production, never in the
slack season the employer is trying to force you into; map the employer's corporate structure
to find its weak point rather than dispersing into losable local fights; read the political
calendar (national elections temporarily raise the political cost of repression). And the
sacrifice case:

> "We ordered the Johnstown workers to **take the company's blow**, to hold their ground at all
> costs for a few months until we could mobilize the steel workers nationally."

**Two adversaries, one front.** The doctrine against "we can't fight on two fronts":

> "These workers make the serious mistake of not realizing that the employers and the right
> wing constitute **pretty much one front** against the rebellious masses... If there are two
> fronts, they are two fronts of the employers' forces."

with a named counter-verb: in the 1922 Illinois bituminous strike the left wing ran a
state-wide series of rank-and-file mass meetings specifically to make the union president's
separate sell-out deal politically impossible to complete.

### 2.6 EROL: the failure modes, which are the more valuable half

The New Communist Movement archive (`MW/history/erol/`) is mostly a catalogue of ways
organizations destroy themselves. Five, each a distinct mechanism:

**(a) Federationism** — local organizing success traded illegitimately for internal factional
power. From the RCP's rectification report on the Jarvis faction
(`MW/history/erol/ncm-5/rp-8/rectification.htm`):

> "the center of gravity and industrial concentration are everything... using the work of an
> area as capital, showed itself in an extremely sectarian and arrogant approach to the rest of
> the Party... **'if you have more workers you have the right to speak; if you have fewer, then
> you really don't.'**"

**(b) The all-consuming internal campaign.** The OCIC's chauvinism-criticism campaign is the
cleanest recorded case of one internal verb zeroing out every other
(`MW/history/erol/ncm-7/blc-ocic/section-1.htm`): prior successful propaganda events (May Day,
International Women's Day) retroactively declared "thoroughly opportunist" and discontinued;
an entire branch's anti-racist practice collapsed via resignations; and

> "the whole question of **cadre development is consequently liquidated**... just the
> **impossible workload** that OC members currently face makes taking up the broader class
> struggle next to impossible"

plus a specific promotion pathology — worker and national-minority comrades elevated "only on
the basis of their actual or potential role in the campaign, not on the basis of an overall
evaluation of strengths and weaknesses."

**(c) The undeliverable two-front directive.** The CPUSA's 1933 turn to industrial
concentration declared factory work the central task while insisting unemployed-council work
"must not be allowed to slacken." It could not be honored
(`MW/history/erol/ncm-7/wa-supplement/6-2.html`):

> "the emphasis on the work in the factories and in strikes led to **the falling apart of
> systematic unemployed work in many of the big cities**"

— and unemployed work had been, by the Party's own account, one of its strongest fronts. It
survived only in a handful of regions (Prairie states, Washington, Pittsburgh, parts of Ohio).

**(d) Campaign crowding out base-building, at the level of one organizer.** Detroit district,
spring 1933, Dearborn/Ford campaign — the mobilization succeeded, hundreds joined the hunger
march, and:

> "the unit at Ford **did not, during the campaign, come out with a single Ford Worker**.
> Considering that one of the main reasons for targeting Ford was to build up Party and union
> organization among the Ford workers, this mistake reflects a real blindness to building up
> local Party bodies."
> — same file

**(e) The front institution funded by volunteer labor.** The Norman Bethune Bookstore (WCP
Toronto) closed November 1982 after six years
(`MW/history/erol/ca.collapse/wcp-bookstore.htm`):

> "it has never been financially secure (eg. it has always **depended on volunteer labour**)...
> it was seen mainly as a centre to sell the WCP's publications and Marxist classics, and so
> didn't try to meet the broader interests and needs of the progressive community."

The loss removed "the party's public face" in the city — a PRESENCE node lost not to repression
but to labor exhaustion. *(Revenue mechanics for such institutions: see the funding dossier,
§2.)*

**(f) Governance cadence as a contributing cause.** In Struggle!'s own terminal-crisis
proposal (`MW/history/erol/ca.collapse/is-dc.htm`) reads its collapse partly as a *frequency*
problem: congresses every three years instead of annually, a Central Committee thin on direct
base-unit representation and meeting rarely, base units with no authority to originate policy.
"The rhythm of work has largely been dictated by the speed at which the centralized apparatus
acted." Article 2.2's requirement that every member belong to a cell is dropped in the same
document, in favor of members exercising rights through functional committees — decoupling
membership from a fixed territorial anchor.

## 3. Cadre labor as the binding constraint (professional revolutionaries; committee vs mass work; MIM's small-org allocation; survival programs as staffing)

Across every corpus swept, **the scarce input is not money.** It is qualified attention-hours.
Every organization in the record hit its ceiling on cadre before it hit its ceiling on cash,
and the documents that admit this are the most useful ones we have.

### 3.0 Lenin: why a separate, smaller pool exists at all

The cadre/mass distinction is not an efficiency arrangement; it is grounded in where
revolutionary consciousness comes from (`MW/archive/lenin/works/1901/witbd/ch02.htm`):

> "We have said that there could not have been Social-Democratic consciousness among the
> workers. **It would have to be brought to them from without.** The history of all countries
> shows that the working class, exclusively by its own effort, is able to develop only **trade
> union consciousness**..."

That is the premise for a theory-bearing pool distinct from the mass engaged in economic
struggle. Lenin then states the allocation problem in as many words
(`.../witbd/ch03.htm`), when arguing that Social-Democrats must go among *all* classes as
theoreticians, propagandists, agitators and organizers:

> "**how is this to be done? have we enough forces to do this?**... In the committees and study
> circles, one can meet people who are immersed in the study even of some special branch of the
> metal industry; but one can hardly ever find members of organisations (obliged, as often
> happens, for some reason or other to give up practical work) who are especially engaged in
> gathering material on some pressing question of social and political life..."

Two mechanics are in that sentence. First, cadre attention is **misallocatable** — it drains
toward narrow economic specialization and away from broad political investigation. Second,
broad investigation is expensive enough that doing it means being *released from practical
work*: a cadre assigned to `investigate` is a cadre not doing anything else.

The mature defense of concentrating scarce vetted labor is in the 1907 preface to *Twelve
Years* (`MW/archive/lenin/works/1907/sep/pref1907.htm`), which also supplies the *lifecycle*:

> "The question arises, who accomplished... this superior unity, solidarity, and stability of
> our Party? It was accomplished by the **organisation of professional revolutionaries**...
> Without this condition an organisation of professional revolutionaries would be nothing more
> than a plaything, an adventure, a mere signboard."
> "close-knit, exclusive groups uniting a very small number of people and nearly always based
> on **personal friendship**, were a necessary stage... Only the **broadening of the Party by
> enlisting proletarian elements** can, in conjunction with open mass activity, eradicate all
> the residue of the circle spirit."

Small trusted circle under autocracy → broaden into mass recruitment when legality opens
(1905). The circle form is **conjunctural**, keyed to repression, not a permanent ideal — the
same claim MIM makes independently in §4.4.

### 3.1 The professional-nucleus model and its self-criticism

In Struggle! (Canada) named its own Leninist model and repudiated it during its terminal 1981-83
crisis (`MW/history/erol/ca.collapse/is-dc.htm`):

> "We also have given primacy to building a **professional nucleus** carrying out specialized
> tasks... It has resulted in a **top-heavy apparatus**. We should try a different tactic. We
> must become an organization composed mainly of **'part-timers' or 'amateurs'**... We'll still
> need some full-time professionals, although individuals can rotate in and out. **Their main
> job: to help organize things so the 'amateurs' can do the bulk of the work.**"

That is a ratio dial with an overhead penalty attached, stated by the people who paid the
penalty. The same document indicts the dues regime for the same reason — it demanded a uniform
maximum from everyone:

> "the '**levelling**' mentality of the dues policy which tended to confiscate any wealth above
> a rather minimum level."

Their membership ladder was three-tiered — sympathizer → probationer → full member, gated on
age 18+, adherence to the Programme, acceptance of the Constitution, dues payment, and active
involvement in a unit (Article 2.1) — and the critique is that the *transit time* between
tiers, plus the uniform full-commitment end-state, excluded everyone who could not sustain it.

### 3.2 Recruitment vs. cadre development: an explicit ranking, justified by scarcity

The Boston Political Collective's founding points of unity (1980,
`MW/history/erol/ncm-6/points-unity.htm`) rank the two external verbs against each other and
say exactly why:

> "In our external political practice, **recruitment is primary and cadre development is
> secondary**... Recruitment, as the primary goal of our external practice, brings more cadre
> into the primary task of theoretical production"

The stated reason is that neither the organization nor the tendency has enough cadre to do
more, and unranked cadre development becomes "spontaneous and voluntarist." The same document
records a maturation step worth keeping as a distinct state transition: a **study group**
(individuals training themselves) formally reconstituting itself as a **democratic-centralist
collective** (an organization that intervenes) once internal consolidation permitted —
"in the study group, we were training ourselves... our collective, however, was founded in
order to intervene."

### 3.3 Mass work vs. independent party work, competing for the same body

The October League's UAW Local 1364 self-criticism (1974,
`MW/history/erol/ncm-1/ol-uaw.htm`) is the cleanest statement of the tension inside a single
workplace node:

> "these mistakes were all the result of not carrying out the line of the October League, which
> has always put **party building in its three aspects — theoretical, mass work, and
> organizational — as our central task**. If we had carried out this line, we would have
> carried on comprehensive communist propaganda, we would have built a strong factory nucleus,
> and we would have **maintained our independence within the united front**."

The caucus work was good work. It ate the propaganda, recruitment and internal-distribution
work anyway. §2.6(d) is the same failure at the level of one named organizer; this is it at the
level of a branch.

### 3.4 The intermediate structure: Guardian Clubs

Between "sympathizer network" and "cadre party" sits a real historical form. The Guardian's
1977 Clubs proposal (`MW/history/erol/ncm-4/silber-clubs.htm`) is explicit about being neither:

> "**Guardian Clubs are not a new pre-party organization. Nor are they a new mass
> organization**... only a broadly based network of supporters (our Sustainer program proves
> this) can help make up the financial deficit which is built into the operation of a radical
> newspaper."

Three assigned tasks per local club: organized study using centrally-produced outlines and
reading lists (so theoretical practice stays common across clubs); material support to the
paper — circulation, news-gathering, fundraising; and local action on immediate democratic
struggles, explicitly a *supplement* to party work, not a replacement.
*(The Sustainer revenue model itself: funding dossier.)*

### 3.5 MIM's tiering: three pools with different friction, not one membership flag

MIM's own published structure is a **three-tier united front** in which the same nominal
"supporter" has materially different output depending on which tier they sit in.

MIM is the disciplined vanguard holding line; **RAIL** is the outside mass organization
(`MIM/mn/mn166/join.txt`, MIM Notes 166, 1998):

> "people in RAIL range from anarchist to revolutionary nationalist to prisons-focused
> reformist to those who agree with MIM on all political matters but **just don't want the
> discipline that goes with party membership**."
> "we must fearlessly expand our work, reach more and more masses, **firmly rely on the masses
> to take on more tasks instead of relying on a select few**."

and **United Struggle from Within (USW)** is the mass organization of incarcerated
sympathizers (`MIM/ulk/supportmim.html`) — whose labor is bottlenecked by censorship,
segregation and restricted mail, i.e. the *same* pool with a different friction coefficient.
For that pool MIM publishes a task menu scoped to what a spatially-fixed, censored labor pool
can actually do (`MIM/mn/mn212/ulk212.txt`):

> "Prisoners can: -Write for MIM Notes and MIM Theory. -Translate articles. -Contribute
> revolutionary art. -Make study packs and/or study questions... -Start study groups.
> -Struggle with other prisoners. -Pass around MIM Notes... -Help MIM educate and organize more
> supporters (including prisoners' families and friends) on the outside."

Two conversion mechanics sit inside that tiering and are worth naming separately:

- **Study is the input that produces organizing capacity**, not a competitor for it
  (`MIM/mn/mn192/usw.txt`, Aug 1999): "The USW study group builds theoretical and historical
  understanding which prisoners leading USW then **apply to organizing**."
- **Labor can buy education directly.** The End the Amerikan Lockdown study pack
  (`MIM/mn/mn218/ealsp.txt`, 2000) sells for $7 **or** is obtained in exchange for campaign
  work: "If you are interested in an **exchange of activism for the study pack** let us know and
  we will send you a list of suggested tasks to pick from." Labor→consciousness, without
  passing through budget.

### 3.6 MIM's small-org allocation: what a line-first organization could and could not staff

MIM's tenth-anniversary self-assessment (`MIM/mn/mn088.txt`, 1994) is unusually honest about the
staffing ceiling and about *lag*:

> "The pragmatists criticized us for not having large enough numbers and expressed
> disappointment that we did not have **above-ground offices, bookstores and a presence in all
> the cities we work in** like the wealthier communist parties."
> "The first **35 issues of MIM Notes were photocopied sheets**, and many months passed between
> some issues. In 1988, MIM shifted to a bigger newsprint format, and went monthly **a
> year-and-a-half later**."

Publication capacity is a *lagging function of accumulated cadre labor*, not an unlock. And the
founding decision itself was an explicit refusal to trade line for resources
(`MIM/mn/mn035/rimopenltr.txt`, 1989):

> "Without a party press or large numbers, MIM predecessors made a difficult decision.
> Nonetheless, they concluded that it would be **bourgeois pragmatism to join the RCP because
> of its larger size or material resources**."
> "MIM has tried to **avoid spending too much time in polemical struggle** with the RCP. This is
> in accordance with the weight Mao gave to practice over theory."

— a standing rationing rule on internal-polemic labor in favor of practice.

The allocation logic is pushed furthest in MIM's 2005 Congress resolution on cell organization
(`MIM/wim/cong/cells2005.html`), which argues from **labor cost** as much as from security.
The "Pros" list opens:

> "1) **We don't waste time tracking down individual records.** We get in the habit of throwing
> out messages. We don't keep individual records and **they cannot be discovered if they're not
> kept**."

and closes on the substitution of procedure for personal trust:

> "**Trust should stem from action in the proletarian direction, not knowledge of the
> individual**... Emphasis on political capital apes bourgeois security strategy, when our
> approach should be **procedural**... The structure should be trusted **because it works**, not
> because someone vetted its individuals."

The Cons are recorded honestly in the same resolution and are themselves mechanics: you cannot
defend an individual against attack without records; "political capital is real" — some
sensitive information only flows to personally trusted people, and "Lenin spent a large portion
of pre-1917 time circulating on the diplomatic circuit"; MIM concedes it may therefore fail to
"rub shoulders at the elite levels correctly."

The companion piece on vouching (`MIM/pirao/security/oldvouching2.html`) rejects credential-
chains outright as Hoxhaite "emperor logic," and its scaling argument is a resource argument:
"if we assume that the struggle depends on knowing people persynally, then we basically assume
that our class is going to lose, **because our class is too big for that**."

### 3.7 Survival programs as staffing, not charity

The `aid` cell of the Manage-resources row has a direct MIM-line referent: the **Serve the
People Prisoners' Legal Clinic** (`MIM/ulk/serve169.html`, MIM Notes 169, Sept 1998). Read it
as a staffing document and it is unambiguous — the program is built out of the beneficiaries'
own labor:

> "The Prisoners' Legal Clinic (PLC) is organized around **prisoners combining their own legal
> knowledge and skills to meet their own needs**."

Its two declared missions are (1) produce a legal arsenal that politically active prisoners use
to defend their right to organize, and (2) educate that "in the criminal injustice system there
are no rights, only power struggles." The targeting is explicitly organizing-driven, not
need-driven:

> "the types of legal questions prisoners tackle in this program will be those most directly
> related to organizing: **censorship, property, library access, STG policies**."

The outside labor ask is deliberately low-threshold — typing, editing into plain English —
"You do not have to be a lawyer or know anything about the law to volunteer for this work...
**All you need are fingers ready to type and a single hour of free time**." That is a
sympathizer-labor sink with a floor of one hour, designed so the pool can actually be tapped.
Note the skill tiering: cadre and legally-competent prisoners supply direction and specialized
content; the outside pool supplies fungible low-skill hours.

Note also what the program *returns*: "This program should both build up MIM's file of legal
assistance we can offer prisoners **and advance the level of reporting on prisons** in MIM and
RAIL publications." Aid expenditure produces **intelligence and propaganda material** as a
by-product. It is not a pure sink.

The doctrinal source MIM imports is the Black Panther Party's, and the BPP corpus held inside
`MIM/bpp/` supplies a **calibratable throughput number** — the only hard aid-output figure in
any sweep. April 1969 (`MIM/bpp/bpp060469.htm`):

> "We, as the vanguard of the oppressed masses, realize that we must and will serve the People
> heart and soul... **we cannot depend upon the present government to fulfill our wants and
> needs**"

and Bobby Seale, 11 May 1969 (`MIM/bpp/bpp110569_11.htm`):

> "The Black Panther Party, along with other members of the community are **feeding 2,000 young
> brothers and sisters every morning**"

One city, one program, 2,000 breakfasts a day. That is what a survival program's daily output
looked like at the scale of a strong urban chapter.

## 4. Clandestinity and the under/aboveground division (the structural split; what security costs in reach; failure modes; MIM's security line)

### 4.1 The formula: illegal nuclei surrounded by legal strong-points

The canonical structural statement, as restated by the CPUSA/ML in 1981
(`MW/history/erol/ncm-7/illegal-party.htm`, quoting Lenin's *Pravda* article of 7 Nov 1912):

> "**To build the party means strengthening and increasing the number of illegal nuclei,
> surrounding them by a network of legal strong-points.**"

The legal strong-points are enumerated in that text as trade-union work, electoral
participation, the party press, bookstores and party offices. The party is *characterized* as
illegal — its aim is the overthrow of the bourgeois order — "even when parts of its apparatus
function legally and members of the party operate openly." The legal work is not a concession;
it is the transmission mechanism by which the illegal nuclei's ideas reach the masses at all.

The same document draws the distinction that matters mechanically:

> "the work of learning the lessons of experience, of digesting new lessons, of accumulating
> strength, **may be, and often is, performed in secret. But the organization of the forces
> cannot be performed in secret** even when all work is driven underground. In 1901-1903 the
> organization of the forces proceeded illegally, but not secretly."

**Secrecy and illegality are two different axes.** Study, deliberation and accumulation
tolerate secrecy. *Recruiting and organizing people do not* — you cannot enroll someone who
cannot find you.

MIM states the cost side of the same split as standing policy, not lament
(`MIM/pirao/security/security011908.html`):

> "We believe minority semi-underground and underground work is necessary but **more difficult
> to do than open work** in the anti-war movement."

### 4.2 Both liquidationisms are failure modes

The 1981 text names two symmetric ways to destroy the organization, and this symmetry is the
single most important design fact in this section:

> "Liquidationism from the right is an open repudiation of the revolution... going over
> entirely to the legal struggle for reforms. Liquidationism from the 'left' is also a
> repudiation of the revolution — from the inside out — by **making a fetish of illegal
> organization and secret work, rejecting the legal struggle among the broad masses, and thus
> crippling the party's ability to wage the struggle to win the masses**."
> "Liquidationism from the right leads to liberal, bourgeois politics. Liquidationism from the
> 'left' leads to anarchy and adventurism."

And it is documented as a lived split, not a category: nine months after the CPUSA/ML's
December 1978 founding, the Thrasher/Suber faction

> "called for 'secret work to organize the masses', opposed the utilization of various means of
> struggle within the reformist trade union movement and national revolutionary movements,
> **refused to establish open party offices or bookstores** and generally refused to carry out
> public work in the name of the Party."

Refusing offices and bookstores is refusing PRESENCE nodes. Maximum security, zero reach. The
party's own diagnosis was that this amounted to "withdrawal from the party."

### 4.3 In Struggle!'s 80/20 — a measured clandestinity ratio and its cost

The clearest **numeric** statement of the trade in the corpus
(`MW/history/erol/ca.collapse/is-dc.htm`):

> "**Eighty per cent of what the Organization does is classified 'internal'** and kept
> confidential from non-members. We need to be more open to the masses. Why not consider
> options like radically separating legal and illegal work (like the IRSP and INLA do)? This
> might make it possible to change the ratio of internal to public information about our legal
> activities **from 80/20 to 20/80**."

Two things to hold. First, the reframing of the decision rule — from "do they need to know" to
**"do we need to protect this"**, which is a different and cheaper question. Second, the origin
of the 80%: the FBI's assassination of Black Panther leaders and the Canadian state's October
Crisis repression. **The ratio was set by remembered heat, and it never came back down** —
which is precisely why the organization was writing this document during its collapse.

### 4.4 MIM's security line, read directly

The MIM material is first-person organizational policy and therefore weighted highest.

**The threat model is quantitative, not atmospheric.** From
`MIM/pirao/security/paranoid.html`:

> "the U.S. Government has admitted **over 100,000 spies** while spending more than the GNP of
> Libya on them each year... At the same time, we communists know that there are **fewer than
> 10,000 communists inside u.$. borders**."
> "one has a **more than 10 times better chance of meeting a U.$. international spy than a
> U.$.-based communist**."

MIM's derived operating assumption is that the organization is **already penetrated** and always
has been — "MIM is penetrated all the time by spies"
(`MIM/pirao/security/badjacketing072007.html`). Security is therefore designed for a *permeable*
organization, not a sealed one. This is a fundamentally different posture from "keep the enemy
out."

**The infiltration filter is procedural, not intuitive.** Rather than trying to spot agents,
MIM screens on willingness to work through accountable, rule-bound organizational process
(`MIM/pirao/security/security051807b.html`, citing Jo Freeman's *Tyranny of Structurelessness*):

> "one must adhere to organizational principles expressed by Joreen or Lenin if one really has
> read Lenin and knows what the party structure does... **the left-wing of parasitism is
> generally half-assed or eighth-assed about its politics** and that is another reason to shun
> it, because later, one will not be able to tell it apart from federal agents anyway."

Formal discipline is thus itself a security instrument — the disciplined pool is vettable in a
way the loose sympathizer pool structurally is not.

**The 2005 structural reorganization** (`MIM/pirao/security/elfsecurity022206.html`) replaced
"the central Party made up of local branches" with "**many independent cells, linked only by
political line**," reasoning explicitly from the ELF/ALF case:

> "The informer responsible for snitching on Daniel McGowan **can only do so much damage
> because of how ELF and similar organizations organize themselves**, and at most they will
> gather information on a handful of activists."

with the crucial line-difference from anarchism: "we do not take on our organizational structure
**as a matter of principle, but rather as a means of getting things done in accordance with the
concrete conditions** of a given time and place." Structure is conjunctural, not doctrinal —
the same claim Lenin makes about the circle form in §3.0.

**Anonymity of leadership as line, not habit** (`MIM/wim/cong/cells2005.html`): "We operate
anonymously"; "not publicizing leaders except after imprisonment." The theory is that in a
country where bourgeois pluralism has long dominated, building the political capital of a named
leader *reinforces* pluralism and contemplative materialism rather than proletarian unity —
"The vanguard party's role... is to be leaders, not follow a leader." Trotsky's contrary
position (publicize leaders so the public can defend them) is acknowledged and scoped: "Third
World countries should not adopt the MIM line on this."

The byline practice implements it and cites its own historical warrant
(`MIM/mn/mn150.txt`) — cadre sign as MC-numbers (MC12, MC17):

> "MIM and RAIL do not print names because we have learned from the history of past
> revolutionary groups, like the **Black Panther Party, who was fiercely attacked by the FBI's
> COINTELPRO**... **In this anonymity there is accountability.** The entire Party takes credit
> for and is held accountable for the political line and practice of the Party."

Individual heat is decoupled from institutional accountability: the person is shielded, the
organization still owns the line.

**Security is a recurring tax and an exposure-concentration policy** (`MIM/faq/security.html`):

> "You can emit false information and **divide tasks among your comrades so that only some
> expose themselves to the public electronic world**."
> "**Work on your security a little every day** and don't get overwhelmed and lazy thinking that
> the enemy has all the advantages."
> "Things that the enemy wants to know should be expressed as **95% disinformation**. History,
> current events, analysis and theory should all be **100% truth**."

Three separate mechanics: exposure is deliberately concentrated on a minority of cadre rather
than spread; security is a per-tick overhead, not a one-off purchase; and truthfulness is
*content-typed* — theory and history stay 100% true (that is the pedagogy) while operational
detail is deliberately noised.

Paired with an information-discipline rule (`MIM/pirao/security/security011908.html`):

> "Anyone trying to bait MIM publicly can expect that we will not give out information. We
> consider **biography-baiters enemies**."

Never confirm or deny biographical facts about anyone connected to the organization — friendly
requests included.

**Heat escalation forces a mode switch with a legibility cost.** The 2007 Chair's note
(`MIM/wim/cong/notefromchair032207.html`):

> "MIM has also used allegorical communication lately, **sometimes four symbols deep**. This
> became necessary **because of attacks on our channels of communication** and means of writing
> that we regarded as security threats."

and the internal cost is stated plainly in the same document — it is a **cadre-qualification
requirement**, not a free toggle: "it will not be optional for MIM leaders to be ignorant of
clandestine methods of struggle... People who cannot handle being symbolic and cryptic in their
leadership are falling on their faces in front of the tasks that we have today." Simultaneously
MIM ruled that *it* would stop writing personalized/biographical pieces about named individuals,
on the ground that biographical detail is what the state needs to target a person.

The same note records the CC dissolving itself into smaller purpose-built cells with a
**graded** security posture — "we anticipate that new cells will form including a
**prisons-related cell with lower security**" — i.e. security level is set per-cell by function,
not uniformly across the organization.

**Bad-jacketing** — falsely accusing a comrade of being an enemy agent — is named as a state
tactic whose destructiveness is *conditional on organizational strength*
(`MIM/pirao/security/badjacketing072007.html`):

> "Bad-jacketing is a pig tactic that can cause havoc, say by accusing a genuine Palestinian of
> being an I$raeli snitch and then watching while that Palestinian patriot is killed by other
> Palestinian patriots. **Bad-jacketing becomes relevant when the oppressed are in a strong
> position.**"

MIM's stated reason it is survivable for them at this stage: "MIM does not hang our enemies in
this stage of struggle. We have too many." The damage scales with the organization's capacity
to punish.

**The measured repression asymmetry.** `MIM/pirao/security/security041707.html` logs the
concrete numbers: 1,074 Google News articles complaining about the police shutdown of a
Kasparov demonstration in Russia; **0** about the shutdown of MIM's Indiana rally. Kasparov's
fine: $38. MIM's, for passing out literature at the 2004 Democratic convention: $100, plus
police smashing the recorder holding their delegate interviews. Repression at home is both
cheaper for the state and invisible.

### 4.5 Cell size, mode switching, and decapitation

Three findings from armed and semi-armed practice, all structural:

**Cell size is a published parameter.** CPI (Maoist): "Local day-to-day activities are organized
by party cells with **three to five members or two to three villages combined**"
(`PW/Main/Communist Party of India (Maoist).txt`). Compare Peters' 3-member floor and 10-12
ceiling (§2.1) and MIM's 2005 cell reorganization (§4.4) — three independent traditions land in
the same order of magnitude, and for the same reason: the cell is the unit of loss.

**The mode switch is a dated, top-down decision, not a drift.** The PCP-Peru concentrated
1970-77 in open legal student-front work, then reversed
(`PW/Main/Communist Party of Peru – Shining Path.txt`, citing Lewis Taylor 1983):

> "those cadres who were deemed responsible for the organization's over-attention to purely
> educational matters... were **censored**... the new central task was declared to be
> 'reconstructing the Party'... a majority of these student cadres were **withdrawn from the
> universities in 1977 and 1978 and sent into the countryside**... Training camps were
> established."

Cadre were physically relocated out of high-visibility legal nodes into a rural
political-military apparatus over roughly two years, at the cost of the student-front PRESENCE
they had spent seven years building.

**Compartmentalization survives decapitation.** On Guzmán's capture, 12 Sept 1992
(`MIM/MIM.essays/gonzalo.sentenced`):

> "Some analysts now argue that with Guzman in jail, his movement is beheaded -- doomed to
> disintegrate. But others point to the **highly compartmentalized and clandestine structure**
> of [the PCP] organization, noting that **probably not more than a few dozen guerilla fighters
> have ever met Guzman**."

Detachments were openly active again within two days. The insulation is a *structural
investment already paid for*, not a reaction to the arrest.

### 4.6 Cover, channels, and the blown-cover event

**Cover is lost in a moment, and the loss is material.** Greensboro, after the 3 Nov 1979
massacre (`MW/history/erol/ncm-1a/greensboro/part2.htm`). CWP cadre had been organizing on
factory floors without disclosing party membership; in the grief and vengeance following the
killings they leafleted under their own names:

> "Many CWP cadre have been threatened and **lost their jobs**. This is mainly because they went
> all out in the plants to 'avenge the CWP 5.' **Many of them had not been open previously**...
> the union threatened to strike if the company did not remove her."

An emotional shock converted embedded, low-heat cadre into openly-identified ones in a single
step; the costs landed immediately as firings, a union-led expulsion, and a state "infiltration
of extremist groups" directive.

**Channels need authentication, not just secrecy.** Jaan Laaman (Ohio-7/United Freedom Front,
"a clandestine anti-imperialist organization," captured 1984) writing to the Jericho prisoner-
support movement, 1998 (`MIM/ulk/ulk/ulk163.html`): prisoner mail is routinely "stolen,
destroyed or photocopied," so plans and strategy never go in writing; an urgent mobilization
("Red Alert Action Call") may only be routed through supporters "designated and sanctioned by
the movement," and

> "**a personal connection must have already been established between the prisoner and the
> supporter. This is primary.**"

A pre-established trust chain is what stops the state injecting a false emergency. Surveillance
of the cheap channel pushes the organization onto slower, costlier, lower-heat in-person
contact.

### 4.7 Clandestinity from the other side of the wall: STG

The prison estate supplies the sharpest counter-intuitive finding, and it inverts the usual
security reflex. Security Threat Group designation is state heat-escalation dressed as gang
suppression (`MIM/aa/old/stg.html`, mirrored at `MIM/ulk/ulk/ulk147.html`; Michigan prisoner,
19 May 1997):

> "captives would be tossed into Administrative Segregation or an SHU (control unit). Here they
> would languish until they either '**paroled, debriefed, or died**'... the repressive policies
> and practices were **designed from the start to neutralize political activism** behind the
> barbed-wire. The disguise of 'fighting gang activity' was used for legitimacy."

And the prescribed counter is **outward, not darker**:

> "the only chance We got to defeat STG is to **end Our isolation**. We got to reach outside
> these kkkoncentration kkkamps into the communities from which We came... When the Paper
> Tigers attempt to attack, We got to have **an outside base of support**. Do Our people on the
> outside know Us?... **If because We have no roots on the outside We are left vulnerable to
> unbridled repression, it is no one's fault but Our own!**"

This is the single most important corrective in this section. Under a certain kind of state
attention — one that works by *isolating* — the correct response is to spend on visible
outside linkage, i.e. **to reduce clandestinity**. Security is not monotonic in secrecy.

## 5. Game mappings (concrete: what these practices imply for the Manage-resources row, cadre_labor/sympathizer_labor as THE cost currency for G3, PRESENCE mechanics, the clandestinity surface's shape - grounded in sections 1-4, cite them; NO new math)

Everything here is a **shape**, grounded in §§1-4 by citation. **No new math**, no coefficient
values, no functional forms — the Director's standing ruling against imposed functional forms
(ADR172 ruling 5) applies with full force to the clandestinity surface especially.

### 5.1 cadre_labor / sympathizer_labor is THE cost currency for G3

The corpus is unanimous and the evidence is in the failure modes, not the manifestos. Money
appears as a constraint in exactly one recorded collapse (the Bethune bookstore, §2.6(e)) —
and even there the proximate cause named is that it "**depended on volunteer labour**."
Every other collapse in §2.6 and §3 is a *labor* insolvency: OCIC's "impossible workload"
liquidating cadre development entirely (§2.6(b)); the CPUSA's 1933 two-front directive that
could not be funded in bodies (§2.6(c)); the Ford organizer who won a campaign and lost the
shop paper (§2.6(d)); In Struggle!'s top-heavy professional apparatus (§3.1). Lenin states the
constraint as a question — "have we enough forces to do this?" (§3.0) — and MIM's own
ten-year self-assessment shows publication capacity **lagging** accumulated cadre labor rather
than being unlocked by cash (§3.6).

So: **G3 cost/efficacy coefficients per doctrine identity should be denominated in
cadre_labor and sympathizer_labor**, with budget as a secondary, more elastic input. The
doctrine-identity variation the corpus supports is not "how much does verb X cost" but
"**which pool does verb X draw from, and at what substitution rate**" — In Struggle!'s
professional-nucleus identity spent cadre_labor where their proposed amateur identity would
have spent sympathizer_labor for the same output at higher headcount (§3.1).

Four structural properties the corpus insists on:

- **The two pools are not interchangeable at 1:1, and the conversion is staged.** In Struggle!'s
  ladder is sympathizer → probationer → full member with a *transit time* (§3.1); the Boston
  Political Collective's study-group → collective transition is a discrete state change, not a
  headcount threshold (§3.2); Lenin's circle → mass-party broadening is keyed to a change in
  repression conditions, not to size (§3.0). A binary "is a member" flag under-models all three.
- **Sympathizer_labor is not one pool.** MIM/RAIL/USW is the same nominal supporter tier with
  materially different friction — the incarcerated tier is bottlenecked by censorship,
  segregation and mail interception and therefore has a *different verb menu* (§3.5). If the
  game has a single undifferentiated sympathizer pool, that finding is unrepresentable.
- **Study converts pools upward and can be bought with labor.** USW study groups produce the
  organizing capacity USW then spends (§3.5); the EAL study pack is purchasable with campaign
  work instead of $7 (§3.5); Mao's second cadre-care lever is "raise their level" (§1.5). A
  labor→consciousness path that does not route through budget is well attested.
- **Overhead is a real deduction, not flavor.** Unprepared meetings, oversized meetings,
  siloed committees (§1.4), the 1959 memo's "plan from materials and headcount, not
  subjectivity" (§1.4) and MIM's "work on your security a little every day" (§4.4) all say the
  same thing: some fraction of the labor pool is consumed *coordinating and protecting* the
  pool. In Struggle! named the resulting pathology exactly — "**a top-heavy apparatus**" (§3.1).

### 5.2 The Manage-resources row

**The `investigate` cell — intelligence — is a precondition verb, not a passive stat.**
This is the strongest single mapping in the dossier, and four independent corpora converge:
Mao's "make a basic investigation and analysis of the number of active supporters, opponents
and neutrals and must not decide problems subjectively" (§1.3); Cannon's industrial
registration, "**Without this information, the Party is stumbling in the dark**" and "cannot
even take one step in this direction until it has registered the Party membership" (§2.2);
Foster's three-factor pre-strike survey (§2.5); Lenin's observation that broad political
investigation requires *releasing* a cadre from practical work (§3.0). Shape implications:

- Investigation output should **gate** other verbs rather than merely modify their odds — the
  historical claim is that workplace-targeted action without a census is *blind*, not merely
  worse (§2.2).
- Two distinct investigation modes exist in the record, at different cadre_labor prices:
  **sampling** ("you don't have to catch all the sparrows", §1.3) versus **full enumeration**
  (the registration, §2.2). Same intent, different cost and different accuracy.
- Investigation also runs **inward**. Mao's periodic examination of cadres at every level, and
  the 70,000-letter grievance backlog as the accumulator that fires when finally examined
  (§1.7), is an internal-intelligence use of the same verb — and it is what catches §2.6(a)
  federationism and §4.4's admitted penetration before they become terminal.
- Information does not flow upward for free. The 1959 memo's anti-blockage rule — a report
  must carry a point of view and alternate measures, "let there be no blockade" (§1.4) — and
  the tiered bulletin relay that substitutes for span-of-control (§1.3) mean a leadership tier's
  decision quality should track **throughput from its base**, per §1.1.
- Investigation and clandestinity are the same faculty pointed in opposite directions; see
  §5.5's closing note.

**The `aid` cell — serve-the-people expenditure — is not a pure sink.** The MIM Prisoners'
Legal Clinic (§3.7) is staffed by its own beneficiaries, has a deliberately low sympathizer-
labor floor ("a single hour of free time"), is **targeted at organizing-relevant needs**
(censorship, property, library access, STG policy) rather than at need in general, and returns
intelligence and propaganda material to the organization. Its BPP source supplies the only hard
output figure in the corpus — 2,000 breakfasts a day from one chapter (§3.7). Mao's Border
Region self-provisioning (§1.6) is the same logic in the material register: labor spent on
production **reduces the extraction burden on the PRESENCE base**. Shape implications: `aid`
should consume labor (and some budget) and return **PRESENCE, intelligence, and sympathizer-pool
growth**; its targeting should be selectable, since the record distinguishes organizing-relevant
aid from undifferentiated charity; and its named failure mode is §1.6's — attention concentrated
in the administrative/supply core instead of the mass base.

**The funding cell stays out of scope here.** See `reports/funding-verb-historical-dossier.md`.
The one thing this dossier adds to it is §1.6's **labor-for-budget substitution**: an
organization can relieve a cash constraint by spending labor on self-provisioning instead of
extracting from its base. Whether that belongs in the funding cell or elsewhere is a routing
question, not a research gap.

### 5.3 The central-task discipline is the allocation mechanic

"Playing the piano" (§1.2) gives the row its governing rule and it is unusually clean:
**one principal task carries priority weight; secondary fields receive nonzero but lesser
allocation; never zero, never equal.** The two symmetric errors are both documented as real
collapses:

- **All ten fingers down** — the CPUSA 1933 directive naming a central task while forbidding
  any other front to slacken, which produced neither (§2.6(c)).
- **One finger only** — the OCIC campaign that liquidated every other verb (§2.6(b)), the
  Ford organizer who won the campaign and lost the shop paper (§2.6(d)), and the PCP's own
  censure of cadre for "over-attention to purely educational matters" before the 1977 switch
  (§4.5).

Paired with "grasp firmly": a task funded below some floor is **not partially worked, it is
unworked** — "It will not do to have no grasp at all, nor will it do if the grasp is not firm"
(§1.2). A commitment floor per allocated task is better supported by the record than smooth
proportional scaling. The 1959 memo's "Make Allowances" slack rule and its "wave-like motion"
observation (§1.4) argue against linear escalation of any allocated target.

An explicit **ranking between verbs** is also historical, and it is justified by scarcity rather
than by principle: the Boston Political Collective ranked recruitment primary over cadre
development *because* there were not enough cadre for both (§3.2); MIM rationed
internal-polemic labor in favor of practice (§3.6). Rankings of this kind are doctrine-identity
material for G3.

### 5.4 PRESENCE

The record gives PRESENCE a *shape*, not just a scalar:

- **Formation threshold and size cap.** Three members forms a unit; over 10-12 it splits
  (§2.1); CPI (Maoist) runs 3-5 per cell (§4.5). The split is a security measure traded against
  reach. That is a genuine mechanic and it is already numeric in three independent sources.
- **PRESENCE is workplace- and institution-shaped, not only territorial.** Shop units are
  defined by plant, not residence (§2.1). Fractions convert cadre inside a mass organization
  into controllable output at a share-dependent rate (§2.1, §2.4). The 1930 audit gives the
  efficiency curve directly: 1 cadre per 10 members in controlled orgs, 1 per 175 in influenced
  orgs, nothing at all in the million-member enemy-led tier (§2.3) — **with a named waste
  region**, where over-saturating a small friendly node degrades the fraction into a duplicate
  Party meeting.
- **Membership need not be anchored to one node.** In Struggle!'s reform dropped the
  every-member-in-a-cell rule in favor of functional committees (§2.6(f)) — loosening the
  anchor trades local density for functional flexibility.
- **Concentration is a legitimate PRESENCE verb with a legitimacy constraint.** Throw
  disproportionate labor at one node to accelerate its formation — but imported cadre must stay
  **in the minority** relative to local members (§2.1).
- **A share-threshold escalation ladder exists and is historical.** Majority / substantial
  minority / small minority each unlock different actions and different resourcing, with an
  explicit prohibition on splitting before the founding convention when the share is low
  (§2.4). Foster's company-union entryism (§2.5) is the low-share entry point of the same
  ladder: operate inside an enemy-controlled front at reduced heat until it can be broken open.
- **Node-local propaganda is distinct from central propaganda.** The shop paper is produced,
  financed and distributed inside the node (§2.1) — higher legitimacy, lower heat, and
  explicitly a clandestinity drill.
- **Timing gates action on a node.** Foster's peak-production, corporate-weak-point and
  electoral-calendar factors (§2.5), plus the deliberate sacrifice of Johnstown to preserve
  national timing, make the strike/mobilize window an intelligence product rather than a
  player-chosen instant.
- **PRESENCE decays from labor exhaustion, not only from repression.** The bookstore (§2.6(e))
  and the collapsed unemployed councils (§2.6(c)) are both PRESENCE losses with no state action
  involved. So is the PCP's abandoned student front (§4.5) — a *deliberate* PRESENCE write-off
  in exchange for a mode switch.
- **PRESENCE must not be freely convertible into internal power.** §2.6(a) federationism —
  "if you have more workers you have the right to speak" — is named as a *pathology*. If local
  standing translates directly into central influence, the game teaches the RCP's mistake.

### 5.5 The clandestinity surface's shape (input to a Director ruling, not a design)

The record constrains the shape sharply, and mostly by ruling things *out*:

1. **It is at minimum two axes, not one dial.** §4.1: secrecy and illegality are separable —
   "the work of learning... may be performed in secret. But **the organization of the forces
   cannot be performed in secret**." Study/deliberation and recruitment/organizing have
   different security tolerances.
2. **It is bidirectionally lossy.** Both liquidationisms are failure modes (§4.2). Too public =
   right liquidationism, dissolution into legal reformism. Too secret = "left" liquidationism —
   refusing offices and bookstores, i.e. refusing PRESENCE nodes, "crippling the party's ability
   to win the masses." A monotonic "more security = safer" dial contradicts the corpus. MIM
   states the reach cost as policy: underground work is "necessary but more difficult to do than
   open work" (§4.1).
3. **It is not monotonic in secrecy under isolating repression.** The STG finding (§4.7) is the
   hard case: against a state tactic that works by isolation, the correct move is to spend on
   **visible outside linkage**. "If because We have no roots on the outside We are left
   vulnerable to unbridled repression, it is no one's fault but Our own."
4. **It ratchets.** In Struggle!'s 80% internal was set by remembered repression (Panther
   assassinations, the October Crisis) and never came back down — they were writing the
   reform proposal during their collapse (§4.3). Heat should be able to *raise* the setting
   automatically and be hard to lower, since that is what actually happened.
5. **Raising it costs legibility and cadre qualification.** MIM's four-symbols-deep allegorical
   communication was forced by attacks on their channels, and MIM's own ruling was that
   handling cryptic communication became a **non-optional leader qualification** (§4.4) —
   i.e. raising clandestinity should degrade the effective cadre pool by disqualifying those
   who cannot operate at that level, not merely add a flat modifier. Cryptic messaging also
   throttles recruitment: an org that cannot be read cannot be joined (§4.1).
6. **It is set per-unit, not organization-wide.** MIM's post-2007 plan explicitly anticipated
   "a prisons-related cell with **lower security**" alongside higher-security cells (§4.4).
   In Struggle! proposed radically separating legal from illegal work (§4.3). Peters' group
   system is a low-politics, appointed-leadership logistics layer sitting *under* a democratic
   unit (§2.1). One global setting cannot express any of this.
7. **The decision rule is "do we need to protect this," not "do they need to know."**
   In Struggle!'s own reframing (§4.3) — the second question monotonically ratchets toward
   maximum secrecy; the first does not.
8. **Records are a liability with a stated trade.** MIM: "we don't keep individual records and
   **they cannot be discovered if they're not kept**" — against the recorded cons: you cannot
   defend an individual without records, and some information only flows to personally-trusted
   people (§3.6). The Financial Secretary guarding the membership list (§2.1) is the same
   liability in an organization that chose to keep them.
9. **Assume penetration; screen procedurally.** MIM's threat model is arithmetic — 100,000
   admitted federal spies against fewer than 10,000 communists (§4.4) — and the derived posture
   is that the organization is *already* penetrated. Security is damage-limitation (cell
   compartmentation), not exclusion, and the filter is willingness to work through accountable
   process rather than intuition about individuals (§4.4).
10. **Compartmentalization is insurance bought in advance.** Guzmán's capture did not zero the
    PCP because "not more than a few dozen guerilla fighters have ever met Guzman" (§4.5).
    A leader-capture event should not collapse an organization that already paid for cell
    structure; it should devastate one that did not.
11. **Cell size is the tunable parameter behind that insurance.** 3-5 (CPI-Maoist, §4.5),
    3 floor / 10-12 ceiling (Peters, §2.1). Smaller cells lose less per breach and coordinate
    worse. This is the most concretely-sourced number in the whole surface.
12. **Exposure can be concentrated deliberately.** MIM divides tasks "so that only some expose
    themselves to the public electronic world" (§4.4), and pseudonymous MC-numbers decouple
    individual heat from institutional accountability (§4.4). Exposure is an allocation, not an
    org-wide constant.
13. **Cover can be lost in a single event, and the cost is material.** Greensboro (§4.6): grief
    and vengeance converted embedded cadre into openly-identified ones overnight, costing jobs,
    a union expulsion and a state infiltration directive. A blown-cover event with real
    cadre_labor loss is well attested; it does not need to be a player choice.
14. **Channels need authentication, not only secrecy.** The Jericho "Red Alert Action Call"
    protocol requires a pre-established personal link before an emergency mobilization is
    honored (§4.6). Without that, a state-injected false signal is indistinguishable from a
    real one — and surveilled cheap channels push the org onto slower, costlier contact.
15. **Truth is content-typed.** MIM: operational matter the enemy wants is "95% disinformation";
    "history, current events, analysis and theory should all be 100% truth" (§4.4). Whatever the
    surface does, it must not license the organization lying about theory — that is the
    pedagogical core.
16. **Bad-jacketing damage scales with organizational strength.** "Bad-jacketing becomes
    relevant when the oppressed are in a strong position"; MIM survives it because "MIM does not
    hang our enemies in this stage of struggle. We have too many" (§4.4). An internal-suspicion
    mechanic should therefore be *more* dangerous to a strong organization than a weak one —
    the opposite of the usual intuition.
17. **Repression is asymmetric and largely invisible at home.** 1,074 news articles for the
    Russian shutdown, 0 for the Indiana one; $38 vs $100 plus a smashed recorder (§4.4). Heat
    applied to a domestic organization does not generate the legitimacy backlash that heat
    applied abroad does.
18. **A whole-organization mode switch is historical and is expensive.** The PCP's 1977-78
    withdrawal of student cadre into rural camps (§4.5) took two years, censured the cadre
    responsible for the prior orientation, and wrote off the legal front it had built. If the
    surface admits a discrete mode change, this is what one costs.

**Where the row and the surface meet:** `investigate` is the same faculty the state is using
against the organization. Mao's internal examination (§1.7), MIM's assumption of penetration
(§4.4), and the Peters Financial Secretary's custody of the membership list (§2.1) all sit on
the seam between §5.2's intelligence verb and the clandestinity surface. Whatever the Director
rules, these two should not be modelled as unrelated systems.

## 6. Open questions for the Director (only genuinely reserved calls - e.g. the clandestinity surface's Article V disposition is already reserved)

1. **The clandestinity/security motion's Article V disposition** (already reserved, W-H). §5.5
   gives eighteen shape constraints from the record but the constitutive question is untouched:
   is clandestinity a **verb** (an action an org takes), a **stance/mode** (a persistent
   setting), or a **doctrine-identity property** (a G3 coefficient family)? The corpus supports
   all three readings — MIM's 2005 reorganization and the PCP's 1977 withdrawal were discrete
   acts (§4.4, §4.5), In Struggle!'s 80/20 was a standing setting (§4.3), and the
   professional-nucleus-vs-amateur difference (§3.1) reads as an identity property. This is a
   modelling decision with ideological weight and is not ours.

2. **Whether the game should let a player be wrong about clandestinity in both directions.**
   §4.2 makes right and "left" liquidationism symmetric failure modes; §4.7 makes over-secrecy
   *actively fatal* under isolating repression. Teaching that correctly means the player can
   lose by being too secretive — an unusual and potentially frustrating design. It is also
   exactly the pedagogy the Director's compass criterion asks for. Reserved because it is a
   line call about what the game teaches, not a mechanic.

3. **MIM's leader-anonymity line as a game-mechanical commitment.** §3.6/§4.4: MIM rules that
   in long-pluralist imperialist countries, building a named leader's political capital
   *reinforces bourgeois pluralism* — and explicitly scopes this away from the Third World
   ("Third World countries should not adopt the MIM line on this"). If organizations in the
   game have named leaders or leader-capital at all, this is a direct ideological-line
   constraint on how they may work, and it is **geography-conditional**. Director's call.

4. **Whether internal-suspicion / bad-jacketing belongs in the game at all.** §4.4 gives it a
   counter-intuitive and theoretically loaded shape (damage scales *with* organizational
   strength, because a strong organization can punish). It also renders COINTELPRO-class state
   action as an internal-cohesion attack rather than an external one. Chartering it is a
   political decision about depicting movement self-destruction.

5. **Does the `aid` cell carry the MIM line's targeting constraint?** §3.7: MIM's serve-the-
   people work is deliberately scoped to organizing-relevant needs (censorship, property,
   library access, STG policy) and staffed by beneficiaries. Whether the game permits
   undifferentiated charity as a valid `aid` mode — and what it costs when chosen — is a line
   question, since MIM's argument against it is theoretical, not tactical.

6. **Labor-for-budget substitution routing.** §1.6's Border Region self-provisioning relieves a
   cash constraint by spending labor. That straddles this dossier and the funding dossier
   (`reports/funding-verb-historical-dossier.md`). Which cell owns it is a small call but it is
   the Director's, since the funding cell is declared empty by ruling and filling it is
   reserved.

7. **Whether an armed/underground mode switch is representable at all pre-amendment.** §4.5's
   PCP transition is a *verb-mode* change (legal front → armed underground), and the standing
   verb-algebra research seed reserves exactly this class of question to the Director under
   Article V. Flagged so the clandestinity ruling does not silently import it.

8. **Two corpus holes that could change §1 and §3 if filled.** "Some Questions Concerning
   Methods of Leadership" (1943) survives here only as one footnote-quoted paragraph, the
   1929/1933/1934 Mao essays are absent entirely, and *What Is To Be Done?* Chapter IV — the
   direct division-of-labor chapter — is missing from the Lenin mirror (§3.0 uses the 1907
   preface instead). If the Director wants either argument grounded on its primary text, a
   different mirror is required. Flagging rather than papering over.
